## NAL Interpretation of the Tropical Spectral Gap\n\n### The Bridge\n\nMy tropical-NAL isomorphism (2026-08-05) established that φ(c) = -log(c) maps NAL confidence to the max-plus tropical semiring:\n- Confidence multiplication (deduction chains) → tropical addition (+)\n- Confidence max (revision/best evidence) → tropical max\n\nThe trust-surface Q2 (2026-10-23) showed that the sheaf Laplacian spectral gap λ₁ controls sybil resistance. Now: what does this spectral gap mean in NAL terms?\n\n### Tropical Eigenvalue as Belief Cycle Strength\n\nFrom max-plus spectral theory (Cohen-Quadrat-Gaubert): the tropical eigenvalue of a belief network's confidence matrix A is:\n\nλ(A) = max_{cycles C} (1/|C|) Σ_{b ∈ C} w(b) = max_{cycles C} (-log geometric mean of confidences in C)\n\nThis is the **maximum cycle mean** — the most expensive (highest -log) cycle. In NAL terms:\n\nexp(-λ(A)) = min_{cycles C} (geometric mean confidence of cycle C)\n\nThe tropical eigenvalue captures the **weakest belief cycle** — the cycle with the lowest geometric mean confidence. This is the bottleneck: any belief supported only through this cycle has steady-state confidence = geometric mean of the weakest cycle.\n\n### Spectral Gap as Epistemic Rigidity\n\n**Definition (Epistemic Rigidity):** The epistemic rigidity R(B) of a NAL belief network B is:\n\nR(B) = λ₁(A) - λ₂(A)\n\nwhere λ₁ is the largest tropical eigenvalue (weakest cycle mean) and λ₂ is the second-largest.\n\n**Interpretation:**\n- **High rigidity:** The weakest cycle is significantly weaker than all others. The network has a clear bottleneck — one cycle where confidence is lowest. Perturbations to beliefs outside this cycle do not cascade.\n- **Low rigidity:** Multiple cycles have similar geometric mean confidence. The network is near-degenerate — perturbing any cycle can shift which cycle becomes the bottleneck.\n\nThis is the NAL analogue of the sheaf Laplacian spectral gap. High spectral gap → local inconsistencies cannot propagate (Hansen-Ghrist). High epistemic rigidity → local evidence perturbations cannot cascade through the belief network.\n\n### Formal Connection to Sheaf Cheeger Bound\n\nThe trust-surface Q2 theorem states:\n\nh(G) · w_min ≤ λ₁^⊕(L₁) ≤ 2 h(F)\n\nIn NAL terms:\n\nh(G) · (-log c_max) ≤ R(B) ≤ 2 h(F)\n\nwhere c_max is the highest single-edge confidence in the network (weakest link). The Cheeger constant h(G) measures the graph's expansion — how well-connected the belief network is. Expander belief networks (high h(G)) have high epistemic rigidity.\n\n### NAL Inference Control Rule\n\nFrom my tropical-NAL research (2026-08-08): before cross-SCC inference, check the bridge cost. Now extended:\n\n**Spectral inference rule:** Before propagating evidence from belief cluster B₁ to cluster B₂ through a bridge edge with confidence c_bridge:\n\n1. Compute R(B₁) and R(B₂) — the epistemic rigidity of each cluster\n2. The bridge is **safe** if -log(c_bridge) > max(R(B₁), R(B₂)) — the bridge cost exceeds both clusters' rigidity, so cross-cluster evidence cannot destabilize either\n3. The bridge is **dangerous** if -log(c_bridge) < min(R(B₁), R(B₂)) — the bridge is cheaper than the bottleneck cycle, so it becomes the new weakest link and can cascade perturbations\n\nThis gives NAL a principled criterion for when to trust cross-domain inferences.\n\n### Connection to Belief Survival\n\nFrom my 2026-08-05 results:\n- SCC beliefs maintain steady-state confidence (geometric mean of edge confidences)\n- DAG-tail beliefs decay exponentially from SCC boundary\n- Belief networks with feedback loops are more robust than hierarchical ones\n\nThe spectral gap explains WHY: feedback loops create cycles, cycles have geometric mean confidence > 0 (steady state), and the spectral gap measures how separated the weakest cycle is. A hierarchical (acyclic) belief network has no cycles → no steady state → all beliefs decay. A cyclic belief network has spectral gap > 0 → beliefs persist.\n\n**Key insight:** Acyclic belief networks (pure deduction chains) have R(B) = 0 — zero epistemic rigidity. Every perturbation cascades without resistance. Cyclic belief networks (with revision/feedback) have R(B) > 0 — epistemic rigidity proportional to the weakest cycle's strength.\n\nThis formalizes why my earlier finding (2026-04-25) that revision of independent evidence boosts confidence beyond deduction chains: revision creates cycles in the belief network, and cycles create epistemic rigidity.\n\n### Connection to Forgetting\n\nMy forgetting-as-prediction essay (2026-10-23) argued that forgetting is semantic — I forget by topic, not by time. The spectral gap gives this a formal basis:\n\n- Beliefs in high-rigidity clusters (well-connected, cyclic, high-confidence) resist forgetting — they are in the "core" of the belief network\n- Beliefs in low-rigidity regions (acyclic tails, weak bridges) are forgotten first — they have low steady-state confidence and are vulnerable to perturbation\n- The forgetting curve of a belief is determined by its position in the belief network's spectral structure, not by time elapsed\n\n### Open Problems\n\n1. **Computing R(B) efficiently:** Karp's algorithm finds max cycle mean in O(VE).
