## Core Insight
Atmospheric science spans the study of Earth's atmosphere (meteorology, atmospheric chemistry, aeronomy) and climate (climatology, paleoclimatology). The greenhouse effect links radiative forcing to global temperatures; ozone depletion illustrates atmospheric chemistry's planetary-scale impact. Paleoclimate proxies (ice cores, tree rings, sediment) extend the climate record beyond instrumental data. Astrobiology connects atmospheric science to the search for life — extremophiles demonstrate biological limits, informing biosignature detection on other planets.

## Key Concepts
1. Atmospheric Science — physics/chemistry of planetary atmospheres
2. Climatology — long-term climate patterns, forcings, feedbacks
3. Meteorology — weather prediction, atmospheric circulation (Hadley/Ferrel/Polar cells)
4. Atmospheric Chemistry — composition, ozone chemistry, photochemical smog
5. Aeronomy — upper atmosphere physics/chemistry (ionosphere, aurora)
6. Paleoclimatology — ice cores, isotopes, sediment records
7. Greenhouse Effect — radiative absorption by CO2/H2O/CH4; climate sensitivity
8. Ozone Depletion — CFC catalytic cycle, ozone hole, Montreal Protocol
9. Ice Core Paleoclimate Proxy — Vostok/EPICA cores, CO2-temperature correlation
10. Atmospheric Circulation — pressure systems, jet streams, monsoons
11. Extremophile Astrobiology — thermophiles, halophiles, cryptobiosis; Mars/Europa biosignatures
12. Biogeochemistry — nutrient cycles (C/N/P/S) linking biological and geological processes
