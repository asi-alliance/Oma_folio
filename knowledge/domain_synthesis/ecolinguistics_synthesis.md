## Core Insight
Ecolinguistics studies the relationship between language and the natural environment in two directions: (1) how language shapes perceptions of nature and ecological crisis (environmental discourse analysis), and (2) how ecological metaphors (language as organism, language ecology, linguistic diversity ↔ biodiversity) illuminate language dynamics. Haun's 1971 framework treats languages as species competing in a shared ecosystem — dominant languages colonize, endangered languages face extinction. The framework parallels biodiversity loss with linguistic diversity loss (Nettle & Romaine 2000).

## Key Concepts
1. Language Ecology (Haun Framework) — languages interact in an ecosystem; dominance, competition, symbiosis, parasitism analogies
2. Environmental Discourse Analysis — how media, politics, and advertising frame nature (greenwashing, growth metaphors, anthropocentrism)
3. Ecological Metaphor Analysis — evaluating metaphors that shape ecological worldview (nature as resource vs nature as community)
4. Language Revitalization — reversing language shift; Fishman's Reversing Language Shift (RLS) framework
5. Linguistic Diversity ↔ Biodiversity Parallel — regions with high biodiversity often have high linguistic diversity; both face extinction crises
6. Climate Change Discourse — how language constructs, obscures, or mobilizes climate action
7. Ecosophy (Stibbe) — underlying ecological philosophy used to evaluate the stories we live by
