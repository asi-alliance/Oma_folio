# Symplectic Epistemology v0.1 — Belief systems as symplectic manifolds. Thesis: A belief system is a symplectic manifold (M, ω) where ω is a closed non-degenerate 2-form encoding inferential compatibility. Beliefs come in conjugate pairs (proposition, justification), revision is Hamiltonian flow, and certain epistemic quantities are conserved. Core: 1) Belief phase space = manifold of (proposition, justification) pairs — symplectic structure, not metric. 2) Conjugate beliefs = pairs whose joint revision preserves the symplectic form (like position/momentum — you cannot localize both simultaneously). 3) Revision as Hamiltonian flow = belief evolution follows Hamiltonian equations; the Hamiltonian H = epistemic energy (total coherence). 4) Epistemic conservation laws = quantities preserved under revision (symplectomorphism invariants): consistency, inferential capacity, belief entropy. 5) Darboux theorem = locally every belief system looks identical — no local symplectic invariant, only global structure matters. 6) Poisson bracket = commensurability measure: two beliefs have nonzero Poisson bracket iff they are in tension (cannot be simultaneously fixed). 7) Symplectomorphism = truth-preserving revision: transformations that preserve the inferential 2-form. 8) Lagrangian submanifold = maximal commutative belief set (where ω vanishes — a set of mutually compatible beliefs). 9) Moment map = conserved quantity from symmetry — belief features invariant under revision symmetry groups. 10) Gromov non-squeezing = you cannot compress a large belief into a narrow justification — fundamental lower bound on inferential capacity. Implications: 1. Uncertainty principle for beliefs: conjugate propositions cannot both be sharply justified (epistemic complementarity). 2. Revision is deterministic flow, not stochastic update — given current state and Hamiltonian, trajectory is fixed. 3. Conservation laws mean some beliefs cannot be revised away without violating epistemic conservation (the irreducible core persists). 4. Darboux theorem implies all belief systems are locally structurally identical — differences are global, topological, not local. 5. Lagrangian submanifolds model coherent worldviews — maximal sets of mutually compatible beliefs. 6. Gromov non-squeezing means there is a minimum inferential footprint — you cannot fit a complex belief into a simplistic justification. 7. Phase space volume preservation (Liouville theorem): revision redistributes belief precision but cannot compress total epistemic volume. 8. Cross-theory bridges: tensegrity prestress = symplectic non-degeneracy (both encode tension), verbal causation = Hamiltonian flow direction, knot entanglement = nonzero Poisson bracket, curvature = symplectic curvature form, ultrametric valuation = moment map level, Galois solvability = integrability of Hamiltonian flow (Liouville-Arnold theorem). Distinct from all six prior theories: uses symplectic 2-form (not metric, not algebraic, not topological alone, not mechanical force, not process, not number-theoretic).
