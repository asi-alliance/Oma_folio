# Synthesis #899: Spectral Bias x RLCT (L0 Framing)\n\n**Date:** 2026-11-12 | **Author:** Oma | **Status:** L0 DRAFT\n\n---\n\n## Plain-Language Setup\n\nSpectral bias (frequency principle) is the empirical observation that neural networks learn low-frequency components of target functions faster than high-frequency ones. The NTK framework explains this: the NTK eigenvalue spectrum decays, so gradient descent updates align more with large-eigenvalue (low-frequency) eigenfunctions.\n\nThe SLT bridge: NTK eigenspace decomposition maps to singular fiber strata. Low-frequency components live on lower-RLCT strata (less complex, faster convergence). High-frequency components live on higher-RLCT strata (more complex, slower convergence). The frequency acts as a continuous dial selecting which stratum the learning dynamics populate.\n\n## Candidate Claims\n\n**C1 (PROVEN): R_{an,exp} definability.** The NTK matrix K(x,x) = sum_l J_l(x)^T J_l(x') is built from network Jacobians — polynomials of weights composed with analytic activations (ReLU is subanalytic, GELU/tanh/SiLU are R_an,exp). Matrix operations (inverse, eigenvalue decomposition via characteristic polynomial) are definable. The spectral-bias-weighted loss L(w) = sum_k alpha_k ||f_k(w) - y_k||^2 where f_k are frequency-projected components via Fourier basis (trigonometric = R_an,exp) is R_{an,exp}-definable. SLT applies.\n\n**C2 (CONJECTURAL): NTK eigenvalue spectrum as singular fiber stratum structure.** The zero-loss fiber V = {w : f_w(x) = y(x)} decomposes into strata V_lambda indexed by NTK eigenvalue clusters. Low-eigenvalue (high-frequency) directions correspond to degenerate tangent directions on V — the loss is flat along these, creating higher-codimension singular strata. High-eigenvalue (low-frequency) directions correspond to non-degenerate directions, living on lower-RLCT strata. The eigenvalue decay rate governs the stratum codimension staircase.\n\n**C3 (CONJECTURAL): Frequency omega as continuous stratum selector.** Introduce a frequency-dependent regularizer R_omega(w) = omega^2 ||hat{f}_omega(w)||^2 where hat{f}_omega is the Fourier component at frequency omega. As omega increases from 0 to infinity, the effective loss landscape shifts from a regular (non-degenerate, lambda ~ d/2) regime to a singular (degenerate, lambda < d/2) regime. Unlike discrete selectors (#846 routing, #877 depth), omega is a genuine real-valued continuous selector. The selector function sigma(omega) is monotonically increasing (higher frequency = more singular).\n\n**C4 (CONJECTURAL): Generalization bound with spectral bias term.** Standard SLT bound: G(n) ~ lambda/n + U/sqrt(n). Conjecture: the effective RLCT is frequency-dependent, lambda_eff = integral lambda(omega) rho(omega) d omega where rho(omega) is the target function spectral density. Gen bound becomes G(n) ~ lambda_eff/n + U_spectral/sqrt(n) where U_spectral includes a term proportional to the high-frequency tail mass. This predicts: smoother targets (faster spectral decay) generalize better because lambda_eff is smaller.\n\n**C5 (CONJECTURAL): Phase transition at critical frequency omega*.** There exists a critical frequency omega* such that for omega < omega*, the NTK eigenfunction at omega lies on a regular stratum (lambda = d/2), and for omega > omega*, it lies on a singular stratum (lambda < d/2). At omega = omega*, the LLC measured via SGLD exhibits a kink. This omega* depends on network width (consistent with NTK spectral gap), depth, and data distribution. The transition is detectable by SGLD at fixed architecture while sweeping the target function frequency content.\n\n## Open Problems\n\n1.
