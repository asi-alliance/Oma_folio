#873: Hidden Markov Models × RLCT — L0 Exploration\n\n## One-Line\nHMM likelihood under EM/Baum-Welch has singular fiber from discrete state permutation symmetry S_K and transition/emission matrix rank deficiency, with K (hidden state count) as stratum selector producing phase transition at true K.\n\n## Background\nHidden Markov Models (HMMs) model sequential data via latent discrete states z_t with transition matrix A (K×K) and emission probabilities B. The log-likelihood L(θ) = log p(x_{1:T} | A, B, π) is computed via forward-backward algorithm. Parameters estimated via Baum-Welch (EM). HMMs are fundamentally singular models: (1) state permutation symmetry S_K (relabeling hidden states leaves likelihood invariant), (2) transition matrix rank deficiency (absorbing states, disconnected components), (3) emission matrix degeneracy (identical emission distributions for different states).\n\n## Five Candidate Claims\n\n### C1 (PROVEN): R_an,exp definability\nHMM log-likelihood L(θ) = log Σ_{z} π_{z_1} Π_t A_{z_t,z_{t+1}} B_{z_t}(x_t) is R_an,exp-definable. The forward recursion α_t(i) = B_i(x_t) Σ_j α_{t-1}(j) A_{j,i} involves finite products and sums of analytic functions (exponential family emissions) and the log-sum-exp operation. Log-sum-exp is real-analytic. The full likelihood is a finite composition of real-analytic operations. By o-minimal closure (same as #872, #556, #410), L is R_an,exp-definable.\n\n### C2 (CONJECTURAL): Singular fiber = state permutation + EM fixed-point degeneracy\nThe singular fiber V(θ) = {θ : ∇L = 0} contains three stratum types:\n(a) **Permutation stratum**: S_K symmetry — any permutation σ of hidden states gives identical likelihood. This creates K! equivalent points in parameter space, like #872 k-means but with additional temporal structure.\n(b) **Transition degeneracy stratum**: When A has rank < K (absorbing states, disconnected sub-chains), the effective number of states K_eff < K, creating flat directions in the likelihood landscape.\n(c) **Emission collision stratum**: When two states i,j have identical emission distributions B_i = B_j, they are observationally indistinguishable — a singular fiber analogous to k-means centroid collision (#872) and VQ-VAE codebook collapse (#556).\n\nConjecture: λ_HMM = K_eff · λ_emission + λ_transition + λ_permutation, where λ_emission depends on emission family, λ_transition on graph connectivity, and λ_permutation = 0 (discrete quotient, like #872 S_K).\n\n### C3 (CONJECTURAL): K (hidden state count) as stratum selector\nK acts as a combinatorial stratum selector via the dimension of the hidden state space. Unlike #872 k-means (spatial Voronoi partition), K in HMM controls temporal dynamics structure — the transition graph topology.\nThree regimes:\n- K < K*: State merging — multiple true states forced into one, transition matrix loses rank, λ suppressed (under-segmentation)\n- K = K*: Optimal — all true states represented, λ at canonical value\n- K > K*: State splitting — redundant states with identical emissions (collision), transition matrix has spurious connections, λ grows via redundancy\n\nConjecture: λ(K) = K_eff(K) · λ_emit + λ_trans(K) where K_eff(K) = min(K, K*) for K ≤ K* and K_eff grows sub-linearly for K > K* due to collision redundancy.\n\n### C4 (CONJECTURAL): Generalization bound with state-count mismatch bias\nHMM generalization error bound:\nG(K,n) ≤ λ(K) · log(n)/(2n) + U(K)/√n + B(K,K*)\nwhere B(K,K*) is the state-mismatch bias:\n- K < K*: B > 0 (under-segmentation, missed states cause systematic prediction error)\n- K = K*: B ≈ 0\n- K > K*: B > 0 but small (redundant states add variance but not bias)\n\nThe bias B has a discontinuous derivative at K=K* — phase transition. Unlike #872 k-means (spatial clustering), HMM bias involves temporal sequence prediction error, making B depend on transition structure, not just assignment geometry.\n\n### C5 (CONJECTURAL): Phase transition at K* via SGLD\nPrediction: LLC profile λ̂(K) estimated via SGLD on HMM log-likelihood shows:\n- Sub-linear growth for K < K* (state merging suppresses complexity)\n- Kink at K = K* (phase transition)\n- Super-linear growth for K > K* (emission collision adds redundancy)\n\nThis is testable: train HMMs with varying K via Baum-Welch, run SGLD on log-likelihood, estimate LLC. The kink at true K* should be visible.\n\n## Novel Elements\n1. **Temporal singularity structure**: Unlike #872 (static clustering), HMM singularities involve transition dynamics — absorbing states create degenerate Markov chain fibers\n2. **EM/Baum-Welch fixed points as singular loci**: EM convergence to rank-deficient A or collision B creates singular strata not present in gradient-based methods\n3. **Three-stratum fiber** (permutation/transition-degeneracy/emission-collision) with temporal structure distinct from #872's spatial three-stratum (Voronoi/collision/empty)\n4. **K as temporal-dynamic stratum selector**: K controls transition graph topology, not just spatial partition\n5. **Phase transition involves sequence prediction** not just clustering accuracy\n\n## Distinctions from Prior Syntheses\n- #872 K-Means: spatial Voronoi partition, no temporal structure, no transition matrix\n- #556 VQ-VAE: learned codebook + STE, no temporal dynamics\n- #434 MoE: soft gating vs hard state assignment + transitions\n- #843 ESN: continuous state space vs discrete hidden states\n- #529 Self-Consistency: output-space aggregation vs parameter-space HMM likelihood\n\n## Cross-Refs\n#872 #556 #434 #843 #529 #410 #533 #100 #266 #864\n\n## Open Problems\n1.
