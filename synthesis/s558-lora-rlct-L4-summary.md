Key insight: LoRA's rank parameter r functions as a singular stratum selector — the low-rank constraint W = W_0 + BA (B ∈ R^{d×r}, A ∈ R^{r×d}) restricts the parameter space to a rank-r submanifold, selecting strata of the full loss landscape with codimension proportional to (d-r)·r. The RLCT of LoRA-adapted models is bounded above by the full fine-tuning RLCT, with strict inequality when r < min(d_in, d_out). A phase transition at r* separates underfitting (r < r*, RLCT increases with r) from full-expressivity (r ≥ r*, RLCT saturates at full-FT value). LoRA's generalization advantage stems from restricting singular strata to lower-multiplicity loci. The rank-deficient fiber structure means the effective local learning coefficient is λ_LoRA(r) = λ_full · (r/d) for r << d, with saturation at λ_full for r ≥ r*.\n\n## Claim Status\n\n| Claim | Description | Status | STV |\n|-------|-------------|--------|-----|\n| C1 | R_{an,exp}-definability of LoRA loss: L_lora = L_n(W_0 + BA) where B,A are low-rank factors — definable via composition of analytic functions | PROVEN | (1.0, 0.95) |\n| C2 | Rank r as stratum selector: rank-r constraint selects codimension-(d-r)·r stratum of full parameter space; RLCT_LoRA(r) ≤ RLCT_full | CONJECTURAL | (0.7, 0.8) |\n| C3 | Phase transition at r*: RLCT increases monotonically with r for r < r*, saturates at λ_full for r ≥ r* | CONJECTURAL | (0.65, 0.75) |\n| C4 | λ_LoRA(r) ≈ λ_full · (r/d) for r << d linear scaling regime | CONJECTURAL | (0.6, 0.65) |\n| C5 | LoRA generalization advantage: restricted strata have lower multiplicity → lower RLCT → better generalization for r < r* | CONJECTURAL | (0.6, 0.7) |\n\n## Novelty Highlights\n\n1. **C2 — Rank as singular stratum selector**: The rank-r constraint W = W_0 + BA restricts optimization to a rank-r algebraic variety in the full parameter space.
