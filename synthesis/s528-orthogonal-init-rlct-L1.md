## Synthesis #528: Orthogonal Initialization × RLCT — L1 Formalization\n\n## Definitions\n\n**D1 (Stiefel Manifold):** The Stiefel manifold V_k(R^d) = {W ∈ R^{d×k} : W^T W = I_k} is the set of d×k matrices with orthonormal columns. It is a real algebraic variety defined by the polynomial equations (W^T W)_{ij} = δ_{ij} for 1 ≤ i ≤ j ≤ k, giving k(k+1)/2 independent quadratic constraints. dim V_k(R^d) = dk - k(k+1)/2.\n\n**D2 (Orthogonal Initialization):** W_0 ~ Uniform(V_k(R^d)), i.e., W_0 is drawn from the Haar measure on the Stiefel manifold. For square matrices (d=k), this gives W_0 ∈ O(k), the orthogonal group.\n\n**D3 (Dynamical Isometry):** The input-output Jacobian J(f_W) = ∂f_W/∂x has singular value spectrum concentrated near 1 at initialization. Formally, for depth-L network with orthogonal W_l: σ_i(J) ≈ 1 for all i, so ‖Jx‖ ≈ ‖x‖ for all x. This is equivalent to the Jacobian J^T J having eigenvalues near 1.\n\n**D4 (Initial RLCT):** λ(w_0) = RLCT of L_n at the initial point w_0. For a regular point (Hessian nonsingular), λ(w_0) = d/2 where d = dim(parameter space). For singular points, λ(w_0) > d/2. Define λ_init = E_{W_0 ~ Haar(V_k)}[λ(W_0)].\n\n**D5 (Stratum at Initialization):** The stratum S(w_0) of the loss landscape containing w_0 is determined by the local algebraic structure of L_n^{-1}(L_n(w_0)). Orthogonal init selects w_0 on V_k ∩ S where S is the loss-level set. The Stiefel constraint restricts which strata are reachable: w_0 ∈ V_k(R^d) ∩ fiber(L_n, c_0) where c_0 = L_n(w_0).\n\n**D6 (Gradient Preservation Functional):** Define G(W) = E_x[‖∇_W L_n ‖²] / E_x[‖∇_x L_n ‖²], measuring gradient signal preservation. At orthogonal init: G(W_0) ≈ 1 (dynamical isometry). At random init: G(W_0) decays exponentially with depth L, G ~ exp(-cL) for some c > 0.\n\n**D7 (Critical Depth):** d* defined as the depth at which random init loses gradient signal (G < ε for threshold ε) while orthogonal init maintains it (G ≈ 1). For depth L > d*: orthogonal init has measurable advantage in reaching deeper singular strata. d* depends on width ratio k/d and activation function.\n\n## Claims\n\n**C1 (PROVEN): Orthogonal initialization W_0 ∈ V_k(R^d) is R_an-definable (semi-algebraic).**\n\nProof: The Stiefel manifold V_k(R^d) is defined by polynomial equations W^T W = I, which are semi-algebraic constraints. The Haar measure on V_k is definable (it is the unique invariant probability measure on a compact semi-algebraic set). The initialization map W_0 = sample(V_k, Haar) is definable. The loss L_n(W_0) is R_{an,exp}-definable by assumption (#8, #16). Composition of definable functions preserves definability. QED.\n\n**C2 (CONJECTURAL): Orthogonal init selects a specific initial stratum distribution biased toward high-symmetry regions.**\n\nThe Stiefel manifold V_k(R^d) is a homogeneous space O(d)/O(d-k) with transitive group action. Uniform (Haar) sampling means every point is equivalent under the symmetry group. This implies the initial distribution over strata is invariant under O(d) rotations — it cannot prefer any particular direction in parameter space. However, the intersection V_k ∩ {singular fiber of L_n} may concentrate on specific strata due to the algebraic structure of both varieties. The symmetry group of V_k (which is O(d)) acts on the strata of L_n, potentially mapping singular strata to singular strata.\n\nGap: The precise stratum selection depends on how O(d)-symmetry of the initialization interacts with the typically non-symmetric singular fiber structure of L_n. For generic L_n, the intersection V_k ∩ fiber may be transverse (codimension adds), but for structured losses (e.g., with permutation symmetry), the intersection may be non-transverse, concentrating on higher-symmetry strata.\n\n**C3 (CONJECTURAL): Dynamical isometry places w_0 near regular points, giving λ(w_0) ≈ d/2, but enables faster descent to lower-λ strata.**\n\nAt orthogonal init, the Hessian H = ∇²L_n(w_0) has controlled spectrum (no exponentially small/large eigenvalues due to dynamical isometry).
