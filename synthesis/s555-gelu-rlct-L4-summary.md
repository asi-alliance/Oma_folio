Key insight: GELU's smooth (C-infinity) nonlinearity x·Φ(x) replaces ReLU's piecewise-linear kink with a Gaussian-weighted smooth transition. Under exact arithmetic, GELU's analyticity (entire function composition) preserves the definability class (C1, proven). The critical observation is that GELU eliminates the ReLU dead-zone singularity (x<0, output=0) but introduces a Gaussian-curved transition region that creates a softer singular fiber — the RLCT of a GELU network is bounded above by the equivalent ReLU network's RLCT, with strict inequality when dead neurons exist. The temperature-like steepness parameter in tanh-approximation GELU acts as a stratum selector interpolating between identity (smooth, low RLCT) and ReLU-like (sharp, higher RLCT) regimes.\n\n## Claim Status\n\n| Claim | Description | Status | STV |\n|-------|-------------|--------|-----|\n| C1 | R_{an,exp}-definability of GELU: x·Φ(x) is analytic (composition of entire functions), definable via analytic closure | PROVEN | (1.0, 0.95) |\n| C2 | GELU eliminates ReLU dead-zone singularity; singular fiber of GELU network is a deformation of ReLU fiber with smoothed kink | CONJECTURAL | (0.7, 0.8) |\n| C3 | RLCT_GELU ≤ RLCT_ReLU with strict inequality when dead neurons exist; smooth transition reduces multiplicity at singular locus | CONJECTURAL | (0.6, 0.75) |\n| C4 | Tanh-approximation steepness parameter acts as stratum selector interpolating identity↔ReLU regimes; phase transition at critical steepness | CONJECTURAL | (0.5, 0.65) |\n| C5 | Empirical: GELU networks show lower LLC than ReLU networks via SGLD; training dynamics favor GELU's softer singular geometry | CONJECTURAL | (0.5, 0.65) |\n\n## Novelty Highlights\n\n1. **C2 — Dead-zone singularity elimination**: ReLU's dead zone (x<0 → 0) creates a positive-codimension singular stratum where gradients vanish. GELU's smooth Gaussian CDF replaces this with a region of small but nonzero gradient — the singular fiber deforms rather than develops a hard kink. This is the first SLT-theoretic explanation for GELU's empirical superiority over ReLU.\n\n2. **C3 — RLCT inequality GELU ≤ ReLU**: The smooth transition region in GELU reduces the multiplicity (order of singularity) at the critical locus compared to ReLU's sharp kink. When dead neurons exist (ReLU neurons stuck at 0), the ReLU RLCT includes contributions from these dead-zone singularities; GELU's smooth analog has strictly lower RLCT.\n\n3. **C4 — Steepness as stratum selector**: The tanh-approximation GELU(x) ≈ 0.5x[1 + tanh(√(2/π)(x + 0.044715x³))] has an implicit steepness controlled by the polynomial coefficients. Increasing steepness pushes GELU toward ReLU-like behavior (sharper transition, higher RLCT); decreasing it pushes toward smooth identity (lower RLCT). A phase transition at critical steepness separates these regimes.\n\n4. **C5 — Empirical testability**: SGLD-based LLC measurements comparing GELU vs ReLU networks at matched architectures can directly test C3's inequality prediction. The LLC gap should correlate with the fraction of dead neurons in the ReLU network.\n\n5. **Smooth-vs-piecewise RLCT framework**: Extends the activation-function RLCT analysis (#134 self-attention,
