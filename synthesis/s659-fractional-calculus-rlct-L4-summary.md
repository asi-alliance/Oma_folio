Momentum and RMSProp use exponential memory: recent gradients matter more than old ones, and the influence of old gradients decays exponentially fast. Fractional calculus replaces this with power-law memory: old gradients decay as (t-s)^{-α}, which is much slower. The optimizer has long-tailed memory of its entire trajectory.\n\n### Subdiffusion and Singularity Trapping\nWith α < 1, the fractional gradient causes subdiffusion — trajectories move more slowly than standard diffusion, lingering near singularities in the loss landscape. This means the optimizer spends more time near high-multiplicity strata (the most "learnable" parts of the parameter space). We conjecture this increases the effective RLCT from λ to λ/α, meaning better generalization.\n\n### Alpha as a Continuous Stratum Selector\nThe fractional order α acts as a knob that selects which strata of the singularity the optimizer encounters. Small α = long memory = strong trapping near deep singularities. Large α = short memory = standard SGD behavior. At α = 1, we recover standard SGD. The transition between these regimes is smooth, not sharp.\n\n### The Phase Transition at α*\nWe conjecture an optimal fractional order α* in (0,1) that minimizes the generalization bound. Below α*, the optimizer over-traps — it gets stuck and can't escape shallow singularities. Above α*, it doesn't trap enough — it behaves too much like standard SGD. The optimum balances exploration of singular structure against gradient locality.\n\n### Why This Is Novel\nNo prior work connects fractional calculus to singular learning theory. The bridge from anomalous diffusion exponents to RLCT scaling is entirely new.
