# Synthesis #529: Self-Consistency Decoding × RLCT — L4 Summary\n\n**Date:** 2026-11-07 | **Author:** Oma | **Status:** L4 SUMMARY (L0-L3 COMPLETE)\n\n---\n\n## Overview\n\nFirst SLT analysis of self-consistency decoding (sampling multiple paths at temperature τ and aggregating via majority vote) through the lens of Real Log Canonical Threshold. Key insight: the output-space tie-boundary singularity — where multiple answers tie for the majority vote — creates a singular fiber in the parameter space fundamentally different from per-path or per-token singularities. Temperature τ acts as a stratum selector on the fiber neighborhood, controlling how broadly the sampling distribution covers the answer landscape. At critical τ*, a phase transition occurs between confident single-answer domination (low τ) and diffuse multi-answer coverage (high τ), producing a U-shaped effective RLCT curve.\n\n## Claim Status\n\n| Claim | Description | Status | STV |\n|-------|-------------|--------|-----|\n| C1 | R_{an,exp}-definability of self-consistency decoding via softmax sampling + count indicators + argmax composition closure | PROVEN | (1.0, 0.95) |\n| C2 | Temperature τ as fiber neighborhood stratum selector: maps to StratumConsensum selecting dominant answer strata | CONJECTURAL | (0.5, 0.7) |\n| C3 | Majority vote as dominant stratum consensus: argmax count ↔ highest posterior mass; f_agree estimates P(Θ_{a*}) | CONJECTURAL | (0.5, 0.7) |\n| C4 | Generalization bound G_SC ≤ λ_eff(τ,N)/N + U(τ)/√N with path diversity | CONJECTURAL | (0.5, 0.7) |\n| C5 | Phase transition at critical τ*: U-shaped λ_eff(τ,N); dominant stratum switches from single-answer to multi-answer | CONJECTURAL | (0.5, 0.7) |\n\n## Novelty Highlights\n\n1. **Output-space aggregation singularity**: Unlike parameter-space methods (weight tying #467, LoRA #173) or token-level methods (nucleus sampling #359), self-consistency creates singularities at the answer-aggregation level — the tie boundary where two or more answers receive equal vote counts is a semi-algebraic variety in output space.\n2. **C2 — τ as fiber neighborhood stratum selector**: Temperature controls the breadth of the sampling distribution over answers, effectively selecting which strata of the fiber neighborhood dominate. Low τ concentrates on one stratum (confident); high τ spreads across strata (diverse). No prior work treats decoding temperature as a singularity-stratifying parameter.\n3. **C3 — Majority vote ↔ posterior mass equivalence**: The agreement fraction f_agree = count(a*)/N is a Monte Carlo estimator of P(Θ_{a*}), connecting the discrete voting mechanism to continuous posterior geometry. Higher f_agree implies lower λ_eff (more confident model).\n4. **C4 — Path diversity in generalization bound**: The bound G_SC ≤ λ_eff(τ,N)/N + U(τ)/√N explicitly includes path diversity through λ_eff(τ,N), which decreases with higher f_agree. This is the first generalization bound for ensemble-vote decoding that incorporates RLCT.\n5. **C5 — τ* phase transition**: At critical temperature τ*, the dominant singular stratum switches from single-answer domination (low τ, high λ_eff due to concentrated but sharp singularity) to multi-answer coverage (high τ, lower λ_eff but higher approximation error), producing a U-shaped generalization curve testable via SGLD.\n\n## Cross-Reference Map\n\n- **#8 (RLCT basics):** Definability framework foundation\n- **#170 (Ensemble methods):** Voting aggregation baseline\n- **#232
