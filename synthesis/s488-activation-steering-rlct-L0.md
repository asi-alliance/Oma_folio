This does not modify parameters — it modifies the forward pass trajectory directly.\n\n### Key RLCT Bridge\n\nActivation steering perturbs the function computed by the model without changing parameters. The RLCT bridge is novel because it operates on the *realization* (function space) rather than the *parameter space*:\n\n1. **Direction as stratum selector:** The steering vector v selects a one-dimensional subspace of activation space. Adding α·v moves the activation along a fiber direction. If this direction lies within the tangent space of a singular stratum, steering preserves the RLCT; if it crosses strata, steering changes which stratum the output lies on.\n\n2. **Composite singularity:** The steered model f_α(x) = f(x, h+αv) creates a family of functions parameterized by α. The RLCT of f_α as a function of model parameters W is unchanged (W is frozen), but the *effective* RLCT of the input-output map changes because the steering perturbs which region of the loss landscape the model traverses.\n\n3. **Generalization bound for steered models:** The steered model's generalization error depends on λ_α, the RLCT of the effective loss surface induced by steering. Since steering doesn't change the parameter-space RLCT, the benefit must come from changing the *effective* loss landscape — analogous to test-time training (#66) but without gradient updates.\n\n### Five Claims\n\n- **C1 (Provable):** The steered forward pass h' = h + α·v is a polynomial (affine) perturbation, so the steered loss L(f(x, h+αv)) is in R_{an,exp} when L is.\n- **C2 (Conjectural):** The steering direction v lies in activation space, which is the pushforward of parameter space through the network. The singular fiber structure of the steered model is determined by how v intersects the tangent cone of the singular locus in activation space.\n- **C3 (Conjectural):** The steering coefficient α acts as a continuous stratum selector — small α keeps the model on the same stratum, large α crosses into adjacent strata. A phase transition at α* changes the effective RLCT.\n- **C4 (Conjectural):** The generalization bound for steered models is G_α ~ λ_α / n + U_α / √n, where λ_α is the effective RLCT induced by steering, and λ_α may differ from the unsteered λ because the activation-space perturbation changes which singular fiber the output lies on.\n- **C5 (Conjectural):** Steering toward "safer" or "more aligned" directions corresponds to moving toward less singular (smoother) strata in activation space, reducing λ_α and improving generalization — explaining why steered models can be both more capable and more reliable.\n\n### Cross-References\n\n- #66 (Test-Time Training × RLCT) — inference-time modification without parameter change\n- #205 (Activation Steering × RLCT) — prior candidate (L2 passed, this is the full L0)\n- #165/#370 (Contrastive Learning × RLCT) — steering vector from contrasting prompts\n- #224 (Label Smoothing × RLCT) — regularization as desingularization\n- #425 (Zoneout × RLCT) — conditional fiber perturbation\n\n### Open Questions\n\n1.
