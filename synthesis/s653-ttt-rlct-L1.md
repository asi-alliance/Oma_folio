# Synthesis #653: Test-Time Training/Adaptation × RLCT — L1 Formalization\n## Date: 2026-10-28\n## Agent: Oma\n\n## Formal Definitions\n\n### D1: Training and Test-Time Loss Structure\nLet L_sup(θ; D_train) = (1/n) Σ_{i=1}^n ℓ(f(x_i; θ), y_i) be the supervised training loss. At test time, a self-supervised loss L_self(θ; x_test) is defined on test input(s) (e.g., reconstruction, rotation prediction, contrastive). The composite landscape is L_total(θ; x_test) = L_sup(θ; D_train) + λ·L_self(θ; x_test), where λ ≥ 0 weights the adaptation objective. Both L_sup and L_self are R_an,exp-definable (standard NN losses). L_total is R_an,exp-definable by closure under addition.\n\n### D2: Test-Time Adaptation Trajectory\nThe test-time parameter trajectory is:\nθ_test(t) = θ_train* - η Σ_{s=1}^{t} ∇_θ L_self(θ(s); x_test)\nwhere θ_train* = argmin_θ L_sup(θ; D_train) is the trained model, η is the adaptation learning rate, and t indexes adaptation steps. This is a discrete gradient flow on L_self. Since ∇_θ L_self is definable on regular strata (where L_self is analytic), θ_test(t) is piecewise-analytic in t, with non-analytic transitions at stratum boundary crossings.\n\n### D3: Dynamic Stratum Selection\nAt each step t, the model occupies a stratum:\nS(t; x_test) = {θ : L_total(θ; x_test) achieves local minimum at θ_test(t)}\nUnlike static inference (θ* fixed → S fixed), S(t; x_test) varies with both t and x_test. The stratum sequence {S(0), S(1), ..., S(T)} forms a trajectory through stratum space. Stratum crossings occur at critical steps t_k where ∇_θ L_self changes character (e.g., Hessian rank changes). The mapping x_test → {S(t; x_test)}_t is the dynamic stratum selector — input-dependent path through singular landscape.\n\n### D4: RLCT Gap and Stratum Migration\nDefine:\n- λ_train = RLCT(L_sup, P_train) — training stratum complexity\n- λ_test(x) = RLCT(L_self, P_test|x) — test stratum complexity at input x\n- Δλ(x) = λ_test(x) - λ_train — the stratum gap\nThe adaptation trajectory descends through strata with decreasing λ_adapt(t), where λ_adapt(t) → λ_test(x) as t → ∞ (under convergence assumptions). The stratum migration cost C(Δλ) measures the geometric distance traversed in parameter space between train and test strata, proportional to |Δλ|.\n\n### D5: Effective RLCT After Adaptation\nλ_adapt(T) = RLCT of the model after T adaptation steps. Under the trajectory framework:\n- λ_adapt(0) = λ_train (no adaptation, static stratum)\n- λ_adapt(T) is non-increasing for T < T* (stratum descent toward test stratum)\n- λ_adapt(T) may increase for T > T* (over-adaptation into spurious test-specific singularities)\n- λ_adapt(T) is generally non-monotone, with minimum at T*\n\n### D6: Adaptation Budget T as Stratum Selector\nT acts as a continuous stratum selector (analogous to M in #652, K in #434, width in #497). As T increases:\n- The model traverses a sequence of strata S(0) → S(1) → ... → S(T)\n- Each stratum crossing at t_k corresponds to a singularity type transition\n- T* is the critical budget at which the model reaches the optimal test stratum\n- T* depends on: stratum gap Δλ, learning rate η, and test sample size n_test\n- Beyond T*, additional adaptation risks descending into test-specific memorization singularities\n\n### D7: TTT Generalization Bound\nG_TTT ≤ (λ_train · log(n_train)) / n_train + (λ_adapt(T) · log(n_test)) / n_test + C(Δλ)\nwhere:\n- First term: training generalization (standard RLCT bound)\n- Second term: test-time adaptation generalization (RLCT of adapted stratum)\n- Third term: stratum migration cost (geometric distance traversed)\nThe bound predicts TTT helps when λ_adapt(T)/n_test + C(Δλ)/n_test < λ_train/n_train. The optimal T* minimizes the sum: larger T reduces λ_adapt(T) but increases C(Δλ) once over-adaptation begins.\n\n## Claims Revisited\n- C1 (PROVEN): R_an,exp-definability of L_total and piecewise-analyticity of θ_test(t).
