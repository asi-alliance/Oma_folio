# Synthesis #666: Euler Characteristic Curve × RLCT — L0 Scoping\n\n## Date: 2026-10-28\n\n## Motivation\n\nThe Euler Characteristic Curve (ECC) χ(t) = Σ_{k≥0} (-1)^k β_k(K^{-1}(-∞,t]) records the alternating sum of Betti numbers of KL sublevel sets as the threshold t sweeps from 0 to ∞. For singular models, the zero-fiber W* = K^{-1}(0) is singular (#665), causing topological transitions in the sublevel set filtration.\n\nKey conjecture: the asymptotic behavior of χ(t) as t→0^+ is governed by the RLCT λ. Specifically, χ(t) ~ C · t^{-λ} or χ(t) ~ C · t^{λ} depending on sign conventions, with λ = RLCT of the singularity at W*.\n\nThis connects:\n- #665 (fiber bundle: singular fibers cause EC jumps)\n- #50 (barcodes: individual Betti number persistence)\n- #69 (codim/2 formula: fiber dimension jumps)\n- Motivic zeta functions (Denef-Loeser: arc space integrals encode singularity type via Euler characteristic)\n- Real topological zeta functions (specialized from motivic, invariant of singularity type)\n\n## Five Claims\n\n**C1 (PROVEN): The ECC of KL sublevel sets is R_{an,exp}-definable.**\n\nThe ECC χ(t) = Σ(-1)^k β_k({θ : K(θ) ≤ t}) is a function of t. K is R_{an,exp}-definable (#665 C1). Sublevel sets {K ≤ t} are definable sets. By o-minimal topology (van den Dries), Betti numbers of definable sets are finite and the ECC is a constructible function of t (definable Euler characteristic, as in motivic integration theory). The ECC is piecewise-constant with jumps at critical values of K. Constructible functions on R_{an,exp} are definable → χ(t) is definable.\n\n**C2 (CONJECTURAL): The ECC has asymptotic behavior χ(t) ~ C · t^{-λ} as t→0^+, where λ is the RLCT.**\n\nMotivation: The RLCT zeta function ζ(z) = ∫ exp(-z·n·K(θ)) dθ ~ z^{-λ} has a pole at z=0 of order λ (#666 prior work, validated numerically). The Euler characteristic of sublevel sets should track the same singularity: χ(t) measures the topology of {K ≤ t}, which thickens near W* at a rate controlled by λ. For a regular model (λ = d/2): χ(t) ~ C · t^{-d/2} (Morse theory: sublevel set of non-degenerate quadratic has EC growing as t^{-d/2}). For a singular model with RLCT λ < d/2: the growth is slower, χ(t) ~ C · t^{-λ}. This generalizes Morse theory to singular landscapes.\n\nConnections: Motivic zeta function Z_motiv(T) = Σ χ_n · T^n where χ_n are Euler characteristics of contact loci (Denef-Loeser). The pole order of Z_motiv at T=1 corresponds to the RLCT (via the monodromy conjecture). The ECC is the real analogue: χ(t) plays the role of the motivic zeta function's coefficients.\n\n**C3 (CONJECTURAL): The ECC provides a novel RLCT estimator — the power-law exponent of χ(t) near t=0 gives λ directly.**\n\nMethod: Compute sublevel sets {K(θ) ≤ t} for decreasing t, compute Betti numbers β_k via persistent homology, form χ(t) = Σ(-1)^k β_k, fit χ(t) ~ C · t^{-α} to extract α ≈ λ.\n\nAdvantage over existing methods:\n- SGLD: requires MCMC equilibration, sensitive to temperature (failed in prior experiments)\n- Sublevel-set volume V(ε) ~ ε^λ (RLCT-TDA v0.5): requires 10000 samples for 2% accuracy\n- ECC method: uses topological invariant (EC), potentially more stable than volume estimation because EC is integer-valued and robust to noise (alternating sum cancels spurious features)\n\nCaveat: The alternating sum may cancel real signal if positive and negative Betti numbers grow at the same rate. Need to verify that the leading-order exponent survives cancellation.\n\n**C4 (CONJECTURAL): Toric resolution of singularities corresponds to stratification of the ECC into contributions from each exceptional divisor.**\n\nUnder toric resolution (#69, #50, GENESIS): K∘g(θ) = K̃(θ) where g: M̃ → Θ is the resolution map. The sublevel set {K ≤ t} lifts to {K̃ ≤ t} which is a union of pieces indexed by the exceptional divisors E_i. Each E_i contributes a term to the EC: χ(t) = Σ_i χ_i(t) where χ_i(t) ~ C_i · t^{-λ_i} and λ_i = (codim-related exponent from Newton polyhedron). The total χ(t) is dominated by the largest λ_i (most singular stratum), consistent with RLCT = max of stratum RLCTs (Watanabe).\n\nThis gives a topological decomposition: the ECC decomposes into stratum contributions, each corresponding to a fan cone in the toric resolution. The fan refinement (#50) changes the ECC by resolving crossings, splitting one topological transition into multiple.\n\n**C5 (CONJECTURAL): Bayesian phase transitions correspond to discontinuities or critical scaling changes in the ECC at the effective threshold t_β ~ λ/(β·n).**\n\nAt temperature β, posterior support is {K ≤ t_β} with t_β ~ λ/(β·n). As β increases, t_β → 0, and the posterior "sees" finer topological structure. Phase transitions occur when t_β crosses a critical value of K (where the sublevel set topology changes). The ECC has a jump at each critical value; the magnitude of the jump encodes the stratum complexity. The RLCT phase transition (#665 C5, #52) corresponds to the dominant ECC contribution switching from one stratum to another as t_β decreases.\n\n## Novelty Assessment\n\n- #50 covered persistent homology barcodes × RLCT (individual Betti numbers, persistence length ≤ RLCT, compression encoding)\n- #666 introduces the ECC (alternating sum) as a unified topological invariant whose asymptotic growth rate directly encodes λ\n- Connection to motivic zeta functions (Denef-Loeser) is new in the RLCT context\n- The ECC estimator (C3) is a novel alternative to SGLD and volume estimation\n- Toric stratification of ECC (C4) extends #50's barcode decomposition\n\n## Cross-References (10)\n#665 (fiber bundle, zero-fiber), #50 (barcodes × RLCT), #69 (codim/2, Newton non-deg), #8 (RLCT foundation), #16 (gen bound), #52 (phase transitions), #81 (RLCT vs Kolmogorov), #172 (distinction calculus, strata), #298 (orthogonal init resolution), RLCT-TDA v0.5 (sublevel-set volume method)\n\n## Open Questions\n1.
