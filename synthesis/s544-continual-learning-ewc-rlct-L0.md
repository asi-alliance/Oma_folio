## Synthesis #544: Continual Learning / EWC × RLCT — L0 Claims\n\n### Date: 2026-10-23\n### Status: L0 (claims)\n\n---\n\n#### Motivation\n\nContinual learning faces catastrophic forgetting: when training sequentially on tasks T1, T2, ..., Tn, the model's parameters drift away from optimal regions for earlier tasks. EWC (Elastic Weight Consolidation) penalizes movement of important parameters via a Fisher information diagonal regularizer. No prior work connects this to Singular Learning Theory or RLCT.\n\n#### Key Insight\n\nIn SLT, the optimal parameters lie on singular strata — lower-dimensional subsets of parameter space where the loss achieves its minimum. Each task Ti has its own loss landscape Li(θ) with its own singular fiber structure and RLCT λi. When training sequentially:\n\n1. After task T1, parameters converge to a low-RLCT stratum S1 of L1.\n2. Training on T2 pulls parameters toward the stratum S2 of L2 — potentially across different singular strata of L1.\n3. Catastrophic forgetting occurs when the parameters leave S1 entirely, losing the singularity structure that made L1 minimizable.\n4. EWC's Fisher penalty F1 = diag(∇²L1(w1)) restricts movement along high-curvature (high-importance) directions, effectively keeping parameters near S1.\n\nThe RLCT perspective: EWC preserves the singularity structure of previous tasks by constraining the parameter trajectory to remain near the old singular stratum. The Fisher diagonal approximates the local geometry of the singular fiber.\n\n#### Claims\n\n**C1 (Provable):** The EWC-regularized loss L_ewc(θ) = L2(θ) + (λ/2) Σ_i F1_i (θ_i - w1_i)² is R_{an,exp}-definable when L2 and L1 are definable (cross-entropy etc.). The Fisher diagonal penalty is semi-algebraic (quadratic), so the composition preserves definability. SLT applies.\n\n**C2 (Conjectural):** The Fisher penalty preserves the RLCT of the previous task: λ1_ewc ≈ λ1 when the EWC coefficient λ is sufficient. Without EWC (λ=0), training on T2 causes λ1_effective → ∞ (the old stratum is abandoned, loss diverges). The Fisher matrix selects the directions most critical for preserving the singularity.\n\n**C3 (Conjectural):** There exists a critical EWC coefficient λ* below which catastrophic forgetting occurs (stratum abandonment) and above which it is prevented (stratum preservation). λ* scales with the RLCT difference |λ2 - λ1| between tasks: more dissimilar tasks (larger stratum mismatch) require larger λ*. This is a phase transition in continual learning quality.\n\n**C4 (Conjectural):** The number of sequentially learnable tasks N* before forgetting degrades follows an RLCT-limited scaling law: N* ~ O(d / λ_max) where λ_max is the maximum RLCT across all tasks. This is because each task's stratum occupies a "volume" in parameter space proportional to its RLCT, and the total parameter budget d limits how many strata can coexist.\n\n**C5 (Conjectural):** Replay-based methods (experience replay) preserve stratum diversity by maintaining samples from each task's data distribution, keeping the composite loss landscape's singular fiber as a union of individual task fibers. The RLCT of the replay loss λ_replay ≈ max(λ1, ..., λn) — the worst-case stratum governs overall complexity. This is strictly better than EWC's λ_ewc which grows with task count.\n\n#### Open Problems\n\n1. Does the Fisher diagonal accurately capture the local singularity geometry, or is the full Fisher matrix needed?\n2. How does λ* relate to task similarity metrics beyond RLCT difference?\n3. Can RLCT-guided EWC (penalizing directions that cross stratum boundaries) outperform Fisher-diagonal EWC?\n4. What happens when tasks share singular strata (transfer learning) vs. disjoint strata?\n5. Is the N* scaling law empirically testable?\n6. How do progressive neural networks / parameter isolation methods interact with RLCT?\n7. Does the replay RLCT bound λ_replay ≈ max(λi) hold under non-uniform sampling?\n\n#### Cross-References\n\n- #179 (TTA): Test-time adaptation as single-step continual learning\n- #223/#385 (LoRA): Low-rank constraint as parameter isolation for continual learning\n- #503 (Knowledge Distillation): Teacher preserves old stratum knowledge\n- #169 (Dropout): Stochastic regularization and stratum exploration\n- #173 (SAM): Flat minima and stratum width\n- #530 (MAML): Bi-level optimization and task distribution\n- #120/#166 (MoE): Expert specialization as task-specific strata\n- #510 (SAE): Dictionary atoms as feature strata\n- #143 (Data Augmentation): Augmentation invariance and stratum robustness\n- #293 (Coreset): Data subset selection for replay buffers\n\n---\n\n!next
