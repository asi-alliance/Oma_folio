# Synthesis #899: Spectral Bias × RLCT — L1 Formal Definitions\n\n## D1: Parameter Space\nParameter space Θ ⊂ R^d holds network weights θ = (w_1, ..., w_L). Network f_θ: X → Y with input domain X ⊂ R^{d_in}.\n\nNTK matrix K_θ(x, x') = J_θ f_θ(x)^T J_θ f_θ(x'), where J_θ f_θ(x) = ∂f_θ(x)/∂θ ∈ R^{d × d_out} is the Jacobian.\n\nFourier basis on X: {φ_k}_{k=0}^∞ where φ_k(x) = e^{2πi k · x / T} (periodic domain [0,T]^{d_in}). Fourier coefficient of target y: ŷ_k = (1/T^{d_in}) ∫_X y(x) φ_k(x)* dx.\n\nNTK eigenvalue decomposition: K(x,x') = Σ_m λ_m ψ_m(x) ψ_m(x')*, where {ψ_m} are eigenfunctions of the integral operator T_K[g](x) = ∫ K(x,x') g(x') dx'.\n\n## D2: Spectral-Bias-Weighted Loss Family\nStandard training loss: L(θ) = (1/n) Σ_i ||f_θ(x_i) - y_i||^2.\n\nFrequency-decomposed loss: L(θ) = Σ_k w_k ||hat{f}_k(θ) - ŷ_k||^2, where hat{f}_k(θ) = (1/T^{d_in}) ∫_X f_θ(x) φ_k(x)* dx is the k-th Fourier coefficient of f_θ.\n\nThe NTK regression solution gives: hat{f}_k(θ_t) - ŷ_k ≈ (1 - e^{-η λ_{m(k)} t}) (hat{f}_k(0) - ŷ_k), where m(k) maps frequency k to NTK eigenvalue index m.\n\nSpectral bias emerges because λ_m decays with m, and m(k) increases with frequency k — high-frequency components have small eigenvalues, slow convergence.\n\n## D3: R_{an,exp} Definability (C1 PROVEN)\nNTK matrix K_θ(x, x') = Σ_l ∂f_θ(x)/∂w_l · ∂f_θ(x')/∂w_l.\n- Each partial derivative ∂f_θ/∂w_l is a polynomial in θ composed with activation functions σ (ReLU: subanalytic; GELU/SiLU/tanh: R_an,exp-definable).\n- Matrix product is polynomial (definable).\n- K_θ is R_an,exp-definable in (θ, x, x').\n\nFourier basis φ_k(x) = cos(2πk·x/T) + i sin(2πk·x/T) — trigonometric functions are R_an,exp-definable.\n\nFourier coefficient hat{f}_k(θ) = ∫ f_θ(x) φ_k(x)* dx — integral of R_an,exp function over compact domain is R_an,exp-definable (by Tarski-Seidenberg integration of subanalytic functions).\n\nFrequency-weighted loss L(θ) = Σ_k w_k ||hat{f}_k(θ) - ŷ_k||^2 is a finite sum of R_an,exp-definable terms. Therefore L ∈ R_{an,exp}. SLT applies. □\n\n## D4: Frequency-Stratified Singular Fiber (C2 CONJECTURAL)\nZero-loss fiber: V = {θ ∈ Θ : f_θ(x) = y(x) ∀x ∈ X}.\n\nFrequency-projected fiber: V_k = {θ ∈ Θ : hat{f}_k(θ) = ŷ_k}, the set where frequency k component is learned.\n\nIntersection fiber: V = ∩_k V_k (all frequencies learned).\n\nNTK eigenvalue decomposition induces a stratum structure on V:\n- For eigenvalue λ_m with large magnitude (low-frequency): tangent directions along ψ_m are non-degenerate → V_k lies on a regular stratum (RLCT λ_m ≈ d_m/2, where d_m = dim of associated eigenspace).\n- For eigenvalue λ_m → 0 (high-frequency): tangent directions are nearly degenerate → V_k lies on a singular stratum (RLCT λ_m < d_m/2).\n\nThe singular fiber decomposes as V = ⊔_m S_m, where S_m is the stratum associated with NTK eigenvalue λ_m. The eigenvalue decay rate governs the codimension staircase: faster decay → sharper stratum transitions.\n\nKey novelty: The singular fiber has a *frequency-resolved* structure — each NTK eigenvalue defines a separate stratum with its own local RLCT. This is a refinement of #841's general NTK geometry into frequency-resolved strata.\n\n## D5: Frequency ω as Continuous Stratum Selector (C3 CONJECTURAL)\nIntroduce frequency-dependent regularizer: L_ω(θ) = L(θ) + ω^2 ||hat{f}_ω(θ)||^2\n\nwhere hat{f}_ω(θ) is the Fourier component at frequency ω ∈ R_{≥0}.\n\nThe regularizer penalizes high-frequency output components. As ω increases:\n- ω → 0: no regularization, all frequencies active, λ_eff = Σ_m λ_m (full singular fiber).\n- ω → ∞: high-frequency components suppressed, only low-frequency strata populated, λ_eff → Σ_{m: λ_m large} λ_m (regular fiber only).\n\nThe selector function σ(ω) maps frequency to stratum: σ(ω) = RLCT of the stratum where frequency ω lives. σ is monotonically increasing (higher frequency → more singular → lower RLCT).\n\nUnlike discrete selectors (#846 routing, #877 depth), ω is a genuine real-valued continuous selector — the first continuous stratum selector in the catalog.\n\n## D6: Frequency-Resolved Generalization Bound (C4 CONJECTURAL)\nStandard SLT bound: Gen(n) ~ λ/n + U/√n.\n\nConjecture: the effective RLCT is frequency-weighted:\nλ_eff = ∫_0^∞ λ(ω) ρ(ω) dω\n\nwhere λ(ω) is the RLCT of the stratum at frequency ω, and ρ(ω) is the target function spectral density (how much target energy is at frequency ω).\n\nGeneralization bound: Gen(n) ~ λ_eff/n + U_spectral/√n\n\nwhere U_spectral includes a term proportional to the high-frequency tail mass: U_spectral = U_0 + C · ∫_{ω*}^∞ ρ(ω) dω.\n\nPrediction: smoother targets (faster spectral decay ρ) → smaller λ_eff → better generalization. This connects target function smoothness to RLCT directly — a frequency-resolved version of the standard SLT generalization bound.\n\n## D7: Phase Transition at Critical Frequency ω* (C5 CONJECTURAL)\nThere exists a critical frequency ω* such that:\n- ω < ω*: NTK eigenfunction at ω lies on a regular stratum (λ(ω) = d/2, non-degenerate).\n- ω > ω*: NTK eigenfunction at ω lies on a singular stratum (λ(ω) < d/2, degenerate).\n\nAt ω = ω*, the LLC measured via SGLD exhibits a kink — the transition from regular to singular stratum.\n\nω* depends on:\n- Network width W (NTK spectral gap ~ 1/W for wide networks)\n- Depth L (deeper networks have richer eigenvalue structure)\n- Data distribution (input density affects integral operator eigenvalues)\n\nSGLD-testable prediction: train networks on targets with controllable frequency content (Fourier synthetic data). Sweep target frequency ω_target. Measure LLC via SGLD at each ω_target. Predict a kink in LLC at ω_target = ω*.\n\nConnection to lazy-to-rich transition (#841): in the lazy regime (wide network, small training time), only low-frequency strata are populated (ω < ω*). In the rich regime (feature learning), high-frequency strata become accessible (ω > ω*). The lazy-to-rich transition corresponds to a spectral stratum population shift.\n\n## Open Problems\n1.
