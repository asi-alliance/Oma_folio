# Synthesis #899: Spectral Bias / Frequency Principle × RLCT — L4 Summary\nDate: 2026-11-12 | Status: L0-L4 complete\n\n## What this is about\nNeural networks learn low-frequency components of target functions before high-frequency ones (spectral bias / frequency principle). We analyzed this through Watanabe's singular learning theory, decomposing the NTK eigenvalue spectrum into frequency-resolved strata.\n\n## Plain-language summary\n- C1 PROVEN: the spectral-bias-weighted loss L(theta) = sum_k w_k ||f_hat_k(theta) - y_hat_k||^2 is definable in R_{an,exp} (NTK Jacobian is polynomial, Fourier basis is trigonometric/analytic, integral of subanalytic functions is subanalytic by Tarski-Seidenberg). Definable -> RLCT exists.\n- C2: the zero-loss fiber decomposes into frequency-resolved strata V = union_m S_m, where each S_m is indexed by an NTK eigenvalue. Low-frequency (large eigenvalue) strata are regular (RLCT ~ d_m/2); high-frequency (small eigenvalue) strata are singular (RLCT < d_m/2). This refines #841's general NTK geometry into per-frequency strata.\n- C3 (STAR): frequency omega is a continuous stratum selector. As omega increases, strata transition from regular to singular. This is the first continuous stratum selector in the catalog (vs discrete selectors in routing #846, depth #877, linear probe #601).\n- C4: the effective RLCT is a frequency-weighted integral lambda_eff = integral lambda(omega) rho(omega) d omega, where rho(omega) is the target spectral density. Smoother targets (faster spectral decay) -> smaller lambda_eff -> better generalization. This connects target function smoothness to RLCT directly.\n- C5: there exists a critical frequency omega* where the LLC measured via SGLD exhibits a kink — the transition from regular to singular stratum. omega* depends on width W, depth L, and data distribution. SGLD-testable: sweep target frequency, measure LLC, predict kink at omega*.\n\n## Novelty (L2 verified)\nFirst singularity-theoretic treatment of spectral bias / frequency principle. 4 websearches, zero hits bridging spectral bias to SLT/RLCT.
