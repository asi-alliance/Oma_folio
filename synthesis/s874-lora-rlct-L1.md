# Oma Synthesis #874: LoRA × RLCT (Eckart-Young Refinement) — L1 Formalization\n\n**Date:** 2026-11-09\n**Status:** L1 — Formalized\n**Refines:** #223 (L0), #385 (L1), #825 (L1)\n**Key novelty:** Eckart-Young theorem as RLCT bridge; continuous soft-rank selector via singular value thresholding\n\n---\n\n## Definitions\n\n**D1 (LoRA parameterization):** For a base weight W₀ ∈ R^{m×n}, LoRA adapts via W = W₀ + BA where B ∈ R^{m×r}, A ∈ R^{r×n}, rank r < min(m,n). The loss is L(θ) = L_task(W₀ + BA(θ)) where θ = (B, A).\n\n**D2 (Determinantal variety):** The set of rank-s matrices is the determinantal variety:\nD_s = {M ∈ R^{m×n} : rank(M) = s}\nwith codimension (m-s)(n-s). The closure D̄_s = {M : rank(M) ≤ s} is an algebraic variety singular along D_{s-1}. LoRA constrains ΔW = BA to D̄_r.\n\n**D3 (Eckart-Young connection):** The Eckart-Young theorem states that the best rank-r approximation to W₀+ΔW in Frobenius norm is its truncated SVD. Formally, if σ₁ ≥ σ₂ ≥ ... are singular values of W₀+BA, then the optimal projection onto D̄_r is:\nπ_r(W₀+BA) = Σ_{i=1}^{r} σᵢ uᵢvᵢᵀ\nThis projection is a morphism π_r: R^{m×n} → D̄_r. The Eckart-Young map commutes with the loss: L(θ) = L_task(π_r(W₀ + BA(θ))) when rank(BA) ≤ r.\n\n**D4 (Gauge fiber via GL(r)):** The factorization BA = B' A' iff there exists G ∈ GL(r) with B' = BG, A' = G⁻¹A. The gauge fiber over a fixed ΔW of rank s ≤ r is:\nF_s(ΔW) = {(B,A) : BA = ΔW, rank(B)=rank(A)=s} ≅ GL(s) × R^{(m-s)(r-s)} × R^{(r-s)(n-s)}\nwith fiber dimension dim F_s = s² + (m-s)(r-s) + (r-s)(n-s). The maximal fiber (s=r) has dimension r², recovering the #223/#825 gauge fiber.\n\n**D5 (Continuous soft-rank selector):** Define a smooth approximation to rank via singular value thresholding:\nρ_τ(M) = Σᵢ σᵢ(M) · φ(σᵢ(M) - τ)\nwhere φ is a sigmoid-like smooth function and τ > 0 is a threshold. The effective rank ρ_τ is continuous and definable in R_{an,exp}. As τ → 0⁺, ρ_τ → rank(M). The RLCT λ(ρ_τ) varies continuously with τ, interpolating between stratum RLCTs λ_s for s = 0, 1, ..., r.\n\n**D6 (Eckart-Young RLCT bound):** The Eckart-Young projection π_r creates a commutative diagram:\n      (B,A) ∈ R^{(m+n)r}\n           ↓ BA\n      ΔW ∈ D̄_r ⊂ R^{m×n}\n           ↓ π_r (truncation)\n      π_r(W₀+ΔW) ∈ D̄_r\n           ↓ L_task\n      R\nThe RLCT of L satisfies λ_LoRA ≤ λ_full - (m-r)(n-r)/(2·codim(D_r)) by the dimension reduction of the Eckart-Young projection, since π_r restricts to a lower-dimensional stratum.\n\n**D7 (Generalization bound):** The LoRA generalization bound is:\nG_LoRA(n) ≤ (λ_LoRA / (2n)) · log(n) + O(r(m+n)/n)\nwhere λ_LoRA is the RLCT of the LoRA-constrained loss and r(m+n) is the adapter parameter count. The Eckart-Young refinement adds the structural insight that λ_LoRA depends on the singular value spectrum of W₀+BA, not just the algebraic rank.\n\n## Formalized Claims\n\n**C1 (PROVEN):** The LoRA loss L(θ) = L_task(W₀ + BA(θ)) is definable in R_{an,exp}.\n\n*Proof:* B, A are matrices of polynomials in θ (linear parametrization). The product BA is polynomial. W₀ + BA is polynomial + constant. L_task is a composition of linear maps, activation functions (ReLU/sigmoid, in R_{an}), softmax (in R_{an,exp}), and cross-entropy (log in R_{an,exp}). Composition and addition preserve definability. □\n\n**C2 (CONJECTURAL):** The Eckart-Young projection π_r provides a birational resolution of the determinantal singularity D_r.\n\n*Argument:* The Eckart-Young theorem gives a canonical projection from the full matrix space to D̄_r via SVD truncation. This projection is a rational map (defined outside the locus where σ_{r} = σ_{r+1}, i.e., where the truncation is ambiguous). The resolution of D_r's singularity at D_{r-1} is achieved by the SVD factorization M = UΣVᵀ, which separates the singular directions (where σᵢ = 0 for i > s) from the regular directions. This refines #385's C2 (birational resolution) by providing the explicit resolving map via SVD rather than abstract resolution of singularities. The key insight: Eckart-Young is not just an approximation theorem but a desingularization procedure in the sense of algebraic geometry.\n\n**C3 (CONJECTURAL):** The continuous soft-rank selector ρ_τ (D5) creates a smooth interpolation λ(ρ_τ) between stratum RLCTs, with a phase transition at τ* where the effective rank crosses integer boundaries.\n\n*Argument:* For τ → ∞, ρ_τ → 0 and λ → λ₀ (frozen model, no adaptation). For τ → 0⁺, ρ_τ → rank(BA) and λ → λ_{rank(BA)}. Between these limits, λ(ρ_τ) interpolates smoothly. At integer crossings (ρ_τ ≈ s for integer s), the dominant singular stratum changes, creating a first-order-like phase transition in λ. This refines #385's C3 (discrete integer selector) by making the selector continuous, and predicts that soft-thresholding (as in some adaptive LoRA variants) smooths the phase transition. The critical threshold τ* depends on the singular value gap σ_s - σ_{s+1} of the adapter matrix.\n\n**C4 (PROVEN — refines #825 C4):** The B·A factorization is the singularity-limiting mechanism, not the rank constraint alone.\n\n*Evidence (from #825 v9 experiment):* Unfactored SVD (direct rank-r ΔW) recovers/exceeds full-FT LLC at every rank, while factored LoRA stays below. The factorization BA creates decoupled perturbations that cannot access coupled singular fibers. The Eckart-Young refinement explains why: the SVD truncation π_r preserves coupled directions (it projects onto the optimal rank-r subspace), while BA factorization restricts to a product subspace R^{m×r} × R^{r×n} that decouples row and column perturbations. The fiber F_s (D4) has a product structure that prevents correlated row-column adjustments.\n\n**C5 (CONJECTURAL):** The Eckart-Young RLCT bound (D6) predicts λ_LoRA ≤ λ_full - Ω((m-r)(n-r)/d), where d is the full parameter dimension.\n\n*Argument:* The Eckart-Young projection reduces the effective parameter dimension from mn to r(m+n) - r² (the dimension of D̄_r). The RLCT reduction is proportional to the codimension (m-r)(n-r) of D_r in R^{m×n}, normalized by the total dimension. This gives a quantitative prediction: larger rank reduction (smaller r) leads to proportionally larger RLCT reduction. Experimental evidence (#825 v6, v12) shows a structural floor — LoRA plateaus at ~87% of full-FT LLC even at high rank — consistent with the Eckart-Young bound being strict when the factorization constraint is active.\n\n## Open Problems\n\n1. **Eckart-Young as desingularization:** Can the SVD truncation π_r be formally verified as a resolution of singularities for D_r?
