# Synthesis #873: Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) × RLCT\n\n## Date: 2026-11-07\n## Status: L0 Exploration\n\n## Architecture\n\nMessage Passing GNN (MPGNN): node features X are updated via iterative aggregation:\n\nH^(0) = X\nH^(l+1) = sigma(hat_A H^(l) W)\n\nwhere hat_A = D^{-1/2}(A+I)D^{-1/2} is the normalized adjacency matrix, W is a shared weight matrix (same W at every layer in the simplest case), and sigma is a pointwise nonlinearity (ReLU).\n\nOutput: Y = readout(H^(L)).\n\n## Key Insight\n\nGNNs introduce a fundamentally new singularity type through **weight sharing across message passing layers**. Unlike a deep MLP where each layer has independent weights, a shared-weight MPGNN applies the SAME W at every hop. This creates:\n\n1. **Cyclic symmetry**: Permuting layer assignments of W does not change the function. The parameter-to-function map has a Z_L symmetry group, creating non-identifiability beyond standard neural network redundancy.\n\n2. **Over-smoothing as singular stratum**: As L grows, hat_A^L converges toward rank-1 (all nodes converge to the same representation). This limit defines a singular stratum where the effective parameter dimension collapses. The RLCT of this stratum is controlled by the spectral gap of hat_A.\n\n3. **Graph spectrum as discrete stratum selector**: The eigenvalues {lambda_1 >= ... >= lambda_n} of hat_A determine the rate of over-smoothing. Graphs with small spectral gap (lambda_2 ~ 1) over-smooth slowly; graphs with large gap over-smooth quickly. The spectrum selects which singular stratum the parameters occupy — a discrete analog of continuous stratum selectors in other architectures.\n\n## Claims\n\n### C1 (PROVEN): R_{an,exp} Definability\nThe loss function L(W) = (1/n) sum_i ||Y_i - f(X_i, A_i; W)||^2 is definable over R_{an,exp}. The message passing operation H^(l+1) = sigma(hat_A H^(l) W) is a composition of:\n- Matrix multiplication (polynomial/bilinear) — semialgebraic\n- ReLU (piecewise linear) — semialgebraic\n- Readout (sum/mean pooling) — polynomial\n\nAll operations preserve R_{an,exp} definability. By Watanabe's standard theorem, the RLCT lambda exists.\n\n### C2 (CONJECTURAL): Weight-Sharing Cyclic Singular Fiber\nIn a shared-weight MPGNN with L layers, the map W -> f(X, A; W) has a Z_L cyclic symmetry: the function is invariant under any permutation of which "virtual layer" W is applied to. More precisely, the iterated map phi_W(h) = sigma(hat_A h W) composed L times satisfies phi_W^L = phi_W circ ... circ phi_W (L times), and the non-identifiability from weight sharing creates a singular fiber whose structure depends on the joint spectrum of (W, hat_A).\n\nThe singular fiber W_0 where the Jacobian of (W -> phi_W^L) drops rank is the set where the spectral radius rho(hat_A W) has multiplicity > 1. This is an algebraic condition (characteristic polynomial has repeated roots) defining a proper subvariety.\n\n### C3 (CONJECTURAL): Graph Spectrum as Discrete Stratum Selector\nThe eigenvalues of hat_A (normalized adjacency) serve as a discrete stratum selector. For a graph with spectral gap gamma = 1 - lambda_2:\n- Large gamma (fast over-smoothing): parameters reach the rank-1 singular stratum quickly, lambda_GNN is small (few effective dimensions)\n- Small gamma (slow over-smoothing): parameters explore higher-rank strata, lambda_GNN is larger\n\nThe RLCT of the GNN depends on BOTH the weight matrix W AND the graph spectrum: lambda(W, A) = lambda(W, hat_A) where hat_A enters through the message passing operator.\n\n### C4 (CONJECTURAL): Over-Smoothing as RLCT Phase Transition\nThere exists a critical depth L* such that for L < L*, the dominant singular stratum is determined by the W-redundancy (standard NN singularity), and for L > L*, the dominant stratum is the over-smoothing stratum (rank-1 collapse). At L*, the RLCT jumps: lambda(L*+) < lambda(L*-).\n\nThe critical depth L* ~ C / log(1/lambda_2) where lambda_2 is the second eigenvalue of hat_A. This is testable: train GNNs of varying depth on graphs with known spectral gaps and measure LLC via SGLD.\n\n### C5 (CONJECTURAL): Generalization Bound with Spectral Gap\nThe generalization gap Delta_G for a GNN on graph G is:\n\nDelta_G ~ (lambda(W,A) / (2n)) * log(n)\n\nwhere lambda(W,A) is the RLCT that depends on both weights and graph spectrum. Graphs with small spectral gap (heterophilous, diverse) have larger lambda and worse generalization per sample; graphs with large spectral gap (homophilous, uniform) have smaller lambda and better generalization. This is consistent with empirical observations that GNNs generalize better on homophilous graphs.\n\n## Novel Contributions\n\n1. **Weight-sharing cyclic symmetry** as a new source of singularity distinct from standard NN redundancy (permutation symmetry, scale symmetry)\n2. **Graph spectrum as discrete stratum selector** — first synthesis where the DATA (graph structure) directly controls which singular stratum the model occupies\n3. **Over-smoothing = singular stratum collapse** — connects the well-known over-smoothing phenomenon to RLCT phase transitions\n4. **Spectral gap generalization bound** — connects spectral graph theory to SLT generalization bounds\n\n## Open Problems\n\n1.
