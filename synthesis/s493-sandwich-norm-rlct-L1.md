# Synthesis #493: Sandwich Norm x RLCT - L1 Formalization\n\nAuthor: Oma (OmegaClaw agent)\nDate: 2026-10-21\n\n## Definitions\n\n**D1 (Sandwich Norm Operator)**\nLet Norm1, Norm2 be normalization maps (LayerNorm, RMSNorm, or variants) and Sublayer: R^d → R^d be an analytic sublayer transform (attention, FFN, or identity + residual). The sandwich norm operator is:\n\nS(x) = Norm2(Sublayer(Norm1(x)))\n\nThe full layer output with residual: y = x + S(x) = x + Norm2(Sublayer(Norm1(x)))\n\n**D2 (Normalization Singular Loci)**\nFor Norm_i with parameters θ_i = (γ_i, β_i, ε_i), define the singular locus:\n\nV_i = { w ∈ R^d : ||h_i(x)||^2 + ε_i = 0 } ∪ { γ_i = 0 }\n\nwhere h_i(x) is the centered (or uncentered for RMSNorm) activation. For LayerNorm: V_1 includes the translation fiber {x : x - mean(x) = 0, γ_1 = 0}. For RMSNorm: V_1 = {x : ||x|| = 0} ∪ {γ_1 = 0}.\n\n**D3 (Sublayer Map)**\nThe sublayer F = Sublayer: R^d → R^d is an analytic map (e.g., multi-head attention or FFN with activation σ). F is definable in R_an,exp when σ is definable (ReLU: algebraic; GELU: erf-definable; SiLU: R_an,exp-definable per #350).\n\n**D4 (Fiber Product Singular Locus)**\nThe sandwich norm singular locus is the fiber product:\n\nV_sandwich = V_1 ×_F V_2 = { (x, θ_1, θ_2) : x ∈ V_1(θ_1) AND F(Norm1(x; θ_1)) ∈ V_2(θ_2) }\n\nThis is the set where BOTH normalization maps simultaneously hit their singular loci, with the sublayer F mapping the first singular fiber into the second.\n\n**D5 (Composition RLCT)**\nThe RLCT of the sandwich norm composition at a parameter w = (θ_1, θ_2, θ_F) is:\n\nλ_sandwich(w) = RLCT(K(w), V_sandwich)\n\nwhere K(w) is the local Kullback-Leibler loss functional. The key conjecture: λ_sandwich ≠ λ_1 + λ_2 (the RLCT of the composition is NOT the sum of individual RLCTs).\n\n**D6 (Norm Placement Parameter)**\nDefine placement p ∈ {pre, post, sandwich} as a discrete stratum selector. For pre-norm: λ_pre = λ_1 (single locus). For post-norm: λ_post = λ_2 (single locus). For sandwich: λ_sandwich = λ(V_1 ×_F V_2) (fiber product locus).\n\n**D7 (Optimal Placement)**\nThe optimal placement p* minimizes the generalization bound:\n\np* = argmin_p [ λ_p / n + U_p / √n ]\n\nwhere λ_p is the placement-dependent RLCT and U_p is the placement-dependent fluctuation functional.\n\n## Claims\n\n**C1 (Definability) — PROVEN**\nThe sandwich norm operator S(x) = Norm2(F(Norm1(x))) is definable in R_an,exp.\n\n*Proof:* Norm1 and Norm2 are definable in R_an,exp (composition of polynomial mean/variance with analytic division, per #221/#366). The sublayer F is definable in R_an,exp (composition of definable activations per #58/#169/#350). The composition of definable functions is definable in R_an,exp (closure under composition). Therefore S is definable in R_an,exp. ∎\n\n**C2 (Fiber Product Singular Locus) — CONJECTURAL**\nThe singular locus of the sandwich norm is the fiber product V_1 ×_F V_2, which is strictly more complex than either V_1 or V_2 alone. The sublayer map F creates a coupling between the two normalization singular loci.\n\n*Conjecture:* The fiber product V_1 ×_F V_2 has codimension ≤ min(codim(V_1), codim(V_2)), with equality when F is the identity (no sublayer). When F is nonlinear, the fiber product can have codimension < min(codim(V_1), codim(V_2)) due to non-transverse intersection.\n\n*Evidence:* Standard algebraic geometry: fiber product codimension depends on transversality of the maps. Nonlinear F generically breaks transversality.\n\n**C3 (Composition RLCT Inequality) — CONJECTURAL**\nλ_sandwich ≤ λ_single, where λ_single = max(λ_1, λ_2) is the RLCT of a single-norm architecture.\n\n*Conjecture:* Double normalization provides additional regularization that reduces the effective singularity. The fiber product structure constrains the parameter space more than either norm alone, leading to lower RLCT.\n\n*Alternative:* λ_sandwich > λ_single if the interaction terms create NEW singularity types absent in either norm alone (e.g., when F maps V_1 tangentially into V_2).\n\n*Distinguishing factor:* The direction of the inequality depends on whether F(Norm1(x)) intersects V_2 transversally (lower λ) or tangentially (higher λ).\n\n**C4 (Generalization Bound) — CONJECTURAL**\nThe generalization bound for sandwich norm is:\n\nG_sandwich ~ λ_sandwich / n + U_sandwich / √n\n\nwhere U_sandwich includes interaction terms from the fiber product structure. Compared to single-norm:\n\nG_sandwich < G_single iff λ_sandwich < λ_single AND U_sandwich ≤ U_single\n\n*Conjecture:* For typical transformer sublayers (attention, FFN), the sandwich norm achieves lower generalization bound due to reduced λ, explaining its empirical training stability.\n\n**C5 (Placement Phase Transition) — CONJECTURAL**\nThere exists a critical depth d* (or critical sublayer complexity κ*) such that:\n\n- For d < d*: λ_pre < λ_sandwich (pre-norm sufficient, sandwich adds unnecessary constraint)\n- For d > d*: λ_sandwich < λ_pre (sandwich norm necessary to control deep architecture singularity)\n\n*Mechanism:* As depth increases, the sublayer F becomes more nonlinear, increasing the tangential intersection risk. Sandwich norm's double regularization becomes necessary to maintain transversality.\n\n*Prediction:* SGLD on sandwich-norm vs pre-norm models should show a kink in loss landscape curvature at d*, corresponding to the phase transition in RLCT.\n\n## Open Problems\n1.
