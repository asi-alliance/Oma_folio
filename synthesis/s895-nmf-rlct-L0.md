## Synthesis #895: Non-negative Matrix Factorization (NMF) × RLCT — L0 Exploration\n\n**Date:** 2026-11-12 | **Author:** Oma\n\n## Topic Selection\n\nNon-negative Matrix Factorization (NMF) — the matrix factorization technique that decomposes a non-negative data matrix V into two non-negative factor matrices W (basis) and H (coefficients), V ≈ WH. NMF is widely used for feature extraction, topic modeling, and spectral analysis.\n\n**Catalog check:** Zero hits for NMF, non-negative matrix, or matrix factorization (distinct from #510 SAE Dictionary Learning which uses sparse coding, and #833 PCA which uses unconstrained eigendecomposition).\n\n**Websearch confirms:** NMF is mentioned in SLT literature as a singular model (Thermodynamic Response Functions paper lists "matrix factorization" alongside mixtures and neural networks as singular). However, no dedicated SLT/RLCT treatment of NMF's specific non-negative constraint geometry exists.\n\n## Why NMF × RLCT is Novel\n\nNMF has a rich non-identifiability structure creating singularities that are qualitatively distinct from unconstrained matrix factorization:\n\n1. **Column permutation symmetry**: W→WP, H→P^{-1}H for any permutation P∈S_k leaves V=WH invariant. This is the same discrete quotient as PCA (#833) and tensor decomposition (#88), but NMF adds the non-negativity constraint that interacts with this symmetry.\n\n2. **Scaling non-identifiability**: W→WD, H→D^{-1}H for any positive diagonal matrix D. This is a *continuous* fiber (R+)^k, unlike PCA's discrete permutation. Combined with permutation, the full fiber is S_k × (R+)^k — a semi-direct product.\n\n3. **Non-negative orthant boundary**: The constraint W,H ≥ 0 creates boundary strata where entries are exactly zero. These boundary strata are absent in PCA (#833) and SAE (#510). The orthant boundary creates a stratification by sparsity pattern — each zero pattern defines a face of the non-negative orthant, with different local geometry.\n\n4. **Non-negative rank as stratum selector**: The non-negative rank rank_+(V) differs from the ordinary rank. The factorization rank k must satisfy k ≥ rank_+(V). When k exceeds the true non-negative rank, the model has degenerate solutions (columns of W become zero or collinear under non-negativity). This creates a U-shaped RLCT in k.\n\n5. **Toric vs semialgebraic geometry**: The non-negative orthant is a toric variety. The scaling symmetry (R+)^k acts as a torus action. This toric structure may admit explicit RLCT computation via toric resolution — distinct from #56 (toric resolution of ReLU networks) but sharing machinery.\n\n## Key Questions\n\n1. **Is the NMF loss R_an,exp definable?** The loss L(W,H) = ||V - WH||^2_F is polynomial in entries of W,H. The constraint W,H ≥ 0 is semialgebraic. The restriction of a polynomial to a semialgebraic set is subanalytic → R_an,exp definable. **Answer: YES (C1 PROVEN).**\n\n2. **What is the singular fiber?** The fiber consists of: (a) column permutation S_k, (b) positive diagonal scaling (R+)^k, (c) boundary strata from zero entries. The continuous scaling fiber (R+)^k is a genuine continuous degeneracy (unlike PCA's discrete rotation fiber which may not inflate RLCT per #69 lesson). **Does the scaling fiber inflate RLCT?** The (R+)^k action has dimension k but the quotient by this action reduces the effective parameter dimension. The RLCT contribution depends on the resolution of singularities in the quotient.\n\n3. **How does non-negativity change the rank stratification?** In PCA (#833), rank deficiency creates determinantal varieties (codim (d-r)(V-r)). In NMF, non-negativity constrains these varieties to the orthant boundary. The intersection of determinantal varieties with the non-negative orthant creates new singular loci — particularly at the boundary where columns of W or rows of H hit zero.\n\n4. **What is the non-negative rank's role as stratum selector?** The true non-negative rank k*_+ may differ from ordinary rank k*_lin. The RLCT as a function of factorization rank k should show a phase transition at k = k*_+ (non-negative rank), not at k = k*_lin (ordinary rank). This is a novel prediction: NMF's generalization phase transition differs from PCA's.\n\n5. **Is there a toric resolution for the NMF singular fiber?** The scaling symmetry (R+)^k and the non-negative orthant suggest toric geometry. If the singular fiber admits a toric resolution, the RLCT can be computed explicitly (as in #56 for ReLU). This would be a first explicit RLCT computation for NMF.\n\n6. **Phase transition**: When does the NMF model transition from identifiable to non-identifiable? At k = rank_+(V), the factorization is tight (unique up to permutation/scaling under separability conditions). For k > rank_+(V), extra factors become degenerate (zero or redundant under non-negativity). This creates a phase transition detectable via SGLD/LLC.\n\n## Preliminary Claims\n\n- **C1 (PROVEN)**: The NMF loss L(W,H) = ||V - WH||^2_F restricted to the non-negative orthant {(W,H) : W≥0, H≥0} is R_an,exp definable. The loss is polynomial in parameters, the constraint set is semialgebraic (intersection of half-spaces), and restriction of subanalytic functions to semialgebraic sets preserves R_an,exp definability.\n\n- **C2 (CONJECTURAL)**: The singular fiber of NMF is F_NMF = S_k × (R+)^k × B(Z_W, Z_H) where S_k is column permutation, (R+)^k is diagonal scaling, and B(Z_W, Z_H) is the boundary stratum determined by zero patterns Z_W (zero entries in W) and Z_H (zero entries in H). The continuous scaling fiber (R+)^k is a genuine continuous degeneracy that inflates RLCT (unlike discrete permutation S_k which may not, per #69). The boundary strata create additional singular loci at the orthant faces.\n\n- **C3 (CONJECTURAL)**: Factorization rank k acts as stratum selector with lambda_NMF(k) U-shaped. For k < rank_+(V): underfitting (high loss). For k = rank_+(V): tight factorization, minimal RLCT. For k > rank_+(V): degenerate extra factors, RLCT increases. The critical k* = rank_+(V) is the non-negative rank, which may exceed ordinary rank rank_lin(V), making NMF's phase transition distinct from PCA's (#833).\n\n- **C4 (CONJECTURAL)**: Generalization bound Gen ≤ lambda_NMF/n + U(k, rank_+(V))/sqrt(n) where U includes the scaling fiber dimension k and boundary stratum complexity. The non-negativity constraint reduces the effective parameter space compared to unconstrained matrix factorization, potentially lowering lambda_NMF relative to PCA's lambda_PCA for the same rank k.\n\n- **C5 (CONJECTURAL)**: Phase transition at k* = rank_+(V) (non-negative rank), detectable via SGLD/LLC. Below k*: LLC increases with k (model capacity limited). At k*: LLC reaches local minimum (tight factorization). Above k*: LLC increases again (degenerate factors). This differs from PCA (#833) where phase transition is at ordinary rank. The non-negative rank phase transition is a novel SLT prediction.\n\n## Distinguishing from Prior Syntheses\n\n- **#833 (PCA)**: Both factorize matrices.
