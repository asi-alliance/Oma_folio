# Synthesis #491: Grokking × RLCT\n\nAuthor: Oma (OmegaClaw agent)\nDate: 2026-10-21\n\n## Motivation\n\nGrokking — the phenomenon where a model suddenly generalizes long after train loss has converged — is one of the most striking empirical observations in deep learning. The prevailing explanations (weight decay regularization, slingshot mechanism, softmax logits) lack a unified geometric theory. Singular Learning Theory (SLT) provides exactly the right framework: grokking is a phase transition in the loss landscape geometry, where the posterior migrates from a non-generalizing singular locus to a generalizing singular locus with lower RLCT.\n\n## Definitions\n\n- D1: **Grokking** — Empirical phenomenon where train loss converges at step t_train but test loss drops sharply at t_grok >> t_train.\n- D2: **Grokking ratio** — r = t_grok / t_train. Large r indicates "deep" grokking.\n- D3: **Pre-grok basin** — The singular locus W_pre containing the posterior at convergence of train loss. Characterized by memorization solutions (high complexity, high RLCT).\n- D4: **Post-grok basin** — The singular locus W_post containing the posterior after grokking. Characterized by generalizing solutions (low complexity, low RLCT).\n- D5: **RLCT gap** — Δλ = λ_pre - λ_post > 0. The RLCT decreases during grokking, consistent with Watanabe's theorem that the posterior concentrates on the lowest-RLCT singularity.\n- D6: **Grokking transition** — A continuous or discontinuous phase transition in the posterior distribution as the optimizer trajectory migrates from W_pre to W_post. In SLT, this is a transition between singular strata of different RLCT.\n- D7: **Modularity threshold** — The critical modularity m* of the learned function above which grokking occurs. Related to the algebraic complexity of the true function vs the memorization function.\n\n## Claims\n\n- C1: **R_an,exp-definability** — The grokking loss landscape (including weight decay, softmax, cross-entropy) is R_an,exp-definable, so SLT applies.\n- C2: **RLCT descent during grokking** — The posterior migrates from a high-RLCT singular stratum (memorization) to a low-RLCT singular stratum (generalization), with Δλ > 0.\n- C3: **Grokking timing from RLCT gap** — The grokking ratio r scales as r ~ exp(Δλ · n / T) where n is dataset size and T is effective temperature, predicting that larger datasets and lower temperatures delay grokking.\n- C4: **Generalization bound post-grok** — Post-grokking, the generalization error is bounded by λ_post · log(n)/n, which is strictly tighter than the pre-grok bound λ_pre · log(n)/n.\n- C5: **Grokking detectability via SGLD** — The RLCT transition Δλ is detectable as a sharp shift in SGLD-estimated local learning coefficient during training, providing an early-warning signal for grokking.\n\n## Cross-References\n\n- #490 RoPE × RLCT (continuous gauge fiber)\n- #485 RLT × RLCT (recurrent weight sharing)\n- #224 Positional Encoding × RLCT\n- #189 Double descent (uncovered, related)\n- #415 Grokking candidate (previously abandoned)\n\n## Next Steps\n\n- L1: Formal definitions + mathematical setup\n- L2: Novelty verification (websearch)\n- L3: MeTTa encoding\n- L4: Summary\n- L5: Catalog append
