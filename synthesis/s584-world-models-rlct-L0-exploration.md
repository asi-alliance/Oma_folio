#584 World Models × RLCT — L0 Exploration\nDate: 2026-10-24\n\n## Topic\nWorld Models (Dreamer, Dyna, HaSe, world-model-based RL and prediction) × Real Log-Canonical Threshold (RLCT)\n\n## Core Question\nHow does the singularity structure of a world model differ from standard autoencoders or sequence models, given the coupled encoder-transition-decoder architecture where latent dynamics must simultaneously support reconstruction and forward prediction?\n\n## Five Claims\n\n### C1 (PROVEN): R_{an,exp}-definability of World Model Loss\nThe world model loss combines: (a) reconstruction loss L_recon(w_e, w_d) between observations x and reconstructions dec(enc(x)), (b) dynamics loss L_dyn(w_t) between predicted latent states z_{t+1} = trans(z_t, a_t) and encoded next states enc(x_{t+1}), and (c) optional reward prediction L_rew(w_r). Each component is a composition of analytic functions (encoder MLP, transition network, decoder MLP) with ReLU/GELU activations — all definable in R_{an,exp}. The composite loss is piecewise-analytic, hence definable in R_{an,exp}. C1 is PROVEN.\n\n### C2 (CONJECTURAL): Coupled Multi-Component Singular Fiber\nUnlike a standard autoencoder (#167, single encoder-decoder), the world model has THREE coupled components: encoder w_e, transition w_t, decoder w_d. The singular fiber at a true parameter w* is the product of singular loci from each component, BUT the transition component introduces a TEMPORAL coupling: z_{t+1} = trans(z_t, a_t) must be consistent with enc(x_{t+1}). This creates a constrained product-space singularity where the transition Jacobian J_t = ∂trans/∂z_t must satisfy a consistency condition with the encoder Jacobian J_e = ∂enc/∂x. The effective RLCT λ_WM is NOT simply the sum of individual component RLCTs — it is governed by the coupled constraint surface. Conjecture: λ_WM ≤ λ_AE + λ_seq, with equality iff the transition and encoder Jacobians are transversal (generic condition).\n\n### C3 (CONJECTURAL): Transition Model as Stratum Selector\nThe choice of transition model class (deterministic MLP vs stochastic latent dynamics vs recurrent SSM) selects different singular strata. Deterministic transition: lower RLCT (fewer effective parameters, no stochastic noise model). Stochastic transition (as in Dreamer): higher RLCT from the additional Gaussian transition distribution, but potentially better generalization from the smoothing effect. The transition model class is a discrete stratum selector analogous to attention type (#86, #302) or sampling method (#359).\n\n### C4 (CONJECTURAL): Generalization Bound with Prediction Horizon\nThe world model must predict multiple steps ahead. At prediction horizon H, the effective loss compounds transition errors: L_H = sum_{h=1}^{H} L(recon(z_h), x_h). The RLCT governing generalization should scale with H because the composite loss at horizon H creates a product-space singularity across H time steps. Conjecture: the free energy F(H) ~ H * λ_WM * log(n) / 2, and the optimal horizon H* trades off prediction accuracy (decreasing with H) against RLCT complexity (increasing with H), creating a U-shaped generalization curve in H.\n\n### C5 (CONJECTURAL): Planning as RLCT Phase Transition\nIn model-based RL (Dyna, Dreamer), the world model is used for planning via rollout in latent space. Short rollouts: the model stays near the training distribution, RLCT is low, planning is reliable. Long rollouts: the model extrapolates beyond training data, entering high-RLCT regions where the loss landscape becomes more singular and predictions are unreliable. Conjecture: there exists a critical rollout depth H* where the model transitions from reliable (low-RLCT stratum) to unreliable (high-RLCT stratum) — a phase transition in planning reliability governed by the RLCT of the latent dynamics.\n\n## Key Novelty\nThe multi-component coupled singular fiber is the central novelty. World models are not just autoencoders with a prediction head — the transition model creates a temporal coupling constraint between encoder and decoder that fundamentally alters the singularity structure. This is distinct from:\n- #167 Autoencoder × RLCT (no transition dynamics)\n- #502 RAG × RLCT (external retrieval, not learned dynamics)\n- #311 Flow Matching × RLCT (continuous normalizing flows, not discrete-time transition)\n- #251 Neural ODE × RLCT (continuous-time dynamics, but no encoder-decoder coupling)\n\n## Open Problems\n1.
