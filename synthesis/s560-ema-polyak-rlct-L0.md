#560 EMA / Polyak Averaging × RLCT — L0 Hypothesis (2026-10-23)\n\n## Topic\nExponential Moving Average (EMA) / Polyak averaging of model weights during training. At each step, maintain a shadow weight vector \bar{w}_t = (1-beta)\bar{w}_{t-1} + beta * w_t (EMA) or \bar{w}_t = (1/t)\sum_{s=1}^t w_s (Polyak). The averaged weight \bar{w} is used for inference, not the final weight w_T.\n\n## Key Insight\nWeight averaging traces a path through parameter space and computes a centroid. If the training trajectory oscillates within or near a singular stratum (flat basin), the averaged point \bar{w} may lie deeper inside the singular set than any individual w_t — the averaging operation concentrates probability mass toward the center of the basin, which in singular models corresponds to the deepest singular point (highest symmetry, lowest RLCT). EMA thus acts as a singularity-seeking operation: it biases the effective parameter toward the most singular point in the visited region.\n\n## Claims\n\n**C1 (PROVEN):** The averaged weight \bar{w} = \sum_s alpha_s w_s (convex combination) is an R_an-definable function of the trajectory {w_s}. The averaging map is linear (polynomial) in the weights, and composition with the analytic KL divergence preserves R_an-definability. The EMA model's loss K(\bar{w}) is definable in R_an,exp.\n\n**C2 (CONJECTURAL):** If the SGD trajectory {w_t} ergodically samples a basin B containing a singular stratum S_0, then EMA averaging converges to a point \bar{w}* that lies in the convex hull of the trajectory. For singular models, the deepest singular point w* (minimizing RLCT) is typically at the center of symmetry of the basin. Averaging moves toward this center of symmetry because: (a) the trajectory is roughly symmetric around w* due to gradient dynamics, and (b) the convex combination of symmetric points returns the center. Thus \bar{w}* is closer to w* than typical w_t, yielding lower effective RLCT: lambda(\bar{w}) <= lambda(w_t) in expectation.\n\n**C3 (CONJECTURAL):** The RLCT of the EMA model relates to the averaging window. For Polyak averaging (uniform weights, window = all history), the averaged point is the trajectory centroid, maximizing proximity to the basin center. For EMA (exponential weights, effective window ~ 1/(1-beta)), the averaged point tracks a shorter recent trajectory. There exists an optimal window size w* that balances: (a) large window → closer to basin center → lower RLCT, but (b) large window → includes stale weights from before convergence → higher variance. The optimal w* ~ O(1/eta) where eta is the learning rate, matching the empirical heuristic beta = 1 - eta.\n\n**C4 (CONJECTURAL):** The generalization bound for EMA: G_ema <= lambda(\bar{w}) * log(n) / (2n) + b(\bar{w}) / sqrt(n), where lambda(\bar{w}) <= lambda(w_T) (EMA achieves lower or equal RLCT). The improvement is most pronounced for models with large non-identifiability fibers (many permutations symmetries), where averaging over trajectory samples the fiber uniformly and the centroid projects to the most singular point. For models with trivial fibers (identifiable), EMA provides minimal RLCT benefit — consistent with EMA being most useful for overparameterized models with permutation symmetries.\n\n**C5 (CONJECTURAL):** Phase transition in averaging benefit: there exists a critical noise scale sigma_c (related to SGD noise temperature) below which the trajectory stays in a single basin and EMA helps (concentrates toward center). Above sigma_c, the trajectory jumps between basins and EMA averages across different singular strata — the convex combination of points from different strata may lie in a non-singular region, INCREASING RLCT. This predicts EMA helps with small learning rates / large batch sizes (low noise) and hurts with large learning rates / small batch sizes (high noise) — consistent with empirical observations.\n\n## Distinction from Prior Syntheses\n- #99 SGD dynamics: Describes trajectory dynamics, not weight averaging as singularity-seeking\n- #213 LR scheduling: Changes step size over time, not averaging\n- #222 BatchNorm: Normalization reparameterization, not weight averaging\n- #505 Weight Norm: Scale-direction decomposition, not temporal averaging\n- #169 Dropout: Stochastic masking, not deterministic averaging\n- #433 Weight Decay: L2 regularization shifts singular locus, not averaging\n- #559 SAM: Sharpness-aware minimization seeks flat regions via perturbation, EMA seeks them via temporal averaging — complementary mechanisms\n\n## Open Problems\n1.
