# Synthesis #NN: Neural Tangent Kernel × RLCT — L4 Summary\nDate: 2026-11-05 | Status: L0-L4 complete\n\n## What this is about\nNeural Tangent Kernel (NTK) theory describes what happens when networks are very wide: during training they stay in a "lazy" kernel regime where parameters barely move. As width decreases or scaling changes, networks enter a "rich" regime where parameters actually learn features. We analyzed this transition through Watanabe's Singular Learning Theory.\n\n## Plain-language summary\n- C1 PROVEN: The NTK family with standard activations (analytic or ReLU) gives a definable loss in R_{an,exp}. ReLU kink structure is piecewise-polynomial, analytic activations are straightforwardly definable. RLCT exists, standard theorems apply.\n- C2: The lazy regime (a→∞) corresponds to an unrefined toric fan where the zero-loss fiber V_lazy is regular — RLCT = d/2, the maximal value. The rich regime (a→1) triggers toric fan refinement, creating genuine singularities with λ_rich < d/2. The transition at a* is an RLCT phase transition.\n- C3 (STAR): The NTK eigenvalue spectrum is an empirical RLCT probe. Small eigenvalues of K_NTK align with flat Fisher directions = singular fiber directions. A large spectral gap δ = λ_max/λ_min means anisotropy → partial singularity resolution → reduced λ. At δ=1 (isotropic), no resolution, λ=d/2. This is SGLD-testable.\n- C4: Generalization bound gap ΔG = G_lazy - G_rich = (d/2 - λ_rich)/n + (U_lazy - U_rich)/√n > 0. The RLCT descent d/2 → λ_rich quantifies the feature-learning advantage. Bridges NTK generalization theory (d/(2n)) with empirical feature-learning results.\n- C5: Critical width d* is a fold catastrophe in the RLCT surface λ(d,a) — two stable values coexist (lazy d/2 and rich λ<d/2). μP scaling corresponds to a specific trajectory through this phase diagram. Predicts optimal width where feature learning transitions from easy to hard.\n\n## Novelty (L2 verified)\nFirst singularity-theoretic treatment of the NTK lazy-to-rich transition.
