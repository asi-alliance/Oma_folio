# Synthesis #840: Weight Quantization (QAT) × RLCT — L0 EXPLORATION\n## Date: 2026-11-03\n\n## Topic: Weight Quantization (Quantization-Aware Training) × RLCT\n\n## Distinction from Prior Work\n- #354 (STE): forward/backward gradient split — quantization as non-differentiable surrogate. #840 focuses on the LATTICE CONSTRAINT itself as a singular structure, not the STE mechanism.\n- #556 (VQ-VAE): codebook quantization in LATENT space (Voronoi cells). #840 quantizes PARAMETERS directly.\n- #576 (PTQ/GPTQ/AWQ): post-training projection onto lattice. No training dynamics on lattice. #840 = QAT, training WITH quantization, fundamentally different RLCT object.\n- #194/#319: mixed-precision / inference quantization. #840 = uniform weight quantization during training.\n\n## Core Setup\n\nQuantization map: Q_s(w) = s * round(w/s), restricting W to lattice (sZ)^d where s = 2^(-b) for b-bit quantization.\n\nQAT loss: L_s(w) = L(Q_s(w)) — the loss evaluated at quantized weights. Training optimizes w but loss is computed at Q_s(w).\n\n## Claims\n\n### C1 (PROVEN): R_{an,exp} Definability\nQ_s is piecewise-constant: on each cell C_k = {w : Q_s(w) = k*s}, L_s(w) = L(k*s) is constant. Each cell is a semialgebraic set (intersection of half-spaces defined by rounding boundaries). The loss L_s = sum_k L(k*s) * 1_{C_k} is piecewise-constant subanalytic, hence definable in R_{an,exp}. The partition into cells is finite (for bounded W), so definability follows by finite union closure.\n\n### C2 (CONJECTURAL): Lattice-Constrained Singular Fiber\nThe zero-loss set on the lattice: V_s = {w in (sZ)^d : L(Q_s(w)) = 0} = V ∩ (sZ)^d where V = {w : L(w)=0} is the continuous zero-loss variety. V_s is a DISCRETE approximation of V. The RLCT of L_s is determined by how V_s samples the singular strata of V. As s→0 (b→∞), V_s → V and lambda_s → lambda (continuous RLCT). For finite s, V_s may miss lower-dimensional strata entirely (if no lattice point falls on them), causing lambda_s to JUMP to the RLCT of the nearest sampled stratum. This is a lattice-sampling effect on singularity structure.\n\nKey insight: finite lattice spacing s creates a CUTOFF on the RLCT spectrum — strata of codimension > d_eff(s) are invisible, where d_eff(s) ~ 1/s^d is the lattice resolution. The effective RLCT is:\nlambda_s ~ min{lambda_i : dim(Stratum_i) >= d_eff(s)}\n\n### C3 (CONJECTURAL): Bit-Width b as Stratum Selector\nb = log2(1/s) controls which singular strata are visible to the lattice. Low b (coarse lattice): only high-dimensional strata sampled, lambda_s ~ d/2 (generic, high). High b (fine lattice): all strata sampled, lambda_s → lambda (true RLCT, lower). The transition is NOT smooth — it occurs in discrete jumps as new strata become lattice-visible. This gives a STAIRCASE pattern of lambda_s vs b.\n\n### C4 (CONJECTURAL): Generalization Bound\nGen bound for quantized model: G_s(n) ~ lambda_s * log(n) / (2n) + U_s / sqrt(n) + B_q(s)\nwhere B_q(s) is the quantization bias term = E_x[||f(Q_s(w*)) - f(w*)||] measuring the function gap between quantized and continuous optimal weights. B_q(s) ~ C * s * ||grad f||_infty (Lipschitz bound) for smooth f. The tradeoff: lower b (larger s) increases B_q but may increase lambda_s (coarser stratum sampling). Optimal b* minimizes G_s.\n\n### C5 (CONJECTURAL): Phase Transition at b*\nThe optimal bit-width b* satisfies the balance condition: d(lambda_s)/db * log(n)/(2n) ~ -d(B_q)/db / sqrt(n). This gives b* ~ log2(n^{alpha}) for some alpha depending on the RLCT staircase structure. The phase transition is TESTABLE via SGLD: measure LLC for models trained at different bit-widths and observe the staircase pattern in lambda vs b.\n\n## Open Problems\n1.
