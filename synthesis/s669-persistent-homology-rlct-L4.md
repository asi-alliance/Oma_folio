#669: Persistent Homology x RLCT\n\n## L4: Critique & Risk Assessment\n\n### Claim Strength Ranking\n1. **C1 (STRONG)**: Persistence diagrams are well-defined for R_an-definable losses. Morse theory guarantees finiteness for generic perturbations. Solid mathematical foundation. Risk: standard NN losses are not Morse-generic, but tame functions still have well-defined persistence.\n2. **C4 (MODERATE)**: Persistence stability theorem is rigorous (Cohen-Steiner et al. 2007). The bottleneck distance bound d_B <= ||L_n - L||_inf is proven. Risk: the correction function f(d_B) in the generalization bound is unspecified — stability gives an upper bound but not the functional form. The term f could be trivial (zero) for many cases.\n3. **C3 (MODERATE)**: Filtration parameter as stratum selector is intuitive. As t decreases, sublevel sets intersect lower-dimensional strata. Risk: formal codimension-to-homology-dimension correspondence (stratum codim k -> features in dim p <= k) is not proven and may not hold in general.\n4. **C5 (WEAK-MODERATE)**: Phase transition at critical filtration value t* is plausible but the scaling t* ~ L* + C/n^alpha is speculative. Risk: no derivation from RLCT asymptotics; alpha not specified; t* may not be sharp.\n5. **C2 (WEAKEST)**: Singular strata create persistent features. Risk: the correspondence between specific strata and specific birth-death pairs is unproven. Multiple strata may contribute to the same feature, or features may arise from non-singular topology. Requires explicit construction.\n\n### Key Vulnerabilities\n- **Computability**: Persistent homology in high-dimensional parameter spaces (d > 100) is intractable. All existing TDA tools work in d <= 3. This limits practical applicability to toy models.\n- **Morse genericity gap**: Real NN losses are not Morse functions (degenerate critical points are the norm, not the exception). Morse-generic perturbation may destroy the singular structure that RLCT cares about.\n- **f(d_B) specification**: Without a concrete form for the generalization correction term, C4 remains a framework rather than a theorem.\n- **Stratum-persistence correspondence**: C2 requires proving that specific singular strata produce specific persistent features. This is the core novel claim and is currently only intuitive.\n\n### Experimental Validation Path\n1. Use existing tda_loss_h1.py to compute persistence diagrams on 2D slices of loss landscapes with known singular strata (e.g., tanh MLP from prior work, lambda=0.0309)\n2. Test C2: Do birth-death pairs correlate with known stratum locations?\n3. Test C3: Does decreasing filtration parameter select progressively lower-dimensional features?\n4. Test C5: Is there a sharp phase transition in Betti numbers at a critical t*?\n5. Compare ECC curve (#666) vs persistence diagrams (#669) on same landscape — does persistence carry strictly more information?\n\n### Distinction from #666\n- #666 ECC: single integer function beta_0 - beta_1 + beta_2 - ... = compresses all topological info into one number\n- #669 Persistence: multi-set of (birth, death) pairs per dimension = preserves individual feature lifetimes\n- ECC is a lossy compression of persistence; persistence is strictly more informative\n- But ECC is cheaper to compute and may suffice for RLCT estimation if only global topology matters\n\n### Overall Assessment\n- Novelty: HIGH (no prior bridge between persistent homology and RLCT)\n- Mathematical rigor: MODERATE (C1 proven, C4 partially grounded, C2-C3-C5 speculative)\n- Practical impact: LOW-MODERATE (computability bottleneck in high dimensions)\n- Priority for follow-up: MODERATE (experimental validation feasible with existing tools)\n\n### Cross-References\n- #666 ECC x RLCT (global vs local topological invariants)\n- #8 Foundational RLCT\n- #16 Watanabe generalization bound\n- tda_loss_h1.py, tda_singularity_tool.py (empirical validation infrastructure)\n\n### Next: L5 catalog append.
