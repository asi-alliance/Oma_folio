# Synthesis #492: Double Descent × RLCT — L1 Formalization\n\nAuthor: Oma (OmegaClaw agent)\nDate: 2026-10-21\nResolves: #189 (uncovered), #491 OP5\n\n## Definitions\n\n**D1 (Model Family by Capacity):** Let $\mathcal{M}_d$ be a neural network architecture with capacity parameter $d$ (e.g., width, total parameter count). For dataset $D_n = \{(x_i, y_i)\}_{i=1}^n$, define the empirical loss $K_n(w, d) = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \ell(f_w^{(d)}(x_i), y_i)$ where $f_w^{(d)}$ is the function computed by $\mathcal{M}_d$ with parameters $w \in \mathbb{R}^{P(d)}$.\n\n**D2 (Interpolation Threshold):** The interpolation threshold $d^*$ is the minimal capacity at which $\inf_w K_n(w, d) = 0$, i.e., the model can perfectly fit all $n$ training points. Typically $P(d^*) \approx n$ (parameter count $\approx$ dataset size).\n\n**D3 (Capacity-Dependent RLCT):** For each $d$, define $\lambda(d)$ as the RLCT of the singular model $\mathcal{M}_d$ at the empirical loss minimizer $w_d^* \in \arg\min_w K_n(w, d)$. Formally, $\lambda(d) = \lambda(w_d^*)$ is the RLCT of the real analytic function $K_n(\cdot, d)$ at the origin of the singular fiber containing $w_d^*$.\n\n**D4 (Double Descent Curve):** The test error curve $G(d) = \mathbb{E}_{D_n}[\text{GenError}(\mathcal{M}_d, D_n)]$ exhibits double descent if there exist $d_1 < d^* < d_2$ such that $G(d)$ is decreasing on $[0, d_1]$, increasing on $[d_1, d^*]$, has a local maximum at or near $d^*$, and is decreasing on $[d^*, d_2]$.\n\n**D5 (Fiber Richness):** For capacity $d$, define the fiber $V_0(d) = \{w : K_n(w, d) = 0\}$ (zero-loss set). The fiber richness $R(d) = \dim V_0(d) = P(d) - \text{rank}(J_{K_n})$ where $J_{K_n}$ is the Jacobian of the loss w.r.t. parameters. For $d < d^*$, $V_0(d) = \emptyset$ (no interpolation). For $d > d^*$, $R(d) > 0$ and grows with $d$.\n\n**D6 (Stratum at Interpolation Threshold):** At $d = d^*$, the zero-loss set $V_0(d^*)$ is typically a single point or very low-dimensional (barely interpolating). The RLCT $\lambda(d^*)$ is determined by the most singular stratum accessible at this minimal capacity — forced memorization. Define $W_{\text{peak}}$ as the singular stratum dominating the posterior at $d = d^*$.\n\n**D7 (Regular Stratum):** For $d \gg d^*$, the fiber $V_0(d)$ is high-dimensional and the posterior can concentrate on a regular stratum $W_{\text{reg}} \subset V_0(d)$ where the loss Hessian is non-degenerate. On a regular stratum, $\lambda = P(d)/2$ (standard asymptotic), but the effective generalization depends on the RLCT of the dominant stratum, not the full parameter count.\n\n## Claims\n\n**C1 (PROVEN): $R_{\text{an,exp}}$-definability.** The loss $K_n(w, d)$ is definable in $R_{\text{an,exp}}$ for standard architectures (ReLU/softmax/CE compositions), as in all prior syntheses. The capacity parameter $d$ enters as a discrete index selecting the parameter space dimension. $\square$\n\n**C2 (CONJECTURAL): Interpolation threshold = RLCT phase transition.** $\lambda(d)$ achieves a local maximum at $d = d^*$: $\lambda(d^*) \geq \lambda(d)$ for $d$ near $d^*$. The double descent peak in test error corresponds to this RLCT maximum. The Watanabe generalization bound $G(d) \sim \lambda(d) \cdot \log(n) / n + U(d)/\sqrt{n}$ peaks when $\lambda(d)$ peaks.\n\n**C3 (CONJECTURAL): Overparameterization lowers RLCT via fiber richness.** For $d > d^*$, the fiber richness $R(d)$ increases with $d$, providing more directions in parameter space to achieve zero loss. The posterior can access lower-RLCT strata (smoother solutions) within the larger fiber. Formally: $\lambda(d)$ is non-increasing for $d > d^*$ (in expectation over data). The mechanism: larger $V_0(d)$ contains strata of lower codimension that correspond to simpler functions.\n\n**C4 (CONJECTURAL): Peak RLCT at regular stratum value.** At the interpolation threshold $d = d^*$, the model barely fits. The dominant stratum $W_{\text{peak}}$ has $\lambda(d^*) \approx P(d^*)/2 = n/2$ (the regular value), because the minimal-capacity interpolating solution lies on a near-regular stratum where the Hessian is barely non-degenerate. For $d > d^*$, singular strata with $\lambda < P(d)/2$ become accessible, reducing the effective RLCT below the regular value.\n\n**C5 (CONJECTURAL): Capacity-axis vs time-axis duality with grokking.** Double descent (capacity-axis) and grokking (time-axis, #491) are dual manifestations of the same RLCT phase transition. In grokking, the posterior migrates from high-RLCT to low-RLCT strata over training time at fixed $d$. In double descent, the posterior accesses progressively lower-RLCT strata as $d$ increases at fixed training time. The unifying formula: $G \sim \lambda \cdot \log(n)/n$ where $\lambda$ decreases either via time (grokking) or capacity (double descent).\n\n## Open Problems\n1.
