For exponential family or neural network models, p(y|x,theta) = exp(f(theta, x, y)) / Z(theta, x) where f is polynomial-analytic in theta and Z is the partition function (exponential integral). K(theta) = E_p[log p] - E_p[log q]. Both terms involve exp, log, polynomials in theta → R_{an,exp}-definable by Wilkie's theorem. The projection pi = K is definable. Fibers K^{-1}(c) = {theta : K(theta) = c} are definable sets (sublevel sets of definable functions). The bundle projection, fibers, and singular locus are all R_{an,exp}-definable.\n\n**D5 (RLCT from Fiber Geometry — C4 CONJECTURAL):**\nFor regular fibers (non-singular): lambda = codim(W*) / 2 = (d - dim(W*)) / 2. This is the codim/2 formula validated in #69.\nFor singular fibers: the RLCT is determined by the local geometry of the most singular accessible fiber. Conjecture: lambda = lim_{epsilon->0} [dim(K^{-1}[0,epsilon]) - dim(W*)] / [2 * ord(K, W*)] where ord(K, W*) is the order of vanishing of K along W*. Under Newton non-degeneracy (#69): lambda = d - dim(W*) - sum(alpha_i * v_i) where (alpha, v) ranges over Newton polyhedron vertices.\nFiber-geometric interpretation: lambda measures how much the fiber "thickens" near W* relative to the vanishing order.\n\n**D6 (Holonomy and Phase Transitions — C5 CONJECTURAL):**\nParallel transport around a loop gamma in R_+ avoiding singular fibers yields holonomy hol(gamma) in GL(d - dim(fiber)). As beta increases (c_beta decreases), the posterior support may cross a singular fiber boundary, changing the effective holonomy class. Phase transition at beta* where posterior support first reaches a singular fiber.\n\n**D7 (Connection Degeneration at Singular Fibers — C3 CONJECTURAL):**\nAt a singular fiber K^{-1}(c*), the Fisher metric g_ij degenerates: rank(g) drops along singular directions. The horizontal distribution H becomes ill-defined (cannot decompose T_theta into H + V uniquely). J_KL is valid at singular fibers where g degenerates. Away from singular points, g_ij is positive definite, giving a Riemannian metric on Theta. The orthogonal complement of V_theta (fiber tangent) under g gives H_theta (horizontal distribution).
