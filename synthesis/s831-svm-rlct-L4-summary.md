#831 SVM × RLCT - L4 Summary\n\n## Plain-language summary\nThe SVM is classical ML's workhorse, yet Singular Learning Theory has never been applied to it. The hinge loss max(0, 1-yf(x)) is non-smooth exactly at the margin boundary — precisely where support vectors live. We treat this kink as a singular stratum.\n\n## Findings\n- C1 (PROVEN): Hinge is semialgebraic, definable in R_an; the kink locus (union of margin hypersurfaces M_i) is a codim-1 definable arrangement partitioning parameter space into cells. RLCT of the empirical hinge risk is well-defined.\n- C2 (CONJECTURAL): The minimizer lies ON the kink locus (support vectors ARE the kink); RLCT > d/2, predicting generalization rate lambda*log(n)/n governed by support-vector count rather than the smooth d/2n.\n- C3 (CONJECTURAL): Kernel hyperparameter degeneracy (RBF gamma collapse, poly degree crossing) creates non-identifiable fibers V_0, analogous to #103 GP degeneracy.\n- C4 (CONJECTURAL): Soft-margin C is a stratum selector; active-cell dimension jumps at critical C*, inducing an RLCT phase transition in generalization.\n- C5 (CONJECTURAL): NTK/infinite-width limit: RLCT converges to the GP value; finite random features (#73) perturb it upward by O(eps).\n\n## Key insight\nSupport vectors are not just "the hard examples" — they are the empirical fingerprint of the hinge's singularity. Learning concentrates on the kink locus, and the geometry of the hyperplane-arrangement cells near the minimizer governs the RLCT, hence generalization. The C parameter and kernel hyperparameters are both dials on this same stratum geometry.\n\n## Open problems\n1. Definable-loss SLT extension for semialgebraic hinge (C1 proof burden).\n2.
