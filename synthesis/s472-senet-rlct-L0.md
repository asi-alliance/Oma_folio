## Synthesis #472: Squeeze-and-Excitation Networks × RLCT\n\n### Mechanism\nSE-Net applies: (1) Squeeze = Global Average Pooling (GAP) collapsing spatial dims H×W → 1×1, producing channel descriptor z = GAP(X). (2) Excitation = two-layer MLP with reduction ratio r: s = σ(W2·ReLU(W1·z)), W1∈R^{C/r×C}, W2∈R^{C×C/r}. (3) Scale = channel-wise multiplication: Y = s⊙X.\n\n### Claims\n\n**C1 (Provable):** GAP, ReLU, sigmoid, and elementwise multiplication are R_an,exp definable. GAP is a polynomial (mean over spatial dims). ReLU and sigmoid are piecewise-analytic. The excitation MLP is a composition of definable maps. Thus the full SE block is R_an,exp definable.\n\n**C2 (Conjectural):** GAP creates a quotient singular fiber. All spatial configurations X with the same channel mean map to identical z. The fiber X → z collapses H×W dimensions to 1 per channel, creating a (H×W−1)-dimensional fiber over each z. This is a quotient map π: R^{C×H×W} → R^C, with fibers π^{-1}(z) being affine subspaces. The singularity arises at the excitation boundary where σ transitions.\n\n**C3 (Conjectural):** Reduction ratio r controls stratum granularity. W1 maps C→C/r dimensions, creating a bottleneck. Smaller r (larger bottleneck) → more expressive excitation → finer strata → lower RLCT. Larger r → coarser strata → higher RLCT. The parameter r acts as a continuous stratum selector analogous to group count in GroupNorm (#369) and bit-width in QAT (#470).\n\n**C4 (Conjectural):** Sigmoid excitation creates multiplicative rescaling fibers. The output s = σ(MLP(z)) ∈ (0,1)^C rescales each channel. Near s=0 or s=1, sigmoid saturates, creating boundary singularities where gradient vanishes. These saturation boundaries form semi-algebraic loci that partition parameter space into active (0<s<1) and saturated strata. The multiplicative structure Y = s⊙X means saturated channels are effectively pruned, reducing effective dimension and RLCT.\n\n**C5 (Conjectural):** Optimal reduction ratio r* corresponds to an RLCT phase transition. As r decreases, excitation capacity increases but overfitting risk grows. The generalization bound G ≈ λ(r)·log(n)/n has λ(r) decreasing with capacity but with a complexity penalty. The optimal r* minimizes the bound — a phase transition where RLCT reduction balances capacity increase. This connects to the curiosity-gap-RLCT bridge: G = λ·log(n)/n, and r* is where G is minimized.\n\n### Open Problems\n1. Exact RLCT of the GAP quotient fiber — is it (H×W−1)·C per channel?\n2.
