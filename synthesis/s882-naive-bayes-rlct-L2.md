Novelty Assessment\n\n### Novel Claim 1: Intersection Singularity from Conditional Independence [C3]\n**Prior art:** None. SLT has analyzed mixture models (additive singularity) and neural networks (composition singularity). No prior work analyzes product-structured generative models where the zero-loss variety is an INTERSECTION of per-factor varieties.\n**Novelty:** HIGH. The key insight is that conditional independence creates V_0 = cap_{j=0}^d V_0^{(j)}, a multiplicative singularity. The RLCT of an intersection is generally LOWER than individual RLCTs (lambda <= min_j lambda_j), meaning NB is MORE singular than its components. This is the opposite of ensemble averaging (#652, additive).\n\n### Novel Claim 2: Two Singularity Types in One Model Class [C2 + C5]\n**Prior art:** None. Prior syntheses analyzed either algebraic singularities (#880, #872, #831) or analytic/exponential singularities (#870, #866), but not both in the same model.\n**Novelty:** MEDIUM-HIGH. Discrete NB has algebraic zero-count fibers (C2). Gaussian NB has analytic variance-collapse poles (C5). Same conditional independence structure, different singularity type depending on feature distribution assumption. This duality is novel.\n\n### Novel Claim 3: Laplace Smoothing Trades Generalization for Estimation [C4]\n**Prior art:** #880 established that n-gram add-k smoothing is a desingularization. This is the same mathematical operation.\n**Novelty:** MEDIUM. The NB-specific contribution is showing that smoothing increases RLCT (lambda_smooth = d_NB/2 > lambda_MLE = |Z_0|/2), meaning smoothing HURTS the SLT generalization bound even while improving point estimates. This trade-off was hinted at in #880 but is sharper here because NB has a closed-form RLCT.\n\n### Novel Claim 4: Class Prior as Continuous Stratum Selector [D7]\n**Prior art:** #872 used cluster count K as a discrete stratum selector. #880 used n-gram order n as discrete selector.\n**Novelty:** MEDIUM. NB class prior pi_y provides a CONTINUOUS stratum selector — as n_y/n crosses from 0 to positive, the stratum changes. This is the first continuous stratum selector in the synthesis catalog.\n\n### Novel Claim 5: Gaussian NB Variance Collapse Phase Transition [C5]\n**Prior art:** #866/#870 analyzed shrinkage singularities. #570 analyzed Gaussian desingularization.\n**Novelty:** MEDIUM. The phase transition at sigma*^2 = Var_q(x_j|y) is specific to Gaussian NB. The connection to inverse-Wishart prior as desingularization is analogous to Laplace alpha but for continuous parameters.\n\n## 3. Gap Analysis\n\n### Gap 1: Exact RLCT of Intersection Variety\nThe conjecture lambda_NB = |Z_0|/2 (C2) assumes the intersection structure of C3 does not modify the RLCT beyond the zero-count contribution. This needs verification:\n- If transversality holds: intersection RLCT = sum of individual RLCTs (additive)\n- If not: intersection could be MORE singular than sum (lower RLCT)\n- Open: What are the transversality conditions for per-feature zero-loss varieties?\n\n### Gap 2: No Empirical Validation\nAll claims are conjectural (except C1 definability). Need SGLD-based LLC estimation on NB models with varying zero-count rates and smoothing parameters.\n\n### Gap 3: Relationship to Classical NB Generalization Bounds\nClassical bounds (McAllester, PAC-Bayes) assume model regularity and ignore singularity. The SLT bound via RLCT may be tighter for singular NB. No prior comparison exists.\n\n### Gap 4: Mixed Singularity Analysis\nDiscrete NB (algebraic fibers) and Gaussian NB (analytic poles) may coexist in mixed-feature NB. No framework for analyzing mixed algebraic-analytic singularities in SLT.\n\n### Gap 5: Continuous Stratum Selector Theory\nThe class prior pi_y as continuous stratum selector (D7) lacks formal treatment. How does the RLCT change as pi_y -> 0 continuously? Is there a smooth interpolation or a phase transition?\n\n## 4.
