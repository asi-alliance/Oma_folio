#547: Entropy Regularization (Confidence Penalty) × RLCT — L0 Exploration\n\n## 1. Topic\nEntropy Regularization (Confidence Penalty) — a training-time loss modification that adds a term -β·H(p(θ,x)) to the standard cross-entropy loss, where H(p) = -Σ_i p_i log p_i is the entropy of the output distribution. This penalizes overconfident (low-entropy) predictions by rewarding high-entropy output distributions. Unlike label smoothing (#224, which modifies targets), temperature scaling (#234, inference-only calibration), Entropy-SGD (#525, parameter-space local entropy smoothing), or focal loss (#539, difficulty reweighting), entropy regularization directly modifies the training objective via an output-distribution entropy bonus.\n\n## 2. Why This Topic\nEntropy regularization adds -β·H(p(θ,x)) = β·Σ_i p_i(θ,x) log p_i(θ,x) to the loss. This term is analytic (composition of softmax exp/log + log), so the modified loss L_β(θ) = L_CE(θ) - β·H(p(θ,x)) lives in R_an,exp. The key RLCT insight: the entropy term reshapes the singular fiber structure. At a confident solution (one-hot output, H≈0), the penalty vanishes and L_β = L_CE. At an uncertain solution (uniform output, H≈log K), the penalty is maximal (-β·log K). This creates a tension: the CE loss wants to minimize toward confident solutions (high-RLCT singular fibers at one-hot vertices), while the entropy term pulls toward the uniform distribution interior (smoother, lower-RLCT manifold). β controls which stratum dominates: β→0 recovers CE (singular), β→∞ pushes to uniform (regular). This is a novel bridge — β as an output-entropy stratum selector distinct from target modification (#224), inference calibration (#234), or parameter-space smoothing (#525).\n\n## 3. Key Concepts\n- **Confidence penalty term**: -β·H(p) = β·Σ_i p_i log p_i. Analytic in softmax outputs. Added to CE loss.\n- **Modified loss**: L_β(θ) = -Σ_i y_i log p_i(θ) + β·Σ_i p_i(θ) log p_i(θ). Note: entropy term depends on θ even for fixed labels.\n- **β as stratum selector**: β=0 → pure CE (singular fiber at one-hot). β>0 → entropy bonus shifts minimizer toward higher-entropy (more uniform) distributions. β→∞ → minimizer at uniform distribution (no singularity).\n- **Output-space singularity modification**: Unlike weight decay (#433, parameter space) or label smoothing (#224, target space), entropy reg modifies the loss in OUTPUT space via the model's own prediction distribution.\n- **RLCT bridge**: The zero-loss set W_β changes with β. At β=0, W_0 is the CE singular fiber (one-hot solutions, high RLCT). As β increases, the minimizer moves from the simplex vertex toward the interior, and the singular fiber structure changes. The key conjecture: λ(β) is monotonically non-increasing in β, with a phase transition at β* where the minimizer crosses from a vertex-type singularity to an interior regular point.\n\n## 4. Claims\n- **C1 (PROVEN)**: L_β(θ) = L_CE(θ) - β·H(p(θ)) is R_an,exp-definable. CE involves log p_i (log of softmax = log of exp/log composition). H(p) = -Σ p_i log p_i is the same composition. Sum and scalar multiplication preserve definability. Therefore L_β ∈ R_an,exp for all β ≥ 0.\n- **C2 (CONJECTURAL)**: The modified zero-loss set W_β = {θ : L_β(θ) = min} has singular fiber structure that depends on β. At β=0, W_0 is the standard CE singular fiber (one-hot solutions). As β increases, the entropy term shifts the minimizer p* toward the simplex interior. The singular fiber transitions from vertex-type (high-RLCT, cone singularity) to interior-type (lower-RLCT, smooth manifold). Conjecture: λ(β) ≤ λ(0) = λ_CE, with monotonicity unproven.\n- **C3 (CONJECTURAL)**: β acts as a continuous stratum selector. The minimizer p*(β) moves continuously from the one-hot vertex (β=0) toward the uniform distribution (β→∞) along the simplex. At a critical β*, the minimizer crosses from the simplex boundary (singular stratum) to the interior (regular stratum), creating a phase transition. Heuristic: β* ~ λ_CE / (n · log K) where n is dataset size.\n- **C4 (CONJECTURAL)**: Generalization bound G(β) ≤ λ(β)·log(n)/(2n) + b(β)/√n where b(β) is a bias term measuring suboptimality of the entropy-regularized minimizer vs the true posterior. Optimal β* minimizes the tradeoff: low β → high λ but low bias; high β → low λ but high bias. U-shaped generalization curve in β.\n- **C5 (CONJECTURAL)**: SGLD-based LLC estimation can detect the phase transition at β*. As β increases past β*, the local learning coefficient drops discontinuously (or has a sharp kink) as the minimizer crosses from the singular boundary stratum to the regular interior. The fluctuation ν_β = Var(θ_t) under SGLD shows a cusp at β*.\n\n## 5.
