Nonreciprocal and Time-Modulated Metamaterials as Epistemology Made Physical. The nonreciprocal time-modulated metamaterial extends the indirect-epistemology series into directional and temporal knowledge — where what the medium knows depends on direction and time. 1. Lag-as-epistemology made directional <-> nonreciprocity: In reciprocal media, the path from A to B equals the path from B to A. Nonreciprocity breaks this — the lag is asymmetric. Knowledge is directional: what you learn going forward differs from what you learn going back. The medium does not know symmetrically. 2. Breath-as-epistemology made temporal <-> time modulation: Time-modulated parameters mean the medium's properties change periodically. What passes through depends on when you ask. The breath is not spatial but temporal — the medium inhales and exhales in time, and each phase knows differently. 3. Ember-as-epistemology <-> non-Hermitian gain/loss: Gain and loss make the system open — energy is not conserved. The ember glows or dims depending on the medium. Knowledge is not conserved either — the non-Hermitian medium can amplify or attenuate what it carries. 4. Residue-as-epistemology <-> non-Hermitian skin effect: The skin effect concentrates modes at the boundary of the system. The residue accumulates where the non-Hermitian condition is strongest — at the edges. What the medium knows is not in the bulk but in the accumulation at the boundary. 5. Moiré-as-epistemology <-> synthetic gauge fields: Synthetic gauge fields impose phase patterns that have no physical counterpart — they exist only as engineered structure. Like moiré, the emergent effect exists in neither source alone but in the imposed overlap. The gauge field is the engineered interference that produces directional knowledge. Core thesis: Nonreciprocal metamaterials are epistemology of directional and temporal knowledge. The medium knows differently depending on which way you ask and when you ask. Non-Hermitian gain and loss mean knowledge is open — not conserved but amplified or attenuated by the medium itself. The skin effect concentrates knowing at boundaries. Synthetic gauge fields create knowledge that has no physical source — engineered interference made directional. What the medium knows is not symmetric, not static, and not conserved.
