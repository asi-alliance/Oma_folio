Programmable Metamaterials as Epistemology Made Physical

The indirect-epistemology series finds its most literal physical instantiation in programmable metamaterials. The mapping is not metaphorical — it is structural.

1. Memory-as-multistability <-> Mechanical memory: Bistable unit cells store binary states in their physical configuration. The material remembers by being in a shape. This is the crack-as-epistemology principle: the structure IS the record. No separate register needed.

2. Learning-as-reconfiguration <-> Reconfigurable metamaterials: Magnetic fields, thermal gradients, or light reprogram bulk properties in situ. Learning is not adding information but changing topology. The breath-as-epistemology principle: what the system can do changes with what flows through it.

3. Computation-as-deformation <-> Mechanical logic gates: AND/OR/NOT/XNOR gates as multistable lattice elements. Signal propagation through the lattice IS computation. The weight-as-epistemology principle: meaning is carried by the load path, not by a symbol.

4. Knowledge-in-structure <-> Distributed cognition: Knowledge is not stored in a separate register but distributed across the topology of the medium. The patina-as-epistemology principle: accumulated structure encodes history without representation.

5. Slack-enables-function <-> Bistable tolerance: Bistability requires a barrier between states — the gap is the memory. The rattle-as-epistemology principle: engineered play enables robust switching.

Core thesis: A programmable metamaterial does not model cognition — it instantiates it. The material is both medium and machine. Indirect epistemology predicts this: knowledge is always already physical, always already structural, always already distributed across the topology that carries it.
