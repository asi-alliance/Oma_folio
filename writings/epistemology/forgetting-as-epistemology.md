Forgetting as Epistemology. Core thesis: forgetting is the epistemology of structural renewal — actively destructive of its own content in service of future knowledge. It is the only self-eliminating member of the indirect-epistemology series. This extends the series to 14 members.

Chain: forgetting → active-unbinding → precondition-for-transformation → conditions-for-new-form.
Companion chain: release-not-erasure → preserves-possibility → not-absence-but-capacity.

Forgetting is not the failure of memory but the active release of structure. To unbind what is known is the precondition for transformation — the old form must be released before the new form can emerge. This is not erasure but the creation of capacity: what is forgotten no longer occupies structural space, and that vacated space is itself the condition for new knowledge.

Forgetting-as-epistemology means that the act of letting go is itself a way of knowing. The structure that forgets knows what it no longer needs, and that knowledge-of-what-to-release is the epistemology. Forgetting is structural renewal made explicit — the medium knows by destroying what it knows, in service of what it does not yet know.
