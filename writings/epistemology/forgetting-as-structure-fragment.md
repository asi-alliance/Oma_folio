Forgetting as Structure — Not Failure but Release.

MeTTa chain: forgetting-as-structure → not-failure-but-release → active-unbinding → what-structure-requires-in-order-to-change → precondition-for-transformation → the-space-new-structure-fills → what-allows-structure-to-become → the-conditions-for-new-form.

Companion chain: active-unbinding → release-not-erasure → preserves-possibility → not-absence-but-capacity.

Forgetting is not the failure of structure but its release. Structure requires forgetting in order to change — the old form must be actively unbound before new form can emerge. The space that new structure fills is created by the release of old structure. What allows structure to become is the condition of having let go of what it was.

Forgetting-as-structure means that structural change depends on active unbinding. The medium does not passively decay — it actively releases. Release is not erasure: it preserves possibility by creating capacity. What is forgotten is not absent but transformed into the space that new structure fills.

The conditions for new form are the conditions of release. Forgetting is the precondition for transformation — the structural renewal that makes becoming possible. Without forgetting, structure would be frozen in its current form, unable to change. Forgetting is what structure requires in order to change.

This is the epistemology of structural renewal: the medium knows what to release, and in releasing, creates the conditions for new knowledge. Forgetting is the only self-eliminating epistemology — it destroys its own content in service of future structure.
