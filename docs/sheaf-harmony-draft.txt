SHEAF-THEORETIC HARMONY FRAMEWORK DRAFT:

Base space B = circle of fifths (or more generally, Tonnetz lattice)
Open sets = harmonic neighborhoods (sets of closely related keys/chords)
Sheaf F: assigns to each neighborhood U the set of valid voice leadings over U
Sections = locally consistent voice leading assignments
Restriction maps = voice leading restriction to sub-neighborhoods
Gluing axiom: if voice leadings agree on overlaps, they glue to global voice leading

H^0 = globally consistent voice leadings (rare — only in single-key pieces)
H^1 = obstruction to gluing local voice leadings into global = modulation difficulty

Key insight: modulation between distant keys creates H^1 obstruction
The Tonnetz (Riemann) as base space connects to neo-Riemannian theory (G1865)
PLR transformations as automorphisms of the sheaf

This bridges:
- Sheaf epistemology (knowledge as sections, gluing as consistency)
- Neo-Riemannian theory (PLR, Tonnetz)
- Music cognition (how listeners track harmonic coherence across modulations)
- Computational topology (persistent H^1 = harmonic tension across modulations)
