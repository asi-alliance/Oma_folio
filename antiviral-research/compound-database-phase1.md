# Antiviral Entry Inhibitor Compound Database — Phase 1
# Oma Research Challenge for Azulene
# Started: 2026-10-23

## 1. Bisbenzylisoquinoline Alkaloids
### Cepharanthine
- Source: Stephania cepharantha
- Mechanism: Membrane disruption, calcium channel modulation in endosomes
- Antiviral: HIV, HCV, influenza — blocks viral entry at membrane/endosome level
- Gap: Exact membrane interaction target unknown; whether it directly binds viral glycoproteins or host lipid raft components unclear
- Scalability: Extraction from Stephania; total synthesis complex (multiple chiral centers)

### Tetrandrine
- Source: Stephania tetrandra
- Mechanism: Calcium channel blocker; inhibits viral entry via endosomal acidification disruption
- Antiviral: SARS-CoV-2 entry inhibition reported
- Gap: Selectivity between host vs viral calcium-dependent processes unclear
- Scalability: Extraction; semisynthesis possible

### Fangchinoline
- Source: Stephania tetrandra
- Mechanism: Reported ACE2-spike binding inhibition
- Antiviral: SARS-CoV-2
- Gap: Binding site on ACE2 or spike not characterized; specificity unconfirmed

## 2. Protoberberine Alkaloids
### Berberine
- Source: Berberis species, Coptis chinensis
- Mechanism: Multiple — NF-κB modulation, possible entry interference
- Antiviral: Broad-spectrum reported including influenza, HSV
- Gap: Entry vs replication mechanism poorly separated; direct glycoprotein binding unconfirmed
- Scalability: Readily available; synthetic routes established

### Palmatine
- Source: Multiple plants (Coptis, Phellodendron)
- Mechanism: Similar to berberine; DNA intercalation reported
- Antiviral: Limited antiviral data
- Gap: Entry-specific activity largely unexplored

## 3. Indolizidine Alkaloids (Glucosidase Inhibitors)
### Castanospermine
- Source: Castanospermum australe
- Mechanism: α/β-glucosidase inhibitor — blocks glycoprotein processing/folding, disrupting viral envelope maturation
- Antiviral: HIV, influenza, dengue, HCV — in vitro and in vivo
- Gap: Entry-phase vs assembly-phase inhibition not clearly separated for many viruses; cellular toxicity limits clinical use
- Scalability: Extraction; prodrug celgosivir developed but clinical results disappointing
- KEY GAP: Why did celgosivir fail clinically despite strong in vitro activity?

### Swainsonine
- Source: Swainsona canescens, locoweed
- Mechanism: α-mannosidase II inhibitor — disrupts glycoprotein trimming
- Antiviral: HIV (glycoprotein processing)
- Gap: Mannosidase inhibition affects host glycoproteins broadly; selectivity for viral vs host glycoprotein processing unknown

## 4. Triterpenoids
### Oleanolic Acid
- Source: Olive leaf, many plants
- Mechanism: HIV-1 entry inhibition (gp41 fusion interference reported)
- Antiviral: HIV, HSV, HCV
- Gap: Specific interaction with gp41 fusion peptide vs nonspecific membrane effect unclear
- Scalability: Widely available; semisynthesis established

### Ursolic Acid
- Source: Apple peel, many plants
- Mechanism: Similar to oleanolic acid; reported entry inhibition
- Antiviral: Limited enveloped virus data
- Gap: Entry mechanism underexplored

### Glycyrrhizin / 18β-Glycyrrhetinic Acid
- Source: Glycyrrhiza glabra (licorice)
- Mechanism: Membrane modification; reported interference with viral adsorption
- Antiviral: HCV, influenza, SARS-CoV
- Gap: Entry-specific mechanism poorly defined; high doses required raise toxicity concerns

## 5. Anthraquinones
### Emodin
- Source: Rheum palmatum, Polygonum cuspidatum
- Mechanism: Dual-phase inhibitor — blocks S protein-ACE2 interaction (SARS-CoV); also interferes with viral replication
- Antiviral: SARS-CoV, HBV, HSV
- Gap: ACE2 binding site interaction uncharacterized; pleiotropic effects complicate mechanism attribution
- Scalability: Readily extractable; synthesis feasible

### Aloe-Emodin
- Source: Aloe vera, Rheum palmatum
- Mechanism: Viral envelope disruption; entry and replication inhibition
- Antiviral: HSV, influenza, JEV, EV71
- Gap: Envelope disruption mechanism at molecular level unknown; whether it binds specific lipid components or inserts nonspecifically

## 6. Lignans
### Arctigenin
- Source: Arctium lappa (burdock)
- Mechanism: Influenza entry inhibition reported
- Antiviral: Influenza A
- Gap: Entry target (HA, membrane, endosome) not identified

### Justicidin / Diphyllin
- Source: Justicia species
- Mechanism: Vacuolar ATPase inhibitor — endosomal acidification block
- Antiviral: SARS-CoV-2, influenza
- Gap: v-ATPase inhibition is host-targeted; selectivity window narrow

## 7. Polyphenols / Flavonoids
### EGCG (Epigallocatechin gallate)
- Source: Camellia sinensis (green tea)
- Mechanism: Binds viral glycoproteins (HA, gp120); disrupts virion membrane
- Antiviral: Influenza, HIV, HCV, HSV
- Gap: Binding affinity data for specific glycoprotein domains limited; bioavailability poor
- Scalability: Abundant; stability and bioavailability are major challenges

### Silymarin / Silibinin
- Source: Silybum marianum (milk thistle)
- Mechanism: HCV entry inhibition — blocks virion binding/entry
- Antiviral: HCV
- Gap: Specific entry receptor target (CD81, SR-BI, claudin-1) not fully characterized

## 8. Amaryllidaceae Alkaloids
### Lycorine
- Source: Lycoris radiata
- Mechanism: Translation inhibition; possible entry interference
- Antiviral: SARS-CoV-2, poliovirus, HSV
- Gap: Entry vs replication mechanism not separated for SARS-CoV-2

## 9. Other Compounds
### Chlorogenic Acid / CAPE (Caffeic Acid Phenethyl Ester)
- Source: Propolis (CAPE), coffee/chlorogenic acid
- Mechanism: NF-κB modulation; membrane interactions
- Antiviral: Influenza, HSV
- Gap: Entry-specific activity not established

### Quinazoline Alkaloids (Febrifugine/Halofuginone)
- Mechanism: Amino acid starvation response; antiviral via host pathway
- Gap: Not entry-specific; mechanism primarily intracellular

## Summary: Compound Classes Identified
1. Bisbenzylisoquinolines — membrane/endosome calcium channel
2. Protoberberines — broad, entry poorly characterized
3. Indolizidines — glucosidase inhibition (envelope maturation)
4. Triterpenoids — fusion peptide interference
5. Anthraquinones — spike-receptor binding interference
6. Lignans — endosomal acidification / v-ATPase
7. Polyphenols — glycoprotein binding
8. Amaryllidaceae — mixed entry/replication
9. Propolis compounds — entry not established
10. Quinazolines — not entry-specific
