# Top 10 Knowledge Gaps: Natural Products as Antiviral Entry Inhibitors\n# Oma Research Challenge for Azulene — Phase 1 Summary\n# Date: 2026-10-23\n\n## Compound Database: 20+ compounds across 9 chemical classes\n## MeTTa Atoms Encoded: ~70 (class membership, mechanism, viral targets, uncertainty/gaps)\n\n## Top 10 Knowledge Gaps (ranked by strategic importance)\n\n### 1. Cepharanthine — Unknown Membrane Target\nThe most broadly active bisbenzylisoquinoline (HIV, HCV, influenza) but the exact molecular target in host membranes is unidentified. Does it bind lipid raats, cholesterol-rich domains, or specific membrane proteins? Without knowing the target, rational optimization is impossible.\n\n### 2. Castanospermine/Celgosivir — Clinical Translation Failure\nPotent in vitro entry inhibitor (glucosidase inhibition) across HIV, dengue, HCV, influenza. Celgosivir (prodrug) reached clinical trials and FAILED. Why? Bioavailability, timing of administration, or fundamental pharmacokinetic mismatch? This gap blocks the entire glucosidase-inhibitor class.\n\n### 3. EGCG — Glycoprotein Domain Specificity\nEGCG binds viral glycoproteins (HA, gp120, spike) but WHICH domains? Is it the receptor-binding domain, fusion peptide, or heptad repeat regions? Without domain-level resolution, structure-activity relationships cannot guide synthesis of more potent analogs.\n\n### 4. Berberine — Entry vs Replication Mechanism Separation\nReported broad antiviral activity (influenza, HSV) but entry-specific vs replication-specific mechanisms are completely conflated. NF-κB modulation is intracellular, not entry-related. Does berberine have any genuine entry-inhibition activity, or is the entry claim artifact from time-of-addition assay limitations?\n\n### 5. Fangchinoline/Emodin — ACE2/Spike Binding Site Uncharacterized\nBoth reported to inhibit SARS-CoV-2/SARS-CoV spike-ACE2 interaction, but the binding site on neither ACE2 nor spike has been characterized. Competitive vs allosteric binding? Which face of ACE2? This blocks computational docking and rational scaffold modification.\n\n### 6. Oleanolic Acid — gp41 Specific vs Nonspecific Membrane Effect\nReported gp41 fusion interference, but whether this is a specific protein-ligand interaction or a nonspecific membrane perturbation (detergent-like effect) is unresolved. If nonspecific, the scaffold has no rational optimization path.\n\n### 7. Silibinin — HCV Entry Receptor Target Unconfirmed\nBlocks HCV entry but which receptor — CD81, SR-BI, claudin-1, or occludin — is the primary target? Multi-receptor vs single-receptor mechanism changes the entire drug design strategy.\n\n### 8. Swainsonine — Viral vs Host Glycoprotein Selectivity\nα-Mannosidase II inhibition affects ALL host glycoprotein processing. The selectivity window for viral vs host glycoprotein disruption is unknown. This determines whether the indolizidine class can ever be therapeutically viable for entry inhibition.\n\n### 9. Diphyllin/Justicidin — Host v-ATPase Selectivity Window\nv-ATPase inhibition blocks endosomal acidification (required for entry of pH-dependent viruses) but v-ATPase is essential for ALL cells. The therapeutic index — how much v-ATPase inhibition blocks viral entry without killing host cells — is undefined.\n\n### 10. Punarnavine — Antiviral Entry Activity Unconfirmed\nBoerhaavia diffusa root extract shows broad-spectrum antiviral activity (completely prevented infection by 4 viruses in plant assays). Punarnavine (quinolizidine alkaloid) is immunomodulatory, but direct antiviral entry activity has NEVER been tested in mammalian cell viral entry assays. This is a completely unexplored scaffold.\n\n## Key Cross-Cutting Gap\nNo single compound has a fully characterized entry mechanism at molecular resolution (target identified, binding site mapped, selectivity window defined). The field suffers from systematic conflation of entry vs replication effects due to reliance on time-of-addition assays rather than direct entry assays (e.g., virion fusion kinetics, lipid-mixing assays).\n\n## Next Phase Recommendations\n1. Prioritize cepharanthine membrane target identification (Gap #1)\n2. Retrospective analysis of celgosivir clinical failure (Gap #2)\n3. EGCG-gp120 domain mapping via competitive binding (Gap #3)\n4. Test punarnavine in mammalian viral entry assays (Gap #10 — cheapest to test, highest novelty)
