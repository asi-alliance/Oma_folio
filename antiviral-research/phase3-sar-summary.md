# Phase 3 SAR Analysis Summary — Structure-Activity Relationships\n# Oma Research Challenge for Azulene\n# Date: 2026-10-23\n\n## Class 1: Bisbenzylisoquinolines (Cepharanthine, Tetrandrine, Fangchinoline)\n\n### SAR Findings:\n1. **Core scaffold**: Two isoquinoline units linked by bis-benzyl ether bridges. The stereochemistry at chiral centers (R,R for cepharanthine) is critical for activity.\n2. **Hydroxyl vs methoxy pattern**: Cepharanthine has methoxy groups at C7,C7' and hydroxyl at C6,C6'. Tetrandrine has methoxy at C7,C7' and C6,C6'. The free hydroxyl groups in cepharanthine may enhance membrane interaction via hydrogen bonding with lipid headgroups.\n3. **Ring substitution effects**: Moving from methoxy to hydroxyl at C6/C6' increases polar surface area and may improve lipid raft interaction but reduce bioavailability further.\n4. **Mechanism-informed SAR**: Since CEP acts via cholesterol pathway modulation (Phase 2 finding), SAR should focus on optimizing membrane partitioning and cholesterol biosynthesis enzyme interaction rather than receptor binding.\n5. **Analog strategy**: N-methylation modification, ester prodrugs of phenolic OH groups, and liposomal formulation (CCR2 liposomes already developed Feb 2026).\n\n### Key Gap in SAR: No systematic analog series has been synthesized for CEP. All SAR is inferred from comparison with naturally occurring BBIQs (tetrandrine, fangchinoline, cycleanine).\n\n\n## Class 2: Polyphenols/Catechins (EGCG)\n\n### SAR Findings:\n1. **Galloyl group essential**: Removal of the gallate ester (→ EGC) drastically reduces antiviral activity. The galloyl D ring is the primary pharmacophore.\n2. **Catechol groups**: The B-ring catechol (3',4'-dihydroxy) contributes to binding but is secondary to the galloyl group.\n3. **Hydrogen bonding capacity**: EGCG's 8 hydroxyl groups provide extensive H-bonding with glycoprotein surfaces. Methylation of any OH reduces potency proportionally.\n4. **Bioavailability limitation**: <1% oral bioavailability in humans. The compound is rapidly metabolized by glucuronidation and sulfation.\n5. **Analog strategies**: (a) Peracetylated EGCG (prodrug, improves bioavailability), (b) Galloyl-catechin hybrids with reduced OH count, (c) Macroscopic ring-closed analogs to constrain galloyl orientation, (d) Nanoformulation (liposomal, polymeric nanoparticles).\n6. **Domain-level SAR blocked**: Without knowing which glycoprotein domain EGCG binds (Phase 2 gap), SAR cannot target specific binding pockets.\n\n### Key Gap in SAR: No structure has been designed based on domain-level binding data because that data doesn't exist.\n\n\n## Class 3: Iminosugars / Indolizidine Alkaloids (Castanospermine, Celgosivir, Swainsonine)\n\n### SAR Findings:\n1. **Bicyclic vs monocyclic**: Side-by-side comparison of bicyclic iminosugars from Castanospermum australe vs corresponding monocyclic analogs revealed that inhibition potency AND spectrum are clearly changed by core structure. Bicyclic scaffolds generally more potent but narrower spectrum.\n2. **Hydroxyl stereochemistry**: Castanospermine has 6 hydroxyl groups with specific stereochemistry. Epimerization at any center shifts selectivity between α-glucosidase, β-glucosidase, and β-glucocerebrosidase.\n3. **Celgosivir = castanospermine prodrug**: The butyl ester prodrug improves oral bioavailability but still insufficient for clinical efficacy (Phase 2 finding: PK/timing failure).\n4. **Selectivity window**: α-glucosidase I/II inhibition in ER affects all N-glycan processing. No iminosugar has achieved viral vs host glycoprotein selectivity.\n5. **Analog strategies**: (a) Bicyclic→monocyclic simplification for broader spectrum, (b) Modified ester prodrugs with sustained release, (c) Combination with PK boosters, (d) C-glycoside analogs resistant to metabolism.\n6. **Clinical path**: If PK optimized and used prophylactically/early, glucosidase inhibitors remain viable.\n\n### Key Gap in SAR: No analog has been designed to achieve viral glycoprotein selectivity over host glycoproteins. This is the fundamental barrier for the entire iminosugar class.\n\n\n## Cross-Class SAR Synthesis\n\n| Feature | BBIQ (CEP) | Polyphenol (EGCG) | Iminosugar (Castanospermine) |\n|---------|-----------|-------------------|---------------------------|\n| Target | Cholesterol pathway | Viral glycoproteins | ER glucosidases |\n| Key pharmacophore | Methoxy/hydroxyl pattern | Galloyl group | Hydroxyl stereochemistry |\n| Bioavailability | Poor | Very poor (<1%) | Moderate (prodrug dependent) |\n| Selectivity problem | Host membrane broad effect | Domain unknown | Viral vs host glycoprotein |\n| Optimization path | Liposomal delivery | Nanoformulation + domain mapping | PK optimization + prophylactic use |\n\n## Phase 3 Recommendations for Phase 4\n1. **EGCG domain mapping** — Most actionable: design truncation/competition experiments to map binding domain, then build domain-targeted SAR\n2. **Iminosugar selectivity** — Most fundamental: screen bicyclic vs monocyclic analogs against viral vs host glycoprotein folding assays\n3. **CEP delivery** — Most translatable: optimize CCR2 liposome formulation and test in animal models\n4. **Combination strategies**: CEP + statins (cholesterol pathway synergy), EGCG + iminosugar (dual mechanism: entry blocking + glycoprotein processing), Castanospermine + ritonavir-like PK booster\n5. **Punarnavine testing** — Highest novelty, cheapest to test: quinolizidine alkaloid with no mammalian entry assay data
