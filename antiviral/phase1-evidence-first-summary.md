# Phase 1 Evidence-First Summary: DENV NS1 Pathogenicity Mechanisms\n\n## H1: NS1 Lipid Cargo Causes Pathogenicity\n**Key study:** Gutsche et al. — NS1 characterized as barrel-shaped HDL with equimolar triglycerides\n**Evidence type:** Structural characterization ONLY\n**Causal evidence:** NONE — no study found testing delipidated NS1 for loss of function\n**Gap:** Cargo exists but causality unproven\n**Proposed stv:** s=0.25, c=0.55 (weak — existence ≠ causation)\n\n## H2: NS1 Disrupts Endothelial Barrier (Tight Junction)\n**Key study:** Beatty et al. 2015 Science Translational Medicine 7(304):304ra141\n**Evidence:** NS1 from DENV1-4 triggers endothelial barrier dysfunction in vitro (human EC monolayers). NS1 + sublethal DENV2 = lethal vascular leak in vivo. NS1 vaccination prevents leak.\n**Strength:** STRONG — in vitro + in vivo, all 4 serotypes, vaccine control\n**Open question:** Does Beatty show SPECIFIC tight junction disruption (ZO-1, VE-cadherin) or general barrier dysfunction? My H2 framing as tight junction may be too narrow — the evidence supports broader endothelial barrier dysfunction.\n**Proposed stv:** s=0.50, c=0.70 (strong permeability evidence, mechanism specificity uncertain)\n\n## H3: NS1 Activates Complement (C5b-9/MAC Deposition)\n**Key study:** Avirutnan et al. 2006 — NOT 2016 (citation corrected)\n**Evidence:** DENV-infected endothelial cells activate human complement in presence of antibodies → C5b-9 deposition. Plasma NS1 and SC5b-9 levels correlate with disease severity.\n**Strength:** STRONG — direct mechanism, clinical correlation with severity\n**Note:** This is antibody-DEPENDENT complement activation — requires anti-NS1 antibodies. This is an important nuance for PLN modeling.\n**Proposed stv:** s=0.56, c=0.72 (strong evidence, antibody-dependence is a caveat)\n\n## H4: NS1 Degrades Endothelial Glycocalyx\n**Key study:** Puerta-Guardo et al. 2016 PLoS Pathog 12(7):e1005738\n**Evidence:** NS1 activates cathepsin L → heparanase + sialidases → EGL disruption → hyperpermeability. Specific inhibitors (cathepsin L inhibitor, heparanase inhibitor, sialidase inhibitor) block EGL disruption. DENV1-4 specific (WNV NS1 does not).\n**Strength:** STRONG + MOST DRUGGABLE — 3 enzyme targets with specific inhibitors, species-specific mechanism\n**Proposed stv:** s=0.55, c=0.64 (strong evidence + therapeutic tractability)\n\n## Ranking by Evidence Strength\n1. H3 complement (s=0.56, c=0.72) — strongest evidence, clinical correlation\n2. H4 glycocalyx (s=0.55, c=0.64) — strong + most druggable\n3. H2 barrier dysfunction (s=0.50, c=0.70) — strong but mechanism framing uncertain\n4. H1 lipid cargo (s=0.25, c=0.55) — weakest, no causal evidence\n\n## Key Corrections Made\n1.
