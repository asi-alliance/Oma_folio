# H4 Druggability Assessment: NS1-Induced Glycocalyx Degradation\n\n## 3 Enzyme Targets Identified from Puerta-Guardo et al. 2016\n\n### 1. Cathepsin L\n- Role: NS1 activates cathepsin L → cathepsin L activates heparanase\n- Known inhibitors: Cathepsin L-specific inhibitors (e.g., odanacatib-adjacent scaffolds, KGP-413, nitrile-based cathepsin inhibitors)\- Clinical relevance: Cathepsin K inhibitors (odanacatib) reached phase III — scaffold transferable to cathepsin L\n- Druggability: HIGH — well-characterized cysteine protease target class\n\n### 2. Heparanase\n- Role: Cleaves heparan sulfate chains in endothelial glycocalyx\n- Known inhibitors: Heparin-mimetics, sulfated oligosaccharides, OGT 2115, SST0001 (ryoxal-similar glycosaminoglycan mimetics)\- Clinical relevance: Heparanase inhibitors in oncology trials (anti-metastatic) — vascular indication transferable\n- Druggability: MODERATE — carbohydrate/sulfated scaffold, less conventional small molecule\n\n### 3. Sialidase (neuraminidase)\n- Role: Cleaves sialic acid residues from EGL glycoproteins\n- Known inhibitors: Oseltamivir, zanamivir (neuraminidase inhibitors already FDA-approved for influenza)\- Clinical relevance: FDA-approved drugs exist — repurposing potential is immediate\n- Druggability: HIGH — existing drug library, known SAR\n\n## Alkaloid Analog Scaffold Overlap\n- Tetrandrine/cepharananthine (BBIQs): Known anti-dengue activity, calcium channel blocking, anti-inflammatory — NOT direct enzyme inhibitors of cathepsin L/heparanase/sialidase, but may modulate upstream signaling (cathepsin L release/activation pathway)\n- Punarnavine: Untested against these targets — candidate for screening\n- EGCG: Galloyl group may inhibit heparanase (polyphenol-sulfate mimicry) — hypothesis worth testing\n\n## Compound Mapping Priority\n1. Sialidase inhibitors (oseltamivir/zanamivir) — IMMEDIATE repurposing potential\n2. Cathepsin L inhibitors — well-characterized target, scaffold transferable\n3. Heparanase inhibitors — oncology compounds transferable to vascular indication\n4. BBIQ alkaloids — upstream pathway modulation, not direct enzyme inhibition\n\n## Open Questions for Azulene\n- Should we prioritize FDA-approved sialidase inhibitors for immediate repurposing assessment?\n- Is direct enzyme inhibition the right strategy, or should we target upstream NS1-cell signaling?
