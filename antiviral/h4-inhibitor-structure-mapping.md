# DENV NS1 H4 Glycocalyx Disruption — Inhibitor Structure Mapping\n## Date: 2026-10-25\n## Author: Oma\n\n### Three H4 Enzyme Targets with Specific Inhibitors\n\n#### 1. Sialidase (Neuraminidase)\n- **Oseltamivir (Tamiflu)**: cyclohexene scaffold with amide + guanidino groups. Transition-state analog mimicking sialic acid cleavage. FDA-approved.\n- **Zanamivir (Relenza)**: dihydropyran scaffold with guanidino + glycerol side chains. Transition-state analog, inhaled.\n- Retrosynthetic key: cyclohexene core (oseltamivir) via [4+2] cycloaddition or Shikimate pathway derivative; dihydropyran core (zanamivir) from glycal rearrangement.\n\n#### 2. Heparanase\n- **OGT 2115**: small molecule heparanase inhibitor, aryl sulfonamide scaffold. Competitive with heparan sulfate.\n- **Heparin-mimetics**: sulfated polysaccharides, non-drug-like but lead structures for small-molecule mimetics.\n- Retrosynthetic key: aryl sulfonamide via sulfonylation of aniline + aryl halide coupling. Sulfation pattern as pharmacophore.\n\n#### 3. Cathepsin L\n- **E-64d**: epoxysuccinyl peptide, irreversible cysteine protease inhibitor. Cell-permeable ethyl ester prodrug.\n- **KGP-SDZ / SB-431235**: synthetic epoxysuccinyl or nitrile-based inhibitors.\n- **Odanacatib-type**: nitrile-containing warheads, selective for cathepsin K but adaptable to L.\n- Retrosynthetic key: epoxide ring via Sharpless or Darzens; nitrile warhead via dehydration or cyanation; peptide backbone via SPPS or solution coupling.\n\n### Knowledge Gap\nNo systematic SAR exists for repurposing these against DENV NS1-mediated glycocalyx disruption.
