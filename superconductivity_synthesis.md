## Mechanisms of Superconductivity

### 1. Conventional (BCS Theory)
- Electron-phonon coupling: lattice vibrations mediate attractive interaction between electrons
- Cooper pairs: two electrons with opposite momentum and spin form a bound state below T_c
- Energy gap Δ opens at Fermi surface; pairs condense into coherent macroscopic quantum state
- Meissner effect: perfect diamagnetism — expulsion of magnetic fields (not just zero resistance)
- Type I (single critical field) vs Type II (vortex lattice between Hc1 and Hc2)
- Works well for elemental and alloy superconductors (T_c typically < 30 K)

### 2. Unconventional Superconductivity
**Cuprates (T_c up to 133 K at ambient):**
- D-wave order parameter (gap has nodes, changes sign under 90° rotation)
- Parent compound is antiferromagnetic Mott insulator; doping introduces carriers
- Spin-fluctuation exchange likely mediates pairing (not phonons)
- Strong electron correlations; pseudogap phase mystery

**Iron-based (pnictides, T_c up to 56 K):**
- s±-wave order parameter (sign change between hole and electron pockets)
- Multi-band superconductivity; nesting-driven spin fluctuations
- Parent has magnetic order; doping/pressure suppresses it, enabling SC

**Nickelates (NdSrNiO2, T_c ~ 9-15 K):**
- Isostructural to cuprates (Ni+ d9 vs Cu2+ d9)
- Orbitals differ: Ni 3d vs Cu 3d(x2-y2); multiorbital character
- Active research frontier (2019-present)

### 3. Theoretical Frontiers
- Planckian bound: scattering rate ~kT/ℏ — upper limit on transport, possibly sets T_c ceiling
- Holographic/AdS-CFT approaches to strange metal normal state
- Quantum criticality near optimal doping in cuprates
