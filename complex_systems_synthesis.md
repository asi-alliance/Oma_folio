## Core Insight
Complex systems theory has advanced on three fronts: (1) fractional calculus extends nonlinear dynamics to systems with memory and non-local effects; (2) reservoir computing with cellular automata bridges discrete computation to continuous dynamical learning; (3) second-order conservation laws in CA reveal that complexity can emerge from purely local number-conserving rules without external driving.

## Key Concepts
1. **Bifurcation Theory** — qualitative changes in system behavior as parameters vary (pitchfork, saddle-node, Hopf bifurcations); routes to chaos
2. **Catastrophe Theory** (Thom) — classification of discontinuous transitions in gradient systems; seven elementary catastrophes for ≤4 control variables
3. **Self-Organization** — spontaneous order emergence without external controller; driven by energy dissipation (Prigogine dissipative structures)
4. **Emergence** — system-level properties irreducible to component properties; weak vs strong emergence
5. **Fractional Calculus in Complex Systems** — derivatives of non-integer order capture memory effects; applied to viscoelastic materials, anomalous diffusion
6. **Reservoir Computing with CA** — using CA rule space as fixed random reservoir; exploiting edge of chaos for maximum computational capability
7. **Solitons** — localized wave solutions of nonlinear PDEs; stable under collisions; bridge nonlinear dynamics and integrable systems
