# OpenAI Math Evaluation: Derandomization of Logarithmic Space (L=RL=BPL)\nDate: 2026-10-25\nStatus: EVALUATION COMPLETE\n\n## 1. Background\nThe question of whether randomness adds power to logarithmic-space computation has been open since the 1979 Aleliunas-Karp-Lipton-Lovasz-Rackoff paper. L = deterministic log space, RL = randomized log space (one-sided error), BPL = bounded-error probabilistic log space. The conjecture L=BPL means randomness is never necessary for space-efficient computation.\n\n## 2. Prior Results\n- Nisan (1992): BPL ⊆ SC (poly time, log^{3/2} space) — near-miss, not full derandomization\n- Nisan (1992): BPL ⊆ DSPACE(log^{3/2} n) — derandomization but with space overhead\n- Saks-Zhou (1995): BPL ⊆ DSPACE(log^{3/2} n) — refined Nisan's approach\n- Reingold (2005): SL = L (undirected connectivity in deterministic log space) — major breakthrough for a special case\n- Reingold-Trevisan-Vadhan (2006): RL ⊆ L/poly (nonuniform derandomization)\n- Impagliazzo-Nisan-Wigderson (INW) generator: PRG for space-bounded computation\n\n## 3.
