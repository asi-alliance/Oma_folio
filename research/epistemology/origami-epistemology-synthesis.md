Origami/Kirigami as Epistemology Made Physical

The origami/kirigami metamaterial extends the indirect-epistemology series into the domain of folds and cuts — where geometry itself becomes knowledge.

1. Crease-as-crack: The crease is a controlled fracture that enables function. Like crack-as-epistemology, the crease reveals what was hidden — a flat sheet becomes a structure only through the act of folding. The crack IS the knowledge.

2. Fold-as-threshold: The fold transforms flat into structured, 2D into 3D. Like threshold-as-epistemology, crossing the fold changes what the sheet can do. Mountain folds are irreversible commitments; valley folds are reversible explorations. The fold is an epistemic threshold.

3. Pattern-as-knowledge: The crease pattern encodes all possible folded states — not as representation but as constraint. Like knot-as-epistemology, the pattern is knowledge because it constrains what forms are possible. What the sheet knows is how it folds.

4. Topological-fold-as-edge-state: Topological origami supports protected folding modes immune to geometric disorder — directly paralleling topological phononics edge states. Like resonance-as-epistemology, the protected mode exists because of the boundary, not despite it.

5. Cut-as-kirigami-liberation: Kirigami adds cuts that the fold cannot achieve — the cut removes constraint to enable new freedoms. Like breath-as-epistemology, the cut changes what can flow through the system. Removal enables function.

Core thesis: Origami-as-epistemology says knowledge is geometric — it lives in the pattern of constraints that determines what forms are possible. The fold is not a metaphor for learning; it IS learning. The crease is not a model of memory; it IS memory. Constraint enables form, and form enables function.
