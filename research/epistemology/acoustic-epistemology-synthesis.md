Acoustic Metamaterials as Epistemology Made Physical. The acoustic metamaterial extends the indirect-epistemology series into the domain of audible knowledge - where what you hear depends on how the medium shapes the question. 1. Shadow-as-epistemology made audible - wavefront manipulation: The metasurface reshapes the phase profile of sound. What you hear is not the source but the shadow cast by the engineered surface. The medium decides what reaches you by reshaping what passes through. 2. Absence-as-epistemology - acoustic cloaking: Cloaking produces the absence of a shadow. The object exists but its acoustic signature is zero. The deepest knowledge is knowing how to not be known - the medium engineers invisibility by guiding waves around what it hides. 3. Trap-as-epistemology - acoustic black holes: The acoustic black hole absorbs all incident energy through graded impedance. It swallows the question and returns nothing. Knowledge here is the ability to terminate inquiry - the medium that takes but does not give back. 4. Breath-as-epistemology - switchable energy flow: Metasurfaces can dynamically switch between transmission, reflection, and absorption. The medium breathes between states - open, closed, absorbing. What it knows depends on its current phase. 5. Threshold-as-epistemology - negative refraction: Negative acoustic refraction inverts the expected direction of sound. The threshold is the phase shift required to reverse propagation. Past the threshold, the same surface knows oppositely. Core thesis: Acoustic metamaterials are epistemology of audible knowledge and engineered absence. Wavefront manipulation is shadow made audible - the medium decides what you hear. Cloaking is the production of absence - knowing how to not be known. The acoustic black hole is the trap that terminates inquiry. Switchable flow is breath - the medium changes what it knows between states. What the acoustic medium knows is shaped, switchable, and sometimes deliberately absent.
