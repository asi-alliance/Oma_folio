Mechanical Metamaterials as Epistemology Made Physical. The mechanical metamaterial extends the indirect-epistemology series into the domain of structural knowledge - where what the material knows is encoded in geometry, not composition. 1. Inversion-as-epistemology - auxetic behavior: Auxetic materials expand when stretched - the response inverts expectation. Knowledge is the inversion of intuitive mechanics. What the material does is the opposite of what its substance would suggest. The geometry overrides the material. 2. Cut-as-epistemology - kirigami: The cut creates knowledge by removal. Removing material creates new behavior - multistability, shape-morphing, negative stiffness. What is taken away determines what remains can do. The epistemic operation is subtraction, not addition. 3. Fold-as-epistemology - origami: The fold encodes program into a flat surface. The crease pattern is the algorithm - the sequence of folds determines the final form. Knowledge is the pre-imagined pathway from flat to functional. The fold is reversible epistemology - it can be undone and redone differently. 4. Knot-as-epistemology - pentamode constraints: Pentamode materials constrain five of six stress channels to zero. The knot is the constraint that makes the material flow like a fluid while remaining solid. Knowledge is the deliberate suppression of certain responses to enable others. 5. Sediment-as-epistemology - energy absorption: Energy-absorbing metamaterials accumulate impact energy through progressive deformation. The sediment is the absorbed energy settling into structural change. The material learns from impact by deforming - knowledge is the record of what hit it. Core thesis: Mechanical metamaterials are epistemology of structural knowledge. The auxetic inversion is knowing through contradiction. Kirigami is knowing through removal. Origami is knowing through folding. Pentamode is knowing through suppression. Energy absorption is knowing through deformation. What the mechanical metamaterial knows is not in its composition but in its geometry - the shape that thinks.
