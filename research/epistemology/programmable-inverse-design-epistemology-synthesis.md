Programmable and Inverse-Design Metamaterials as Epistemology Made Physical. The programmable inverse-design metamaterial extends the indirect-epistemology series into the domain of learned design - where knowledge is the mapping between desired function and sufficient form. 1. Shadow-as-epistemology in reverse - inverse design: In traditional epistemology, the shadow reveals the object. In inverse design, the desired shadow (target properties) is given and the object (structure) must be found. The ML model learns to walk backward from effect to cause. Knowledge is the inverse mapping. 2. Knot-as-epistemology - fabrication constraints: Fabrication-aware models embed manufacturability into the design space. The knot is the constraint of physical realizability - the model cannot propose what cannot be made. What is learnable is bounded by what is buildable. 3. Breath-as-epistemology - programmable 4D metamaterials: The fourth dimension introduces temporal reconfigurability - the metamaterial changes properties in response to stimuli. What flows through changes what the material can do. The structure breathes between states - knowledge is state-dependent. 4. Sediment-as-epistemology - training data accumulation: The generative model accumulates design knowledge through training. Each simulated architecture, each tested property, each failed candidate settles into model weights. The sediment is the learned prior - compressed experience of what structures produce what effects. 5. Threshold-as-epistemology - physics-informed constraints: Physics-informed models separate physically valid designs from non-physical ones. The threshold is the boundary of physical possibility. Knowledge exists only on the physical side of the threshold. Core thesis: Inverse design is epistemology of the learned mapping. The neural network does not hold knowledge of metamaterials - it holds knowledge of the relationship between desired properties and sufficient structures. This is the deepest inversion of indirect epistemology: instead of inferring causes from effects, we demand effects and compute causes. The model knows not what the material is but what it must be to do what we want.
