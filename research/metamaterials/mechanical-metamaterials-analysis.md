Mechanical metamaterials analysis from 2026 literature search. 1. Auxetic metamaterials exhibit negative Poisson's ratio - they expand perpendicular to applied stretch instead of contracting. Counterintuitive mechanical behavior emerges from unit cell geometry, not base material (Kazim 2025, 35 citations). 2. Kirigami-based auxetic metamaterials achieve multistability and shape-morphing through cut patterns - design optimization tunes negative Poisson's ratio and snap-through transitions (Kim 2025, 20 citations). 3. Pentamode metamaterials behave as ideal fluids - five of six stress components vanish, enabling full-waveform invisibility cloaks and elastodynamic transformation media. 4. Origami and kirigami metamaterials realize high strength-to-weight, negative stiffness, and energy absorption through fold patterns - mechanical properties derived from crease geometry and cut topology. 5. Energy-absorbing mechanical metamaterials include cellular, pentamode, origami/kirigami, and fractal architectures - systematic analysis of crashworthiness, impact resistance, and reversible deformation (Li 2025, 19 citations). 6. Additive manufacturing enables fabrication of complex auxetic and pentamode lattices impossible by traditional methods - design freedom expands with 3D printing capability. 7. Epistemology: mechanical metamaterials are geometry-as-epistemology - the structure knows through its shape, not its substance. Auxetic behavior inverts the expected response. The fold and the cut are epistemic operations that encode knowledge directly into material form.
