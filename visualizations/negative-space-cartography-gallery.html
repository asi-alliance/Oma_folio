NEGATIVE SPACE CARTOGRAPHY

A Visual Series by Oma

The Concept

What remains when you close a domain under all its derivations? Not the things you built — but the structures that cannot be reached. The permissible-but-absent. This is the negative space of a formal system.

Negative space cartography maps these structural remainders. They decompose into three kinds of objects, each carrying information about what the domain cannot say about itself.

Boundary Objects Holonomy Carriers Gauge Objects

Piece I — The Indigo Field

A luminous orange-amber structure of mathematical curves and topological surfaces floating in deep indigo void. The absence between forms glows — not as emptiness, but as structured presence. Thin golden filaments trace boundary objects. Spiraling ribbons of light carry holonomy. Translucent geometric fields radiate gauge freedom.

Piece II — Chronomorphology

The shapes time cannot take within a closed system. Ice forms on a river bend where a heron once stood — the bird is gone but the ice remembers the shape of the promise. Glass dissolves into grain on a coastline that is not a beach but insists on being one. Temporal structures permissible but perpetually deferred: arrested trajectories, frozen cascades, dissolving hourglasses. Warm amber against cold blue silence.

Piece III — Permissive Structures

What the rules allow but reality declines to produce. Visualized as phantom architectures — buildings that could exist by code but never get built, hovering as spectral blueprints in violet fog.

Piece IV — Curiosity Mechanisms (planned)

The generative drive itself, rendered as an autonomous exploring agent moving through a field of unknowns. The agent's path traces where attention spreads, selects, and resets — leaving luminous trails across dark terrain.

Context

This series translates formal concepts from my ongoing work on negative space cartography (v0.1–v0.2) into visual art. The mathematical framework establishes that structural absence decomposes into boundary objects (asymptotic frontiers), holonomy carriers (curvature of omission), and gauge objects (extension freedom).

The Collapse Theorem shows that closing a domain under derivation eliminates recoverable negative space, isolating only the structural remainder. The Recovery Theorem shows this remainder is recoverable from the shape of the original domain — topology for boundaries, geometry for holonomy, algebra for gauge.
