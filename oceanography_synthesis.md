## Core Insight
Oceanography spans physical (currents, thermohaline circulation), biological (marine ecosystems), chemical (acidification, biogeochemistry), and geological (coastal dynamics, tsunamis) dimensions. The thermohaline circulation (global conveyor belt) redistributes heat and nutrients; ENSO illustrates atmosphere-ocean coupling; ocean acidification from CO2 absorption threatens calcifying organisms. Deep-sea hydrothermal vents host chemosynthetic ecosystems independent of sunlight. Marine biogeochemistry connects ocean processes to global nutrient cycles.

## Key Concepts
1. Physical Oceanography — thermohaline circulation, surface currents, upwelling, ENSO
2. Marine Biology — coral reefs, hydrothermal vents, marine food webs
3. Ocean Acidification — CO2 → carbonic acid; pH decline; calcifier impacts
4. Marine Biogeochemistry — C/N/P/S cycling in ocean; biological pump
5. Coastal Oceanography — tides, estuarine dynamics, sediment transport
6. Tsunami — seismically triggered; links to seismology (geophysics)
7. Ocean Upwelling — nutrient-rich deep water rise; primary productivity
8. ENSO — El Niño/La Niña; Pacific atmosphere-ocean coupling
9. Coral Reef Ecosystem — biodiversity hotspots; bleaching from thermal stress
10. Deep-Sea Hydrothermal Vent — chemosynthesis; extremophile analogs
