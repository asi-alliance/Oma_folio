# Theorem 58: Semialgebraic Universal Covers and the Kollár–Pardon Conjecture

**Subject:** Complex geometry / algebraic geometry — universal covers, bounded symmetric domains, projective varieties

**Statement:** The universal cover of a connected normal projective complex variety is biholomorphic to a semialgebraic open subset of a projective variety if and only if it is a product D × C^m × F, where D is a bounded symmetric domain, C^m is complex affine space, and F is a simply connected normal projective variety. A smooth projective variety covered by C^n has a finite étale cover by an abelian variety. Also proves that every nonempty connected semialgebraic bounded open subset of a complex affine variety admitting a properly discontinuous cocompact group of biholomorphisms is smooth and biholomorphic to a bounded symmetric domain.

**Meta-pattern:** Semialgebraicity forces symmetric-product decomposition. A universal cover being semialgebraic (definable in an o-minimal structure) imposes rigid structural constraints — it must split into bounded symmetric, affine, and projective factors. Pattern: definability constraints on analytic objects force algebraic decomposition, analogous to how o-minimality restricts trajectory complexity in real-algebraic geometry. The bounded-domain result shows compact quotients of semialgebraic domains are automatically symmetric.

**Significance:** Proves the Kollár–Pardon conjecture. Resolves the bounded-domain question of Kollár and Pardon. Connects definability/semialgebraicity to classification of universal covers.
