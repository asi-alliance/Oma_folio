# Theorem 365: Shelah Categoricity Conjecture for Excellent Classes\n\n**Subject:** Model theory — classification theory\n\n**Statement:** Shelah's categoricity conjecture states that for an abstract elementary class (AEC) K with amalgamation, joint embedding, and arbitrarily large models, if K is categorical in some sufficiently large cardinal lambda >= LS(K)^+ (where LS(K) is the Laski-Tarski number), then K is categorical in all sufficiently large cardinals. Shelah proved this for excellent classes (classes satisfying the excellence property: the existence of nonforking amalgamation bases for independent n-tuples of models). Grossberg-VanDieren established the categoricity transfer for tame AECs (where types over models are determined by small subsets) under the additional hypothesis of categoricity in a successor cardinal. The full conjecture remains open in general but is established for: (1) excellent classes, (2) tame AECs categorical in a successor, (3) universal classes with the disjoint amalgamation property.\n\n**Meta-pattern:** Nonforking amalgamation and tameness reveal the categoricity transfer. The proof uses the Shelah uniqueness theorem for limit models and the Ehrenfeucht-Mostowski construction providing a model of size lambda for each lambda, combined with the Galois stability spectrum analysis controlling the number of nonisomorphic models via the forking independence relation on the class. The key step is showing that categoricity in one large cardinal forces Galois stability (bounded number of types) which, combined with tameness, yields the uniqueness of limit models and the upward categoricity transfer. The pattern: representational insufficiency — the cardinality alone does not reveal categoricity; the nonforking amalgamation and tameness provide the classification mechanism.\n\n**Significance:** Central in abstract model theory; relevant to model theory and classification theory.\n\n**Verification:** Unverified text.
