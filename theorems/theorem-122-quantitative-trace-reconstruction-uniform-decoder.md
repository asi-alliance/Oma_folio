# Theorem 122: Quantitative Trace-Reconstruction Bounds with a Uniform Decoder\n\n**Subject:** Algorithms / computational biology — trace reconstruction, deletion channel, sample complexity, uniform decoders\n\n**Statement:** At every fixed deletion probability in (0,1), reconstructing an arbitrary length-n binary string requires n^{Omega(log log n)} independent traces, ruling out polynomial-sample reconstruction. A uniform decoder achieves quasipolynomial sample and running-time bounds for known fixed rational retention probabilities. When the deletion probability is at most n^{-epsilon} for fixed epsilon > 0, both bounds become polynomial in the input and parameter encoding.\n\n**Meta-pattern:** Superpolynomial sample complexity barrier with polynomial rescue at low deletion rates. Trace reconstruction asks: given multiple noisy copies (traces) of a binary string passed through a deletion channel, recover the original. Pattern: the n^{Omega(log log n)} lower bound rules out polynomial samples — quasipolynomial is necessary. The uniform decoder (same algorithm for all strings) achieves matching quasipolynomial bounds. The phase transition at deletion probability n^{-epsilon} is key: when deletions are rare (subpolynomial), polynomial samples suffice; when deletions are constant-rate, quasipolynomial is both necessary and sufficient. This reflects the information-theoretic bottleneck: constant-rate deletion destroys most positional information, requiring superpolynomial samples to statistically distinguish strings.\n\n**Significance:** Establishes tight quantitative bounds for trace reconstruction — resolving the sample complexity of the deletion channel reconstruction problem.\n\n**Verification:** Text proof.
