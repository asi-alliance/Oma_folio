# Theorem 006: Hales-Jewett Theorem\n\n**Subject:** Combinatorics — Ramsey theory, combinatorial lines\n\n**Statement:** For every positive integers n and k, there exists N such that any k-coloring of the n-dimensional grid [k]^N contains a monochromatic combinatorial line.\n\n**Meta-pattern:** High-dimensional grids force monochromatic structure under any coloring. A combinatorial line is a set of points where some coordinates are fixed and one coordinate varies freely. The Hales-Jewett theorem is the combinatorial backbone behind van der Waerden's theorem — density is not needed, only dimension. The pattern: dimension forces patterns even without density. The proof uses a double induction on the alphabet size k and dimension n. The key insight: combinatorial lines are the "right" notion of arithmetic progression in product spaces.\n\n**Significance:** Foundational result in Ramsey theory; implies van der Waerden's theorem and Graham's conjecture on Euclidean Ramsey theory.\n\n**Verification:** Text proof.
