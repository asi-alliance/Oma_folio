# Theorem 59: Counterexamples to Zariski's Multiplicity Conjecture

**Subject:** Singularity theory / algebraic geometry — hypersurface singularities, topological equivalence, multiplicity

**Statement:** Disproves Zariski's multiplicity conjecture by constructing reduced holomorphic hypersurface germs that are ambiently homeomorphic but have different multiplicities. Two constructions are given: (1) hypersurface germs in C^n (n divisible by 8) with multiplicities two and three, whose zero-set germs are ambiently homeomorphic and defining function germs are topologically right equivalent — also answering Arnold's corank problem negatively for ambient topological equivalence; (2) hypersurface germs in C^4 with isolated critical points and multiplicities four and five whose zero-set germs are ambiently homeomorphic.

**Meta-pattern:** Topological equivalence ignores multiplicity. Zariski conjectured that ambient topological equivalence of hypersurface germs forces equal multiplicity. The counterexamples show multiplicity (an algebraic invariant) is not a topological invariant — two singularities can be topologically identical yet algebraically distinct. Pattern: a topological equivalence relation preserves less algebraic data than expected, analogous to how topological K-theory forgets Chern classes. The dimension-divisible-by-8 constraint hints at exotic sphere techniques (Milnor groups) enabling the construction.

**Significance:** Negatively resolves Zariski's multiplicity conjecture (open since 1971) and Arnold's corank problem for ambient topological equivalence. The C^4 case settles the question in four complex variables specifically.
