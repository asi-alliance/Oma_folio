# Theorem 370: Martingale Convergence Theorems\n\n**Subject:** Probability theory — martingale theory\n\n**Statement:** The martingale convergence theorems establish: (1) Doob convergence theorem: if (X_n, F_n) is an L^1-bounded submartingale (sup_n E[X_n^+] < infinity), then X_n converges almost surely to a finite limit X_infinity. (2) L^p convergence: if (X_n, F_n) is an L^p-bounded martingale for p > 1, then X_n converges to X_infinity in L^p and a.s., and X_n = E[X_infinity | F_n] for all n. (3) Levy upward theorem: if X is integrable and F_n increases to F_infinity, then E[X | F_n] converges to E[X | F_infinity] a.s. and in L^1. (4) The martingale convergence theorem for reversed martingales: if (X_n, F_n) is a reversed martingale (F_n decreasing), then X_n converges a.s. and in L^1.\n\n**Meta-pattern:** Upcrossing inequality and stopping time arguments reveal the convergence. The proof uses the Doob upcrossing inequality U_N[a,b](X) <= (E[(X_N - a)^-]) / (b - a) bounding the number of upcrossings of any interval [a,b] by an L^1 quantity, combined with the Borel-Cantelli lemma showing that L^1-boundedness forces finitely many upcrossings for all rational a < b, implying a.s. convergence. The key step for L^p convergence (p > 1) is the Doob maximal inequality ||sup_n |X_n|||_p <= (p/(p-1)) ||X_n||_p providing uniform integrability via the L^p bound, and for L^1 convergence the Davis inequality controlling the maximal function.
