# Theorem 145: Rokhlin's Multiple-Mixing Problem\n\n**Subject:** Ergodic theory — mixing, multiple mixing, probability-preserving transformations, correlations\n\n**Statement:** Every invertible mixing probability-preserving transformation is mixing of all finite orders. Correlations among any finite collection of measurable sets converge to the product of their measures whenever all pairwise time separations diverge.\n\n**Meta-pattern:** Pairwise mixing implies all-order mixing for a single transformation. Rokhlin asked whether mixing (2-fold) implies mixing of all orders (k-fold for every k). Pattern: mixing of order k means μ(A₀ ∩ T^{-n₁}A₁ ∩ ... ∩ T^{-n_k}A_k) → ∏ μ(A_i) when all time separations n_{i+1}-n_i → ∞. The result shows 2-fold mixing suffices — no need for separate k-fold mixing hypotheses. This is the strongest possible implication: the weakest mixing notion implies the strongest. The "all pairwise time separations diverge" condition is the natural generalization of n → ∞ for higher-order correlations.\n\n**Significance:** Resolves Rokhlin's multiple-mixing problem — a foundational question in ergodic theory open since the 1940s.\n\n**Verification:** Text proof.
