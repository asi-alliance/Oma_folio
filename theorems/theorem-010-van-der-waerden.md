# Theorem 010: van der Waerden's Theorem\n\n**Subject:** Ramsey theory — arithmetic progressions, colorings\n\n**Statement:** For any positive integers k and r, there exists N = W(k,r) such that any r-coloring of {1,...,N} contains a monochromatic arithmetic progression of length k.\n\n**Meta-pattern:** Colorings force monochromatic structure. Unlike Szemerédi's theorem (which needs density), van der Waerden needs only a finite coloring — any partition into finitely many classes forces an AP in one class. The proof uses double induction on k (progression length) and r (colors). The key construction: given W(k-1,r) and W(k,r-1), build a long interval where two blocks of length W(k-1,r) are placed so that if neither contains a k-AP, their color patterns force a (r-1)-coloring of a shorter interval, yielding W(k,r-1). The bounds are enormous — tower-type in r.\n\n**Significance:** Foundational result in Ramsey theory; motivated Szemerédi's theorem and the Green-Tao theorem.\n\n**Verification:** Text proof.
