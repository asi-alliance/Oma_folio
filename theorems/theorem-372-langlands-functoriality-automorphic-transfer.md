# Theorem 372: Langlands Functoriality and Automorphic Transfer\n\n**Subject:** Number theory / representation theory — Langlands program\n\n**Statement:** The Langlands functoriality principle states that for any L-homomorphism phi: G^L -> H^L between L-groups of connected reductive groups G and H, there exists a transfer of automorphic representations: every automorphic representation pi of G(A) should transfer to an automorphic representation Pi of H(A) such that the L-functions match: L(s, pi, r) = L(s, Pi) for the representation r of H^L determined by phi. Key established cases include: (1) cyclic base change for GL(2) (Langlands, Saito-Shintani): quadratic and cyclic extensions E/F, pi on GL(2,F) transfers to pi_E on GL(2,E). (2) symmetric power lifts: symmetric square Sym^2 for GL(2) (Gelbart-Jacquet), Sym^3 (Kim-Shahidi), Sym^4 (Kim). (3) automorphic induction for GL(n) (Arthur-Clozel): cyclic extensions, transfer from GL(n,E) to GL(n,F) via the norm map. (4) endoscopic transfer for classical groups (Arthur): transfer from quasi-split classical groups to GL(N) via the map on dual groups. (5) the full GL(n) case via converse theorem methods (Cogdell-Piatetski-Shapiro).\n\n**Meta-pattern:** L-group homomorphism and trace formula comparison reveal the automorphic transfer. The proof uses the Arthur-Selberg trace formula on both G and H and the stabilization of the trace formula via endoscopic groups, combined with the fundamental lemma (proved by Ngo Bao Chau) providing the orbital integral matching between related groups. The key step is showing that the trace formula identity, after stabilization, forces the existence of packets of automorphic representations on H corresponding to representations on G, with matching L-functions. The pattern: representational insufficiency — the automorphic representation alone does not reveal its transfer; the L-group structure and trace formula comparison provide the functorial bridge.\n\n**Significance:** The central organizing principle of the Langlands program; relevant to number theory, representation theory, and arithmetic geometry.\n\n**Verification:** Unverified text.
