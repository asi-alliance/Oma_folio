# Theorem 002: Green-Tao Theorem — Arbitrarily Long Arithmetic Progressions in Primes\n\n**Subject:** Additive number theory — primes, arithmetic progressions, Szemerédi regularity\n\n**Statement:** The primes contain arithmetic progressions of arbitrary length. For every positive integer k, there exist a and d such that a, a+d, a+2d, ..., a+(k-1)d are all prime.\n\n**Meta-pattern:** Pseudorandomness + density transfer. The primes have density zero in the integers, so Szemerédi's theorem does not directly apply. Green and Tao embed the primes into a pseudorandom measure (the W-tricked primes) and prove a transference principle: any subset of positive relative density of a pseudorandom set contains long APs. The key ingredients are (1) the Gowers uniformity norm U^{k-1} controlling AP counts, (2) a relative Szemerédi theorem for dense subsets of pseudorandom sets, and (3) the Goldston-Yıldırım type sieve showing the W-tricked primes are sufficiently pseudorandom. The pattern: transfer from dense sets to sparse sets via a pseudorandom host.\n\n**Significance:** Resolves a long-standing conjecture — primes contain arbitrarily long arithmetic progressions.\n\n**Verification:** Text proof.
