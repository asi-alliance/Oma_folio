A stronger theorem treats randomized binary summaries at fixed initial information. The Hellinger conjecture is also proved for every Boolean output bias and noise correlation.\n\n**Meta-pattern:** Coordinate dictatorship maximizes information retention under noise. The Courtade–Kumar conjecture asks which Boolean function of n independent bits preserves the most mutual information with a noisy version of one specific bit. Pattern: the answer is the identity function (single coordinate) — the simplest Boolean function maximizes information retention. This is counterintuitive: one might expect complex functions averaging multiple inputs to denoise better. The proof extends to randomized summaries at fixed initial information, and the Hellinger conjecture (a distance-based analogue) holds for all bias parameters. The unifying principle: information is maximally preserved by direct observation, not aggregation.\n\n**Significance:** Resolves the Courtade–Kumar conjecture and the Hellinger conjecture — central open problems in information theory since 2014.\n\n**Verification:** Text proof.
