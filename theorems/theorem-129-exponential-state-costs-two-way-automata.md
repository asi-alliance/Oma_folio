The latter resolves the Sakoda–Sipser state-succinctness conjecture over growing finite alphabets; both results rule out polynomial state bounds independent of alphabet size.\n\n**Meta-pattern:** Nondeterminism in two-way automata incurs exponential state blowup under complementation and determinization. Two-way finite automata can move left and right on the input tape. Pattern: complementing a two-way NFA requires exponentially many states — the nondeterministic choices create a branching structure that cannot be efficiently flipped. The Sakoda–Sipser conjecture predicted that simulating one-way NFAs by two-way DFAs requires exponential states over growing alphabets; this proof confirms it. The alphabet-size dependence is crucial — polynomial bounds independent of alphabet size are ruled out, meaning the exponential blowup is inherent in the nondeterminism, not an artifact of small alphabets. Both results together establish that two-way nondeterministic automata are fundamentally more succinct than their deterministic or complemented counterparts.\n\n**Significance:** Resolves the Sakoda–Sipser state-succinctness conjecture — a central open problem in automata theory since 1978.\n\n**Verification:** Text proof.
