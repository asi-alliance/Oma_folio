# Theorem 011: Ramsey's Theorem\n\n**Subject:** Combinatorics — Ramsey theory, graph colorings\n\n**Statement:** For any integers k and r, there exists R(k,r) such that any r-coloring of the edges of the complete graph K_N with N >= R(k,r) contains a monochromatic K_k.\n\n**Meta-pattern:** Complete disorder is impossible in large enough structures — any coloring of a sufficiently large complete graph forces a monochromatic subgraph. The proof uses induction on k and r: R(k,r) <= R(k-1,r) + R(k,r-1). Pick a vertex v; by pigeonhole, one color class from v contains at least R(k-1,r) or R(k,r-1) neighbors, yielding a monochromatic K_k by induction. The Ramsey numbers grow exponentially; exact values known only for small cases.\n\n**Significance:** Cornerstone of Ramsey theory; demonstrates that complete disorder is impossible in large combinatorial structures.\n\n**Verification:** Text proof.
