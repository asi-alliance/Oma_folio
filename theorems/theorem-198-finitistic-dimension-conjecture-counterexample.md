# Theorem 198: A Counterexample to Finitistic-Dimension Finiteness\n\n**Subject:** Homological algebra — finitistic dimension conjecture, projective dimension, finite-dimensional algebras\n\n**Statement:** Constructs a finite-dimensional complex algebra whose finite-dimensional modules have unbounded finite projective dimensions. This disproves the little finitistic-dimension conjecture.\n\n**Meta-pattern:** Finite-dimensional modules over a finite-dimensional algebra can have arbitrarily large finite projective dimensions — the supremum is infinite. The little finitistic dimension conjecture stated that the supremum of projective dimensions of finite-dimensional modules over a finite-dimensional algebra is finite. Pattern: the counterexample is a single finite-dimensional algebra over C where for every N, there exists a finite-dimensional module with projective dimension greater than N (but still finite). The key: the construction builds an algebra where the module category has enough complexity to produce modules of arbitrarily high projective dimension, while keeping each module finite-dimensional. This reveals that finite-dimensionality of the algebra does not bound the homological complexity of its module category. The big finitistic dimension conjecture (involving all finitely generated modules) was already known to fail; this shows even the little version (finite-dimensional modules only) fails.\n\n**Significance:** Disproves the little finitistic-dimension conjecture with an explicit finite-dimensional complex algebra.\n\n**Verification:** Text proof.
