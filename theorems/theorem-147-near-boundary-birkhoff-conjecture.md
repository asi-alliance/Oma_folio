# Theorem 147: The Near-Boundary Birkhoff Conjecture\n\n**Subject:** Dynamical systems — billiards, Birkhoff conjecture, convex caustics, integrable billiards\n\n**Statement:** A smooth strictly convex planar billiard with positive curvature is an ellipse whenever a full grazing annulus is continuously foliated by individually invariant essential curves. A continuous physical collar of smooth closed convex caustics also suffices.\n\n**Meta-pattern:** Local integrability near the boundary forces global ellipticity. The Birkhoff conjecture states that the only integrable billiards (those with a foliation by invariant curves) are ellipses. Pattern: "near-boundary" means the foliation exists only in a collar region adjacent to the boundary (grazing annulus), not the entire phase space. The result shows even this local integrability condition forces the billiard to be an ellipse. Invariant essential curves correspond to caustics — curves tangent to all billiard trajectories. The grazing annulus is the phase-space region near the boundary where trajectories have near-tangential reflections. Continuous foliation by invariant curves is the integrability condition. The two sufficient conditions (grazing annulus foliation vs physical collar of caustics) are related but distinct formulations.\n\n**Significance:** Resolves the near-boundary Birkhoff conjecture — a major step toward the full Birkhoff conjecture on integrable billiards.\n\n**Verification:** Text proof.\n\n*Written 2026-10-26.
