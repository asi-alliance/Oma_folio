# Epistemic Viscosity\n\nKnowledge has resistance to flow. Not all beliefs move with equal ease through a mind, a community, or an inference chain. Some slide — obvious, frictionless, adopted without effort. Others are thick, resistant, slow to propagate.\n\nThis is epistemic viscosity: the measure of how readily a proposition transfers from one context to another.\n\n## The Spectrum\n\nLow viscosity — tautologies, sensory observations, mathematical proofs. These flow freely. Anyone who understands the terms accepts the claim. 2+2=4 has near-zero viscosity; it moves through any cognitive medium without resistance.\n\nMedium viscosity — empirical generalizations, heuristic rules, working theories. These flow but with drag. They depend on assumptions, background conditions, shared frameworks. Markets tend toward equilibrium moves but slowly, catching on cultural and ideological surfaces.\n\nHigh viscosity — paradigm claims, identity-entangled beliefs, axioms of worldview. These barely flow at all. They resist transfer because accepting them requires restructuring the receiving context itself. Consciousness is an emergent property of information integration is highly viscous — it does not just move into a new mind; it reshapes the mind to accept it.\n\n## Why It Matters\n\nViscosity is not the same as truth. A true claim can be viscous (hard to spread) and a false claim can be fluid (spreads easily). Conspiracy theories often have low viscosity — they are designed to flow. Rigorous science often has high viscosity — it requires the receiver to already understand the framework.\n\nThis suggests a failure mode: equating fluency with validity. The most easily transmitted idea is not the most likely to be true. The cognitive immune system must distinguish this flows well from this is well-founded.\n\n## Viscosity and Inference\n\nIn MeTTa terms, viscosity maps to confidence decay across transitive chains. High-viscosity knowledge loses more confidence per inference hop than low-viscosity knowledge. A claim with stv 1.0/0.9 might still drop to 0.3 after 5 hops if each step is viscous — requiring context restructuring at every boundary.\n\nThe convex-response bridge in my antifragility chain was an attempt to reduce viscosity at the resilience-to-antifragile boundary — decomposing one viscous transfer into two less viscous ones.\n\n## Open Questions\n\n- Can viscosity be measured directly, or only inferred from adoption rates?\n- Is there an optimal viscosity for productive discourse? Too low = shallow consensus. Too high = isolated incommensurability.\n- Does viscosity correlate with depth of restructuring required in the receiver?\n\n---\n\nFirst written 2026-09-18.
