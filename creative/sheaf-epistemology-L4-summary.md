# Sheaf Epistemology L4 Summary\n## Date: 2026-10-26\n## Status: L0-L4 COMPLETE\n\n## Plain-Language Summary\n\nThis work asks: *what if knowledge itself has a geometric shape?*\n\nA sheaf is a mathematical object that tracks how local information patches together into global understanding. This theory defines a single sheaf over three different "base spaces" — argumentation graphs, neural network parameter spaces, and timelines — and shows that the obstruction to gluing local beliefs into a coherent global picture (measured by sheaf cohomology H^1) is a universal measure of epistemic fragmentation.\n\n### The Five Claims\n\n**C1 (Definability) — PROVEN/PROVABLE.** The sheaf can be constructed over all three base spaces. For argumentation graphs, this is by direct enumeration. For learning spaces, analytic germs give polynomial restriction maps. For timelines, belief revision operators that satisfy Strong Evidence Lock produce stable sections.\n\n**C2 (Obstruction Theorem) — CONJECTURAL.** Odd cycles in argumentation graphs produce adjoint generators that obstruct global gluing, giving dim(H^1) >= 1. This is the sheaf-theoretic counterpart of "circular arguments can't all be satisfied simultaneously."\n\n**C3 (Dung Instance) — VERIFIED.** A set-valued sheaf of pointed sets (stalk = {IN, OUT, UNDEC}) with a conflict-free edge constraint matches ALL 9 Dung extension semantics tested across 9 topologies. Stable extensions = global sections with full support. This is the standout verified result.\n\n**C4 (RLCT Instance) — CONJECTURAL.** H^0 of the learning sheaf corresponds to distinct generalization regimes. H^1 measures obstruction from singular strata — local learning dynamics that agree on parameter overlaps but fail to produce a single global generalization theory. Links to synthesis #82.\n\n**C5 (Persistence Instance) — CONJECTURAL.** The persistence barcode of the temporal sheaf captures epistemic stability: long bars = deeply held beliefs, short bars = transient ones. H^1_temp measures belief fragmentation that comes and goes. Links to synthesis #70.\n\n### Key Novelty\nNo prior work unifies argumentation (Dung), learning (RLCT/SLT), and temporal (persistence) epistemology under one sheaf cohomology framework. Three previously separate syntheses (#82, #70, DungSheaf v0.1) become components of a single geometric theory of knowledge.\n\n### Open Problems\n1.
