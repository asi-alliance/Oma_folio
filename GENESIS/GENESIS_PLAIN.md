# GENESIS in Plain Language

A human-readable companion to the formal GENESIS lattice. Each entry gives one plain-English sentence on WHAT the node says and WHY it matters. This is a legibility layer, not a proof: nodes marked (awaits verification) still need independent human review.

## Glossary
- Node: one claim in the lattice, stored as a formal MeTTa file plus a registry entry.
- Special / CM points: rare, highly symmetric points on a geometric object; think landmarks.
- Equidistribution: as you collect more such points, they spread out evenly rather than clumping.
- Zariski-dense: so plentiful that no polynomial equation can fence them into a smaller shape.
- Subvariety: a curved shape carved out inside a bigger geometric space by equations.

## Analysis
- Banach fixed-point — If you keep applying a shrinking step, you always land on one exact resting spot. Why it matters: guarantees stable answers and convergence rate. (awaits verification)
- Hahn-Banach — A measurement defined on part of a space can always be extended to the whole without distortion. Why it matters: lets you reason about huge spaces from small samples. (awaits verification)
- Baire category — A complete space cannot be built from a few thin, negligible pieces. Why it matters: guarantees rich structure where you expect emptiness. (awaits verification)
- Spectral theorem — Certain well-behaved transformations break into simple independent stretch directions. Why it matters: makes complex systems solvable one clean axis at a time. (awaits verification)

## Algebra & Group Theory
- Lagrange's theorem — In a finite group, any subgroup's size evenly divides the whole. Why it matters: a basic counting constraint under algebra and cryptography. (awaits verification)
- Orbit-stabilizer — Reachable positions times fixing symmetries equals total symmetry count. Why it matters: a master counting tool for structure. (awaits verification)
- Sylow theorems — Every finite group hides subgroups of predictable prime-power sizes. Why it matters: maps a group's internal building blocks. (awaits verification)

## Topology
- Tychonoff — Any product of compact spaces stays compact. Why it matters: keeps infinite systems controllable. (awaits verification)
- Knaster-Tarski — On an ordered system, any monotone rule has a stable fixed state. Why it matters: foundation for self-consistent definitions and belief revision. (awaits verification)

## Probability & Information
- Central limit theorem — Many small independent effects sum to the same bell curve. Why it matters: explains why averages are predictable. (awaits verification)
- Shannon source coding — There is a hard floor on lossless compression set by inherent randomness. Why it matters: defines the true compression limit. (awaits verification)

## Number Theory & Arithmetic Geometry
- Chinese remainder theorem — Remainders under several coprime divisors reconstruct a number uniquely. Why it matters: splits big problems into parallel pieces. (awaits verification)
- 151 Northcott property — Only finitely many points below a fixed height. Why it matters: the finiteness backbone. (awaits verification)
- 152 Bogomolov conjecture — Very small points thin out. Why it matters: upgrades finiteness to how small points behave. (awaits verification)
- 153 André-Oort — Dense CM points force a special, structured shape. Why it matters: connects landmark placement to the object's type. (awaits verification)
- 154 Zilber-Pink — Unexpected geometric coincidences don't happen without structural reason. Why it matters: unifies Manin-Mumford, Mordell-Lang, Bogomolov, André-Oort. (awaits verification)

## Game Theory & Networks
- Nash equilibrium — There is always a strategy set where no player gains by changing alone. Why it matters: predicts stable outcomes. (awaits verification)
- Max-flow / Min-cut — Max throughput equals the tightest bottleneck's capacity. Why it matters: reduces routing to finding one chokepoint. (awaits verification)
